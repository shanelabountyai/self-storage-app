A 10x10 is the size we rent most, and the size people guess wrong about most
often. Ten feet by ten feet is a hundred square feet of floor — about half a
standard single garage — and the usual ceiling is eight feet, which is the part
that gets forgotten. The floor area is what you pay for; the height is what
decides whether it all goes in.

## What usually fits

A 10x10 takes the contents of a one-bedroom apartment with room to walk in:

- A three-seat sofa and an armchair
- A queen mattress, base and headboard
- A dining table with four chairs
- A washer and a dryer
- A chest of drawers and a wardrobe's worth of boxes
- Twenty to thirty medium boxes, stacked

That list assumes you stack. Furniture on the floor and boxes on top of the
furniture is the difference between a 10x10 that holds a home and one that
holds a sofa and a walkway.

## What does not fit

- **A car.** Almost every car is longer than ten feet. A [10x20 is the smallest
  size that takes one](/storage/size-guide), and it leaves room to open a door.
- **A full two-bedroom home**, if you mean all of it. Two bedrooms plus a living
  room is a 10x15 before you have argued with yourself about what to leave.
- **Anything you need weekly.** A unit you have packed to the walls is a unit you
  have to unpack to reach the back of. If you will be visiting, leave the middle
  empty on purpose.

## How to tell before you rent

Stand in a room you know. A single garage bay is roughly 10x20; half of it,
front to back, is your 10x10. Most people who measure a room this way get the
answer right first time, and the ones who guess without measuring choose too
small.

If you are between two sizes, take the larger one. The rent difference is
usually a few dollars a month, and it is a great deal cheaper than finding out
on moving day. You can move to another size later if you get it wrong.
