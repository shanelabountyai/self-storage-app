### B-271 — a live log that ends in the past (2026-09-08, `864e6f0`)

`reportRange`'s rolling window built its exclusive end in the wrong coordinate system. `businessDateFor` returns **UTC midnight of the facility-LOCAL date**, and `new Date(today.getTime() + 86_400_000)` added a day to that local date and then let the result be read as a UTC instant. For every hour the local date lags the UTC date — 00:00–05:00 UTC in Texas, wider further west — the "exclusive end" was **already in the past**, and the log hid the rows written in the hours the operator opened it to look at. That is precisely the failure the line's own comment says it exists to prevent, so the intent was right and only the arithmetic was wrong.

**What it built.** The two default windows are now reckoned against **opposite ends of the portfolio**, because they ask opposite questions. `last-complete-month` keeps B-223's westernmost rule — "has this period finished everywhere the figures come from?" is answered by the site that has advanced least. `rolling-30-days` takes the **latest** date instead, **with UTC among the candidates**, because "does this window still hold what just happened?" has to hold in the coordinate the rows are actually stored in, and that is UTC rather than any facility's local date. Because `max` includes the UTC date, the end is now **always strictly after `now`** — a property the old arithmetic could not state at all.

**What it decided.**

- **The end moved to a different anchor date; it was not clamped to `now`.** The row warned against clamping, and the warning is right: `toValue` is what the operator sees and resubmits, and an end that is an instant rather than a whole day cannot be typed back into a date input. The window stays exactly 30 whole days, `toValue` stays a `yyyy-mm-dd`, and resubmitting the values the form renders reproduces the identical range — asserted, not assumed.
- **The two-ended reckoning is documented as intentional in `DefaultWindow`.** A future reader finding `min` on one branch and `max` on the other will otherwise "fix" the inconsistency. It is not an inconsistency; it is completeness versus coverage.
- **`last-complete-month` was audited rather than assumed safe, and is NOT the same defect.** It bounds a local month with UTC midnights, so for a US portfolio the last few local hours of a month (31 August 19:00–23:59 in Chicago) report in the **next** month, and the same hours of the previous month are pulled into this one. That is a boundary **misclassification**, not a blind spot: every row still falls in exactly one window, and consecutive ranges still tile with nothing counted twice or skipped, so a year still sums. The rolling defect was categorically worse — those rows appeared in **no** window at all. Correcting the month edge means bounding a month in ONE zone, which is the choice B-223 rejected on purpose, so it is left alone and named below.
- **The regression test carries a facility zone AND a `now` inside 00:00–05:00 UTC**, because either one alone goes green against the unfixed code. That is why the existing suite could not see the bug: `tests/report-range.test.ts` exercised the rolling window with **no `timeZones`**, and with none the list falls back to `UTC` — the single configuration in which adding a day to the local date happens to be right — at a `now` of 18:30 UTC, which would have missed the band anyway.
- **The property is asserted as a property.** Beyond the one reproducing instant, a loop over 24 UTC hours × 8 zones from Honolulu to Kiritimati asserts that the live log never ends in the past and stays exactly 30 days wide. The bug was invisible 19 hours out of 24, which is exactly why one red CI run looked like a flake; a test pinned to a single instant would have left the next variant of this equally invisible.

**All three `rolling-30-days` callers were affected, not just the one the spec caught** — `/admin/impersonation`, its `.csv` route, and `/admin/access` ("Gate activity"). All three are fixed by the single change; none needed editing. Impersonation is the one that stings: **D-13a** gives the tenant no notification and no activity view, which makes that record the *only* misuse-detection channel there is, and it was dark for five hours a day across the end of the evening shift.

**Not customer-facing.** Three admin screens, no public surface, so `/accessibility` is untouched and no claim on it changed in either direction. `LAST_REVIEWED` not bumped (D-115).

**What it left behind.**

- **The `last-complete-month` month-edge skew described above.** Real, understood, deliberately not fixed here, and owned by no row — fixing it requires reopening B-223's "which zone bounds a multi-zone month" question, which is a decision and not a build.

Marking the whole `<label>` would reproduce the mirror defect one level further in. **`Field` needed no change** — the row expected to have to extend it, but `lang` already arrives through `React.InputHTMLAttributes` and is spread into `shared`, which lands on the `<input>` alone and not on the `<label>` rendered beside it. That is exactly the desired placement, confirmed by reading the component rather than by the types compiling.
- **The row named three surfaces and there are four.** The locale links, the subject input and the body textarea were named; the **preview panel** renders `preview.subject` and `preview.text` — the rendered template — and is the one place it is read as *prose* rather than as the value of a form control, which makes it the most worth marking of the four. Its `From:`/`Reply-to:` lines are English chrome around an address and stay unmarked. Counting the surfaces rather than trusting the row is B-265's lesson, paid again.
- **The guard was extended, not copied, and it is a different SHAPE on purpose.** The public site has no shell that can speak for all of it — `(public)` wraps translated and untranslated pages alike — so its claim is per page and `ENGLISH_UNDER_A_TRANSLATED_SHELL` is that claim. Admin is the opposite, so the admin half checks two things instead of listing eighty screens: (1) the layout still declares `lang="en"`, and (2) any admin page that renders from the dictionary is listed in the new `TRANSLATED_UNDER_THE_ADMIN_SHELL` and declares the locale back. Eighty rows saying "English" would be eighty chances to forget one, guarding a fact a single attribute already states.
- **A third `why` value, `mixed`, and it carries an obligation the other two do not.** `decision` is settled and `untranslated` is a gap somebody will close; `mixed` means the page renders both on purpose, so a page-level `lang` is a lie about the half it does not describe and the row must declare the *other* language back. The walker checks for a **dynamic** `lang={locale}`/`lang={option}` on a `mixed` admin row, not a literal — a `lang="en"` there would be the defect. `/storage/[state]/[city]` was reclassified from `untranslated` to `mixed` at the same time: it has been the worked example of a mixed page since B-272 and its note already said so, so the value was simply behind the note.
- **The public accessibility statement was not touched, deliberately.** Not customer-facing; the "our staff-facing screens have known problems" bullet already covers it, and a language sentence there would be a **new public claim about screens no customer reaches**. The only edit to that file is a code comment: B-272's block said the mirror was "owned by B-273 rather than fixed here", which would now read as an open gap. `LAST_REVIEWED` not bumped (D-115).

**Every new guard was proven to bite before being called green**, because B-272's own lesson is that a scan pointed at the wrong page is a green test that tests nothing. Reverting just the two source files (`git stash push` on the layout and the editor, keeping the list and the walker) turns the shell test and the declare-it-back test **red**; deleting the editor's row from `TRANSLATED_UNDER_THE_ADMIN_SHELL` turns the third **red**. Axe still cannot see any of this and no scan was added — no rule reads prose and decides what language it is in, and the route loops carry no locale cookie, so every automated run visits as an English visitor where the markup is trivially correct.

**A real limit of the guard, found while building it and written into the code rather than left to be discovered.** The walker reads each `page.tsx` and asks whether **it** imports the dictionary. An admin page rendering a translated **child component** without importing `@/lib/i18n` itself would pass while announcing Spanish under `lang="en"`. `StatementView` is exactly that shape — `/admin/tenants/[tenantId]/ledger/[leaseId]/statements/[period]` renders it and imports no dictionary — and it is safe **only** because B-260 gave its `dict` prop an English default on the argument that the admin surface is English throughout. **That default is now load-bearing for SC 3.1.2, not just for copy**, which matters before somebody makes it required and helpfully passes the tenant's locale in from the admin ledger.

- **The mixed-page marks are asserted structurally, not behaviourally.** The walker proves the attribute is there and is dynamic; nothing proves a screen reader switches voice, which needs the manual pass D-115/B-254 has been waiting on.
- **`/mfa`, `/login` and the other staff-reachable routes outside `app/admin`** are under no shell declaration — they sit in different route groups, are reached by tenants too, and are outside this row's scope.
