### B-275 — a live log that ends in the past (2026-09-08, `864e6f0`)

**Renumbered B-264 → B-266 → B-268 → B-271 → B-275** (merges with `main`; the last on 2026-09-09, when `main`'s own B-271 landed). The commit `864e6f0` says B-271, because history is not rewritten.

`reportRange`'s rolling window built its exclusive end in the wrong coordinate system. `businessDateFor` returns **UTC midnight of the facility-LOCAL date**, and `new Date(today.getTime() + 86_400_000)` added a day to that local date and then let the result be read as a UTC instant. For every hour the local date lags the UTC date — 00:00–05:00 UTC in Texas, wider further west — the "exclusive end" was **already in the past**, and the log hid the rows written in the hours the operator opened it to look at. That is precisely the failure the line's own comment says it exists to prevent, so the intent was right and only the arithmetic was wrong.

**What it built.** The two default windows are now reckoned against **opposite ends of the portfolio**, because they ask opposite questions. `last-complete-month` keeps B-223's westernmost rule — "has this period finished everywhere the figures come from?" is answered by the site that has advanced least. `rolling-30-days` takes the **latest** date instead, **with UTC among the candidates**, because "does this window still hold what just happened?" has to hold in the coordinate the rows are actually stored in, and that is UTC rather than any facility's local date. Because `max` includes the UTC date, the end is now **always strictly after `now`** — a property the old arithmetic could not state at all.

**What it decided.**

- **The end moved to a different anchor date; it was not clamped to `now`.** The row warned against clamping, and the warning is right: `toValue` is what the operator sees and resubmits, and an end that is an instant rather than a whole day cannot be typed back into a date input. The window stays exactly 30 whole days, `toValue` stays a `yyyy-mm-dd`, and resubmitting the values the form renders reproduces the identical range — asserted, not assumed.
- **The two-ended reckoning is documented as intentional in `DefaultWindow`.** A future reader finding `min` on one branch and `max` on the other will otherwise "fix" the inconsistency. It is not an inconsistency; it is completeness versus coverage.
- **`last-complete-month` was audited rather than assumed safe, and is NOT the same defect.** It bounds a local month with UTC midnights, so for a US portfolio the last few local hours of a month (31 August 19:00–23:59 in Chicago) report in the **next** month, and the same hours of the previous month are pulled into this one. That is a boundary **misclassification**, not a blind spot: every row still falls in exactly one window, and consecutive ranges still tile with nothing counted twice or skipped, so a year still sums. The rolling defect was categorically worse — those rows appeared in **no** window at all. Correcting the month edge means bounding a month in ONE zone, which is the choice B-223 rejected on purpose, so it is left alone and named below.
- **The regression test carries a facility zone AND a `now` inside 00:00–05:00 UTC**, because either one alone goes green against the unfixed code. That is why the existing suite could not see the bug: `tests/report-range.test.ts` exercised the rolling window with **no `timeZones`**, and with none the list falls back to `UTC` — the single configuration in which adding a day to the local date happens to be right — at a `now` of 18:30 UTC, which would have missed the band anyway.
- **The property is asserted as a property.** Beyond the one reproducing instant, a loop over 24 UTC hours × 8 zones from Honolulu to Kiritimati asserts that the live log never ends in the past and stays exactly 30 days wide. The bug was invisible 19 hours out of 24, which is exactly why one red CI run looked like a flake; a test pinned to a single instant would have left the next variant of this equally invisible.

**All three `rolling-30-days` callers were affected, not just the one the spec caught** — `/admin/impersonation`, its `.csv` route, and `/admin/access` ("Gate activity"). All three are fixed by the single change; none needed editing. Impersonation is the one that stings: **D-13a** gives the tenant no notification and no activity view, which makes that record the *only* misuse-detection channel there is, and it was dark for five hours a day across the end of the evening shift.

**Not customer-facing.** Three admin screens, no public surface, so `/accessibility` is untouched and no claim on it changed in either direction. `LAST_REVIEWED` not bumped (D-115).

**What it left behind.**

- **The `last-complete-month` month-edge skew described above.** Real, understood, deliberately not fixed here, and owned by no row — fixing it requires reopening B-223's "which zone bounds a multi-zone month" question, which is a decision and not a build.
