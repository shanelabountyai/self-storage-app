### B-258 — Authorized users on a business account: the people allowed to see it (2026-09-04)

**Commit:** `9d572e5`

B-090 part 5's last piece, split out of B-256 rather than deferred silently: it is a table, a migration, a control and an authorization boundary. Until this, an account had exactly one person who could see it, so giving the office manager sight of it meant making them the payer — a money change to solve a visibility problem, which moves who the consolidated Pay button belongs to and who a receipt names.

**What it built.**

1. **`BillingAccountMember`** (`packages/db/prisma/schema.prisma`, migration `20260905025407_billing_account_member`) — an account and a `Tenant` who holds no lease. `Cascade` on the account, `Restrict` on the tenant: a membership without its account is nothing, while a tenant who can see an account is not deleted out from under it. No `facilityId`, and the schema-invariant test's exemption list says why in the same commit.
2. **`addMember` / `removeMember`** (`apps/web/lib/billing/accounts.ts`) — both audited (`billing_account.member_added` / `.member_removed`), both gated on `billing_accounts:manage` at the account's own facility, and both refusing the payer, a duplicate, and anybody who is not already a tenant. `existingTenantByEmail` is that last rule extracted from `createAccount`, so the payer field and the member field cannot drift on what "must already be a tenant" means.
3. **The control, in the same item as the table** — "Who can see this account" on `/admin/billing/accounts/[id]`: the members, a Remove per row, and an add-by-email form. This repo's own rule; a table nothing can reach from a screen is a database client's feature.
4. **`portalAccountsForPayer` → `portalAccountsFor`, with a `payable` flag** — one read model for both the payer and a member, and `payable` is the whole of the difference. Every caller reads it: `payableAccount` (the money path) and the consolidated statement page both test `row.payable` as well as the id, so a member typing either URL gets the null a stranger gets.
5. **The member's card at `/portal`** — the account's units, each balance and the one total, with no Pay button, no "Rented by" column, no autopay paragraph and no statements link. It says *"{payer} is the payer and settles it"* in words, because a screen that silently drops a control reads as a bug to the person who was told they now have access.
6. **The demo member** — Robin Bookkeeper, holding no lease and carrying `DEMO_TENANT_PASSWORD`, so the member's card is a portal state that can be signed into and scanned rather than one described in prose.

**What it decided (D-120).** All three of the questions the row said it must settle rather than assume were settled as **no**.

- **A member may look and may not pay.** The safer default the row itself named. A member who can pay is a second person able to move money on somebody else's account, and B-256's Pay button is the payer's.
- **`payableLeaseWhere` is deliberately UNCHANGED, which reverses this row's own instruction (b).** The row assumed membership would be folded into that fragment so what a person can see and what they can pay cannot drift apart. Look-only makes those two different questions, so the fragment stays the definition of PAYING and the relationship becomes a strict containment rather than an equality: everything payable is visible, and the dangerous direction — paying for a unit you cannot see — stays impossible by construction. Widening it would have granted the payment the decision above refuses.
- **A member does not see the renters' names**, and the whole column is removed rather than its cells emptied, so the table a screen reader announces never has a header with nothing under it. The payer is told whose unit their money settles because they are settling it; a member was added to see what the account owes.
- **The account's statements stay the payer's** — both the consolidated month and the per-unit documents. Not an arbitrary line: the consolidated statement is a row per unit naming its renter, with every row linking into that renter's whole ledger, so giving a member the account month means either handing over another tenant's full financial history or shipping a second, degraded version of a bookkeeping document. That is its own decision and it is named as open below rather than taken here.
- **`resolveAudience` (D-47) needed no change**, which the row had also expected to touch. A member is a `Tenant`; a `Tenant` carries the password and already resolves as the tenant audience. Being a member is a fact about what the portal renders, never about who somebody is.
- **D-111 is inherited, not answered.** A member must already be a tenant, so two people at one household address cannot both be members for exactly the reason they cannot both be tenants. B-238 owns that question and this row invents no second answer to it.

**What it left behind.**

- **A member cannot see the account's statements** — the decision above, stated as a gap because a bookkeeper reconciling last month still has to ask the payer, which is one of the three people the row names. Its own row when somebody asks, and it needs an answer to what a member sees of a unit's own ledger first.
- **A member who may PAY is unbuilt rather than refused.** It needs an owner decision and, plausibly, a per-member flag rather than a second global rule.
- **Nothing tells a member they have been added.** No email, no portal notice: they discover the card next time they sign in. Adding one is a comms template and a send, and this row shipped no message.
- **A member holding no lease and no password cannot sign in until they set one.** The forgot-password flow reaches them (they are a tenant), and nothing in the add form says so.
- **No PO number, no per-account allocation order, no payer autopay, no account close.** Unchanged from B-256; none has a caller.

**A pre-existing check that fired, and was right to.** `tests/schema-invariants.test.ts`'s "scopes every facility-bound model by facilityId" rejected `BillingAccountMember` on the full sweep. The exemption is correct — the account carries the facility — but the reasoning is worth recording: a `facilityId` copy here would be worse than absent, because `BillingAccount` is pinned to one facility by a composite foreign key precisely so a lease and its account cannot disagree about the site, and a second column nothing pins could. It is the same argument that exempts `PaymentPlanInstallment` and `AuctionAdvertisement`.

### B-086 part 2 — Phone unlock, and the answer to OQ-2 (2026-09-04)

**Commit:** `68a1cc8`

The half of US-8 that was still a question. `AccessCredentialType.mobile_key` had sat in the enum since B-002 with no reader, no writer and no UI, and the unlock it implied was blocked on an open question the PRD had carried since it was written: **OQ-2, native app vs. PWA for Bluetooth unlock.** The row asked for a spike; the spike changed the shape of the feature rather than confirming it.

**What it decided — D-121: neither, and no Bluetooth.** Web Bluetooth cannot reach iOS at all. Every browser there is WebKit and WebKit has declined the API, so a BLE unlock written into this web app would work for some Android tenants and silently not exist for roughly half the people holding a lease — the worst possible shape for a control whose failure state is somebody standing outside a gate in the dark. A native app does not rescue it, because the vendors who actually ship BLE (Nokē/Janus) run it inside *their own* app against *their own* locks: the blocker is a partnership, the same one B-085 and B-133 sit behind, and writing our own radio protocol against no vendor is exactly the simulator D-63 refuses. **The unlock is server-side** — the phone asks us over the ordinary portal, we tell the controller — which is what every cloud gate API already exposes, needs no install, works on every phone with a browser, and is decided by the same suspension and schedule the keypad obeys. Web-first stops being provisional.

**What it built.** A `mobile_key` is one credential on the tenant's own grant per facility, issued and revoked from a new section on `/portal/access`, keyed on the FACILITY rather than the unit — a tenant with three units at one site has one gate, one code and one button, which is the mistake D-54 already found in the three PINs a three-unit checkout used to mint. The secret is 256 bits of `randomBytes` rather than a PIN: nobody types it, so the only reason to keep it short would be to make it guessable, and the length also means it can never collide with a PIN in the facility's uniqueness scope nor be presented at a keypad by somebody who read it over a shoulder. The unlock itself runs that secret through **`evaluateKeypadEntry`** — the same function the virtual keypad calls — so the delinquency suspension, the window the controller was last pushed, the fault injection and the signed webhook that writes the `AccessEvent` are the keypad's rather than a second implementation that eventually disagrees. A phone unlock is somebody presenting a credential at a gate; the only difference is that it is not typed.

**`revoke_credential` is the first credential-scoped revocation in this system**, and it had to exist. Every other revocation here is grant-scoped, which was right for exactly as long as a holder had one credential — revoking a person's access and revoking their code were the same act. A mobile key breaks that: it is a second credential on the tenant's own grant, and "I lost my phone" must not send them to the office for a new gate code. Handled in all three adapters (the simulator deactivates, PTI deletes and treats a 404 as success, the manual queue gets an instruction that carries **no** code, because the value is a token nobody can key in and printing it would leak a working credential onto a clipboard).

**The accessibility criteria US-8 AC4 wrote for this control were built rather than retrofitted**, because the default build of an unlock button fails four SCs at once: `aria-busy` on a real submit button (4.1.2, 2.1.1), the label changing in TEXT so no state is carried by colour (1.4.1), the outcome announced from `AdminForm`'s pre-existing `role="status"` and `role="alert"` rather than a toast (4.1.3), and a failed unlock naming the facility's phone number in words. `aria-pressed` was deliberately not used though the AC offers it — this is a momentary action, and a button reported as "pressed" tells a screen-reader user the gate is being held open.

**Four real defects found along the way, none of them reported and none of them mine to introduce.**

1. **A credential added to a grant whose window had already been pushed reached the simulated controller with no window at all.** `set_time_window`'s idempotency key is a digest of the schedule, so re-pushing an unchanged schedule to an existing grant is deduped by the outbox — correctly. Harmless while a grant held one credential and got its window at provisioning; a *second* credential then landed with `windowSchedule` null, which the controller reads as no restriction. That is unrestricted access, which is `extendedHours` — the add-on the facility sells (D-100) — obtained by tapping a portal button. `set_credential` now carries the window the way the PTI driver always has, which meant moving `scheduleForGrant` into `@storage/core/access` so the adapter can reach it without a cycle.
2. **`codeForLease` would have rendered the mobile-key token as the tenant's gate code.** It took the newest active credential on the grant, `orderBy createdAt desc`, and a mobile key is newer than the PIN. Every query that means "the code this tenant types" is now `type: pin` — including `provisionAccessForLease`'s idempotency check, which would otherwise have read a phone-unlock credential as proof of provisioning and minted no PIN for the next move-in.
3. **A gate event that could not be reported home threw, and the throw reached the caller.** `evaluateKeypadEntry` created the vendor-side event row, then `deliver()` raised `Hardware webhook secret unavailable` and everything after it was lost — for a *configuration* gap, not a hardware one: `hardwareWebhookSecret()` deliberately refuses to invent a per-process dev secret under `NODE_ENV=production`, which is exactly what the e2e suite serves and what any deployment without the variable runs. The admin virtual keypad has had this since B-028; the tenant's unlock button is what made it matter, because the 500 lands on the one screen whose subject is somebody standing at a gate. The function's own comment three lines above already drew the right distinction — the gate decides from local memory, only reporting home fails — and the throw was the single path that did not honour it. Undelivered now, replayable from the backlog, same as the injected webhook fault.
4. **React carried one form's action state into another.** The enrol form and the unlock form are conditional siblings in one slot, so React reconciled the first into the other: "Phone unlock is on for this gate" appeared in the UNLOCK form's status region as though the gate had just opened. Found by the e2e, not by review. Keys fixed the identity, and the two forms that remove themselves on success now announce through `AnnounceRegion` (B-170's case) instead of a region the revalidation unmounts in the same commit.

**What it left behind, deliberately.** Nothing repairs a `mobile_key` minted where `ACCESS_CODE_ENCRYPTION_KEY` is unset — enrolment refuses outright instead. A PIN degrades honestly without a key (the controller has the digits and the tenant was told them once); a mobile key is read back on *every* unlock, so an unenrollable environment would hand somebody a working-looking button attached to a credential that can never open anything, discovered in a car park. A lost-key incident is not a state to auto-heal. Related: **the e2e environment had never had that key at all**, so gate codes were `unrevealable:` and the demo portal has been showing "your gate code isn't ready yet" for every tenant — the throwaway key `vitest.config.ts` already injects is now in `.env.test` and in both CI jobs, which is what let this feature be asserted end to end. `HARDWARE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` went in beside it for the same reason: the e2e lane serves a production build, so nothing could sign a simulated gate event and the signed webhook path FR-8 asks to be exercised was not being. Also unbuilt: a mobile key for an authorized person (the query filters are already written for the day that changes), and any per-device labelling — one key per tenant per site, because two live keys on one grant is two things to revoke when one phone is lost.

**One structural change that came with the section rather than from a review.** `/portal/access` had the tenant's units as its only `<h2>`s, so a phone-unlock section above them would have left two unrelated things reading as peers — each unit a sibling of "Open the gate from your phone" rather than a child of the list it belongs to. The authorized-people list is now its own named `<section>` under a "Who else can get in" heading, and the unit headings drop to `<h3>`. axe does not flag `h2` following `h2`, so nothing would have caught it.

**Testing.** `tests/mobile-key-db.test.ts`, ten cases: the unlock writes a real granted `AccessEvent` attributed to a `mobile_key` credential; revoking the phone leaves the PIN readable *and* deactivates the row on the controller rather than merely hiding it from our own reads; the token never surfaces as a gate code and a second move-in still mints a PIN; the enrolled key inherits the window the grant was already on (the guard for defect 1 — it fails without the adapter change); an unreadable signing secret still opens the gate and leaves the event undelivered rather than throwing (the guard for defect 3, forced with a `GateWebhookSecret` row nothing can decrypt — a rotated-then-lost key, which is the failure a real deployment has, and reachable without redefining `NODE_ENV`, which vitest refuses); an offline controller **refuses** a remote unlock, because a real standalone keypad keeps deciding locally and a remote unlock is the network, and hiding that would erase the one honest limitation of choosing a server-side transport; a suspended tenant is refused; a manual-adapter site is not offered it; an unconfigured environment is not offered it and mints nothing; and a double tap returns one key. The suite was run twice back to back against the same database. **One trap cost a debugging pass and is worth naming:** resetting a Json column between tests with `data: { gateHours: undefined }` is a NO-OP in Prisma — `undefined` means "leave this column alone" — so hours set by one test leaked into every later one and denied an unlock for being outside them, but only when the suite ran outside those hours. `Prisma.DbNull` is the reset. Full unit suite green — 4135 passed, 8 skipped, 253 files. `e2e/portal.spec.ts` gained a spec that enrols, presses Open the gate against the past-due demo tenant, and asserts the refusal lands in the alert box with focus, then scans it — registered as `/portal/access | phone unlock refused` in `SCANNED_STATES`, so the branch that matters is audited rather than promised. It is repeatable by construction rather than by a self-skip (B-120): enrolling is idempotent and the assertion is on a refusal, so no run of it opens a gate. **`apps/web/app/(public)/accessibility/page.tsx` was re-read** — no route added, the new state went in as covered rather than excepted, all three "Where we fall short" entries still true and untouched, the "no screen-reader or keyboard pass has been carried out" sentence still true (none was done here), and `LAST_REVIEWED` did not move (D-115 / B-254 owns the date).

### B-090f — Spanish on the move-in path (B-090 part 6) (2026-09-05)

**Commit:** `aba7a48`

The last unbuilt part of B-090, and the only backlog row left that is not blocked on a partner agreement, an owner decision or a person running a screen reader. PRD 01 §9 Phase 2 has said "Spanish-language support (highest-impact i18n)" since the PRD was written and has never said anything else, so this item settled the two questions that decide the shape before writing any code.

**What it decided — D-122, and both halves are load-bearing.** *A cookie and a header toggle, with the URLs unchanged.* Locale rides in `st_locale`, `<html lang>` follows it, and `/storage/tx/austin/demo-austin-south` is one URL in both languages. The alternative was an `app/[locale]` segment with `/es/…` and hreflang; it was refused because PRD 04 §3 already scopes multilingual SEO out ("English-only in MVP"), so indexable Spanish pages would have been a bigger change than the commitment asked for *and* a silent reversal of a written decision. Googlebot carries no cookie and keeps seeing exactly the English pages it indexes today. *And the translation covers the INTERFACE, not the CONTENT.* Translated: every label, button, heading, status, error and instruction on the move-in path plus the shared chrome. Not translated, each for a reason rather than for want of time — operator-typed data (facility copy, amenities, reviews, unit names, promotion terms) because it is somebody's own words; generated SEO prose and the guides because they are what the crawler reads and the crawler reads English; the admin surface because staff screens are English throughout; and **anything a lawyer wrote** because a translated disclosure recorded against an English version constant is evidence of a consent nobody gave. D-15's lexicon binds in Spanish too — tamaño / unidad / precio en línea / precio en tienda / horario de la puerta / horario de oficina — with `contrato` keeping the agreement out of the customer's Spanish exactly as D-15 keeps "lease" out of their English. Register is **usted**, not tú.

**What it built.** `apps/web/lib/i18n/`: `en.ts` and `es.ts` as flat dictionaries, a pure `index.ts` (`translate`, `plural`, `isLocale`), and `server.ts` — the only file that touches `next/headers`. Server components take the dictionary from `getLocale()`; client components read it from one `LocaleProvider` mounted in the public layout, so a new client component cannot forget to be passed one. A `LanguageToggle` in the header: one button per language, each carrying its own `lang` (SC 3.1.2, so "Español" is not read with English phonemes), the current one `aria-current` and still focusable, driven by a server action so it works with the bundle off like the rest of the public path. Stripe's Payment Element gets `locale` — without it a Spanish checkout ends at a card form labelled in English, which is the last screen before the charge and the worst place to make somebody guess. **No dependency was added.**

**The type is the completeness check.** `es` is typed as `Dictionary` (the keys of `en`, values widened to `string`), so a key added and not translated fails `npm run typecheck` rather than rendering `checkout.pay` to a visitor. `tests/i18n.test.ts` covers the half types cannot see: a translation that **drops** a `{placeholder}` (the sentence renders with the number missing) or **invents** one (the reader sees literal braces). That test was verified against a deliberately broken translation before being trusted.

**Four real defects, every one found by running the thing rather than reading it.**

1. **The language toggle broke 26 of the 320px reflow specs.** The header has wrapped since B-082 and its comment says so — but the `<nav>` inside it was `flex` with no `flex-wrap`, so the header wrapped and its nav could not. Four controls fitted 320px and six did not, and every reflow spec failed on a horizontally scrolling page (SC 1.4.10). "The page reflows to 320px without sideways scrolling" is a bullet in the accessibility statement's *"What is true today"*, so it was briefly a false one on this branch.
2. **`next/headers` beside the dictionaries broke `next build`** — after typecheck and 4,148 unit tests had passed. `index.ts` exports `translate`, which client components need, so importing `cookies` there dragged a server-only module into the browser bundle. Split into `server.ts`. Nothing but a production build could have caught it, which is the argument for the convention that e2e runs against one.
3. **`getLocale()` threw "cookies was called outside a request scope" in six TCPA-consent unit tests.** They call the checkout server actions directly, as tests and scripts legitimately do. Fixed once in `getLocale` rather than at each caller — no request means no cookie, and no cookie already means English, so the fallback is the same answer by a different route.
4. **"Quedan solo 1".** Spanish agrees the verb with the count where English does not, so `facility.onlyLeft` needed a singular/plural split English can skip. Found by rendering a facility page with one unit left, not by reading the dictionary.

**Accessibility coverage was re-checked and it had quietly narrowed.** The a11y route loops carry no locale cookie, so all of them scan English — and the site now ships a second set of strings no scan had ever seen. Rather than leave the "automated tests on every page" claim covering a language it does not, `SCANNED_STATES` gains the facility page in Spanish (scanned by axe **and** measured for reflow, zoom and text spacing through `STATE_REACH`, because Spanish runs ~20% longer and that route is the tightest public layout at 320px), and `STATE_EXCEPTIONS` names the rest of the public site and the checkout as English-only. Both render on the public page. `STATE_REACH` gained a `public` audience — every other one signs somebody in, and a Spanish visitor is nobody holding a cookie. `LAST_REVIEWED` is **not** bumped: an axe run in a second language is exactly the evidence B-254 says cannot move that date.

**What it left behind, deliberately, each with its own row.** **B-259** — the three consent disclosures on checkout step 1 are still English, and translating them into the dictionary is the change that looks like a one-liner and cannot be undone after a dispute; they need their own version constants and a legal read. **B-260** — the Spanish stops at the move-in path: the city and size landing pages, the facility FAQs, the guides, the static/legal pages and, biggest of all, **the portal a renter is sent to at the end of checkout** ("Ir a mi cuenta" lands on an English page). **B-261** — the cookie is a browser preference and nothing durable, so every email and text still goes out in English, including the dunning ladder, to the account least able to read it and most likely to end in a lien file; that needs a `Tenant.preferredLocale` and a Spanish variant per seeded template.

**Test verification.** Typecheck clean including `tsconfig.tests.json`; lint clean (6 warnings, all pre-existing `_prev`/`_formData`, unchanged in count from B-258); `prisma migrate diff --exit-code` reports no drift (no migration in this item). Unit suite **4,148 passed, 8 skipped, 254 files** — reconciled, not just exit 0. e2e on `desktop-chrome`: the new `e2e/i18n.spec.ts` (8 passed, including an axe scan of the Spanish facility page), `a11y.spec.ts` + `consent-banner.spec.ts` (130 passed, the reflow regression above), `a11y-own-spec-routes.spec.ts` + `i18n.spec.ts` (29 passed), and `smoke.spec.ts` + `checkout-unit-lost.spec.ts` + `portal.spec.ts` + `i18n.spec.ts` (153 passed). The English strings were restored character-for-character after a first pass had silently replaced `&apos;` with a curly apostrophe — this item changes the language a page can be read in, not a word of the English.

### B-260 part 1 — The portal in Spanish (2026-09-05)

**Commit:** `dbda712`

B-090f translated the move-in path and then handed the renter a button reading "Ir a mi cuenta" that landed on an English account. This is the other side of that button: all seventeen portal routes, the nav, the shell, and the components the portal shares with checkout.

**What it built.** `/portal` and its sixteen sub-routes — overview, pay, receipt, payment methods, statements (list, per-lease, per-account), documents (list and viewer), payment plan, protection, contact details, notifications, refer, transfer, move-out, access. The portal sits outside the `(public)` route group, so it inherits neither `LocaleProvider` nor the toggle; both are mounted in `app/portal/layout.tsx` for the reasons they are mounted on the public side, and the toggle is *in* the portal deliberately — a tenant who chose Spanish while renting has no other way back to English once signed in.

**Four shared catalogues moved from words to keys, and one of those was a correctness fix.** `recurringParts` now returns tokens (`rent | tax | protection`) with `chargePartsSentence` naming them, so `@storage/core` keeps the arithmetic the billing tests pin and holds no customer copy; `listParts` takes its conjunction so Spanish gets "y" without an `Intl.ListFormat` that would have added an Oxford comma the English deliberately does not have. `SHARED_ACCESS_PRESETS` and `REFERRAL_STATE_LABELS` carry `labelKey`s, and the admin screens that read them resolve against `en` — the admin surface is English throughout (D-122). **`PORTAL_TRANSFER_PROBLEM_KEYS` stopped inheriting the ADMIN refusals**, which was overdue independently of language: the portal was showing tenants sentences written for staff, including "This unit is in the lien pipeline. Moving a tenant out of it needs a facility manager or above."

**`statementLabel` became locale-aware through `Intl.DateTimeFormat` rather than a hand-written `MONTH_NAMES` template.** Spanish puts "de" between the month and the year, so `${month} ${year}` is wrong in Spanish however the month is spelled. English output is unchanged character-for-character and the existing assertions still pin it.

**Two real defects found along the way, neither of them language bugs.**

1. **`/portal/methods` named the wrong day for the next autopay charge.** It formatted with a bare `Intl.DateTimeFormat('en-US', …)` and no `timeZone`, against a date `nextBillingDate` holds at UTC midnight — so in every US timezone it rendered the day before.
