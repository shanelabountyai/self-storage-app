### B-303 — the exception report named a remedy the product could not carry out (2026-09-15, `4e7b60e`)

The second row of the eighth review block and the one the block calls its most valuable. `/admin/reports/ledger-exceptions` had stated the repair in its own words since B-277 — *"an adjustment on that lease's ledger, or a corrected invoice"* — and **neither existed**. Every writer of `ledgerEntry.create` was an automated path (`transfer.ts`, `reversals.ts`, `move-out.ts`, `payments/reconcile.ts`, `checkout/provision.ts`, `billing/refunds.ts`, `late-fees.ts`, `invoices.ts`, `fee-invoice.ts`, `allocation.ts`, `auctions/service.ts`); `write_off` was written in exactly one place, `closeMoveOut`, so an **open** lease's bad debt could not be written off at all; and a rent invoice could be voided by nobody, `waiveFeeInvoice` refusing anything whose `kind` is not `fee`. The manager opened the report at 7am, saw the list, and there was no button. Meanwhile the delinquency engine cures on the ledger balance, so a lease on that list can be a tenant who has paid and is still overlocked; `claimForNotice` refuses a lien notice on exactly those leases; and a lease that can never be noticed can never be auctioned while the balance compounds and the unit stays full of somebody else's belongings. The only path to agreement was a database client.

**What it built.** `apps/web/lib/billing/corrections.ts` — three operations, one gate.

1. **`postLedgerAdjustment`** restates a lease so its ledger and its invoices agree. It takes a **signed balance change**, not an entry amount, and zero is a legitimate answer (see the decision below). It writes one or two `adjustment` entries, never touches a `Payment` row and never touches an invoice.
2. **`writeOffOpenLeaseBalance`** forgives what an open lease owes as bad debt. It marks each invoice it covers `uncollectible` — the enum member that has existed since the first migration and had no writer — and posts a write-off entry per invoice plus one bare entry for any uninvoiced remainder, so the lease **still reconciles afterwards**. The lease stays `active`.
3. **`voidRentInvoice`** withdraws a rent invoice that should never have been raised: the cancelling entry is posted beside the charge, carrying the invoice id, and the invoice goes to `void` — the same shape `waiveFeeInvoice` uses for a fee, deliberately as a second function rather than a widening of the first.
4. **`LedgerEntry.isCorrection`** (migration `20260915120000_b303_ledger_correction`), excluded from `reconciliationInputs`' uninvoiced term. **Without it no correction can move the difference at all** — see below.
5. **UI.** A "Corrections" section on `/admin/tenants/[tenantId]/ledger/[leaseId]`, gated on `credits:manual`, with the three controls and their reason-code vocabularies; server actions in that route's new `actions.ts`; `leaseLedger` now returns `voidableInvoices` and `canCorrect`. The exception report's prose says where the button is, and says so to a staffer who does not have the authority too.
6. **Audit.** `ledger.adjusted` and `invoice.voided` join the catalog, both `requiresReason: true`. `balance.written_off` was already there and is reused.
7. **`tests/ledger-corrections-db.test.ts`** — 9 tests for this row, building both of B-292's shapes the way the real paths leave them.

**What it decided.**

- **The correction takes a BALANCE CHANGE, not an entry amount, and that is the whole design.** **One ledger entry provably cannot repair both of the shapes B-292 named as permanent**, which is not obvious and was found by doing the arithmetic rather than by reading the row. With `difference = ledgerBalance − invoiceOutstanding − uninvoiced`: a payment split across units before B-257 leaves lease Q saying a tenant owes $100 they have paid, so the **balance** is wrong and has to move by −$100; but an invoice carried away by a transfer leaves the old lease's balance **already correct at $0** and only its classification wrong, and moving the balance there would hand the tenant a $50 credit they do not have — which the delinquency engine would then cure on. So the operator states the balance change, and the function derives the rest: the uninvoiced term must move by `difference + change`, which is one ordinary entry of that amount (landing on both sides) and one `isCorrection` entry of the remainder (landing only on the balance). Either can come out at zero and is then not written, so a lease that already reconciles gets exactly one plain adjustment.
- **`isCorrection` rather than attaching every correction to an invoice.** `transfer.ts` already gets the same effect by putting its entry pair against the invoice they move, and its comment explains why. That does not reach B-292's second shape, where the gap sits on a lease whose invoice has gone elsewhere and there is nothing local to attach to. Existing rows are all `false` by construction: every adjustment written before this came from a transfer (which carries an invoice id) or a payment reversal (which carries a payment id).
- **One permission, not a fourth.** All three go through `checkMonetaryAuthority(actor, 'credit', …)` — `credits:manual` plus B-197's per-role `maxCreditCents` — measured on the **magnitude**, so a correction that adds debt to a tenant's account is capped exactly as one that removes it. The row was explicit that this must not mint a new permission shape.
- **The write-off settles the invoices behind it.** A write-off that only reduced the ledger would leave the invoices outstanding and the lease failing to reconcile from that moment on — it would MAKE an exception of the kind this row exists to clear. `uncollectible` rather than `void`: the money was genuinely owed and the revenue report has to be able to tell forgiven debt from an invoice that should never have existed.
- **The whole balance, not a typed amount.** An invoice bigger than what is left to write off is left alone rather than part-written-off, which would invent a third state for it. That only arises on a lease that did not reconcile to begin with, and the correction above is the tool for that.
- **Nothing is pre-filled.** The form's hint states the number to type when the balance is what is wrong; it does not put it in the box. A pre-filled restatement of what somebody owes is one press away from being posted by a staffer who did not decide it.

**Verification.** Typecheck clean, including `tsconfig.tests.json`. Lint: 0 errors, the same 6 pre-existing warnings. `prisma migrate diff --exit-code`: no difference. `db:migrate:test` and `db:migrate:e2e` both applied.

**What it left behind.**

- **A voided rent invoice's period can never be billed again — `B-327`**, raised for it. `invoice_one_rent_per_period` does not exclude voided rows. Releasing it is four lines of SQL and was **deliberately not done**: `markDiscountApplied` and `markReferralRewardApplied` are consumed per period inside the transaction that raised the original invoice, so a bare re-raise would issue the corrected invoice without the promotion the tenant was promised. The working repair today is `postFeeCharge`, and it produces a `fee` invoice, so the period reads as unbilled on every report that counts rent.
- **`/admin/reports/ledger-exceptions` has no e2e coverage**, which is B-277's gap rather than this row's. The lease ledger screen does — `a11y-own-spec-routes.spec.ts` scans it, and so scans the new section.
- **Nothing reverses a correction.** A correction posted in error is corrected by another correction, which is what append-only means here, and is the same posture `reversals.ts` takes.

### B-305 — at the counter, picking a unit for a business-account payer did not pay that unit (2026-09-15, `d51e772`)

`recordCounterPayment` passed no narrowing to `applyPayment`, so a counter payment went through `claimsFor(tenantId, facilityId)` — `{ tenantId } OR { the account this tenant pays for }`. When the subject was a lease whose tenant **is** an account's payer, the cash spread oldest-first across every unit on the account regardless of which unit the staffer selected, while the form showed that lease's own balance and a "Pay in full" button derived from it. Acme's foreman hands over $161 cash for C-7, the manager picks "C-7 — $161 due", and B-278's receipt comes back honestly naming C-3. The foreman's unit is still open, still ageing, still walking toward an overlock on a tenant who paid.

**Which remedy, and why.** The row offered two. **`restrictToInvoiceIds` — the allocation, not the wording.** A before-submit statement would have made a paragraph of small print the only thing standing between a payer and money landing on somebody else's unit; a staffer taking cash at a desk reads the picker and the amount. The statement was written anyway (see below), but as a description of what the code now does rather than as the protection.

**What it built.**

1. **`CounterPaymentInput.restrictToLease`** — when the counter's picker named one unit, `recordCounterPayment` reads that lease's open and partially-paid invoices inside the payment transaction and passes them to `applyPayment`. `takePaymentAction` sets it for every unit pick (`!accountId`).
2. **`applyPayment`'s `restrictIsAbsolute`** — B-189's narrowing falls back to every claim when nothing in the restricted set is still claimable, because a plan installment whose covered invoices were settled by another payment must still land somewhere. A directed counter payment must do the opposite: a unit that owes nothing takes the cash as **credit on that unit**, never as a payment on the employer's arrears. One flag, honoured in one line.
3. **The form states it.** The unit branch's standing paragraph now reads "Settles unit N only; anything over its balance stays as credit on it", in both the current-lease and former-tenant cases. The account branch's wording is unchanged and still says it settles oldest first across the named units.
4. **Three tests.** Two in `tests/counter-account-payment-db.test.ts` — a payer's payment against one named unit **of** their account, and a payer's own 5×5 that is on **no** account (which also covers the overpayment and the owes-nothing case) — both asserting the ledger, the allocations and `paymentCredits`, so the receipt and the assertion cannot disagree. One in `tests/payment-plan-autopay-db.test.ts` pinning B-203 under the new restriction. Both new tests were confirmed to **fail** with the narrowing disabled and pass with it.

**What it decided.**

- **Opt-in per call site, not blanket inside `recordCounterPayment`.** The other caller is B-230's walk-in move-in (`counter-tender.ts`), which passes the **first lease of a basket that can hold two units** (B-106) and relies on one payment settling both move-in invoices. A blanket restriction there would have left the second unit unpaid and banked the difference as credit on the first — the same defect, introduced by its fix. The counter picker is the only place a person *directs* money at a unit, and that is where the flag is set.

### B-329 — the staff mirror: English under `lang="es"`, and Spanish under `lang="en"` (2026-09-16)

**Commit:** `c1e88a8`

B-328 closed SC 3.1.2 across the public site and left the staff side open on purpose, because the two halves are not the same job. `st_locale` is set from the public header and read by the root layout, so a staff user who ever pressed *Español* on the way in gets `<html lang="es">` around **every** admin screen for the life of that cookie.

**What it built.** One `lang="en"` on `app/admin/layout.tsx`, and `lang={locale}` on the four surfaces of the message-template editor that render Spanish inside it: the locale links, the subject input, the body textarea and the preview. Plus `TRANSLATED_UNDER_THE_ADMIN_SHELL`, `ADMIN_SHELL_LAYOUT` and a second walker.

**What it decided.**

- **The guard is a different SHAPE, not a copy of B-328's, and that is the part worth reading before extending it.** `(public)` has no shell that can speak for all of it — it wraps pages that are translated and pages that are not — so its claim has to be per page, and the list is that claim. Admin is the opposite: nothing under `app/admin` renders a dictionary string except the template editor, so ONE attribute is true for the whole surface at once. Eighty rows saying "English" would be eighty chances to forget one, guarding a fact a single attribute already states. So the admin half checks two things instead of a list: that the layout still declares its language, and that any admin page which DOES render from the dictionary is listed and declares the locale back.
- **The check requires a DYNAMIC `lang={locale}`, not a literal.** A `lang="en"` on a mixed admin page is the mirror defect — the failure the shell attribute would otherwise introduce one level down — so matching a literal would have passed the exact state the row exists to prevent.
- **The `lang` goes on the CONTROL, not on its label.** "Subject" and "Message" are English admin chrome; the VALUE is the template, and it is Spanish when the editor is on the Spanish half of the catalog. `Field` needed no change to carry it: `lang` arrives through `React.InputHTMLAttributes` and is spread onto the `<input>` alone, which is exactly where it belongs.
- **The row named three of the four surfaces; the PREVIEW was the fourth and is the one most worth marking**, because it is the only place the template is read as prose rather than as the value of a form control.
- **The admin layout's comment was written NOT to contain the literal `lang="en"`.** An early draft did, and the shell check — `toContain('lang="en"')` — would then have been satisfied by the comment describing the attribute rather than by the attribute. Caught by deleting the attribute and watching the test pass. The comment says "one shell-level declaration" instead, and the probe now fails as it should.

**Both admin guards were proven to bite**: deleting the layout's attribute fails "keeps the admin shell declaring its language"; stripping the editor's `lang={locale}` fails "makes every listed admin page declare the other language back".

**Not customer-facing**, so no rendered sentence on the public accessibility statement changed in either direction; adding one would be a new public claim about screens no customer reaches.

**Found — the split's own SHA guard made the repo's two-commit workflow un-greenable, and it was found by following it.** `tests/docs-retrieval.test.ts` failed every `B-` entry whose commit cell in the generated index is an em dash. CLAUDE.md says to write the entry, commit, and then record the SHA in a **small follow-up commit** — because amending would change the SHA just written down — so between those two commits an entry legitimately carries none, and the guard failed the first of the two every single time.
