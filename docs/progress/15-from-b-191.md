### B-191 — A payment plan told the tenant nothing, and went quietest at the moment it broke

`6e3c9aa`

**What it built.** PRD 05 **CN-24**, in full: four messages, plus the dashboard card that was the entire tenant-facing signal before them and was wrong in two ways.

Four new events (`payment_plan.agreed`, `.installment_due_soon`, `.broken`, `.completed`), each with a seeded template and rule, all on entity `Lease` with `planId` in the payload — a lease can have a chain of plans (D-98) and the message is about one of them. **Agreed** is emitted inside `createPaymentPlan`'s own transaction and lists every installment with its date and amount, the total, what the plan pauses and what happens if a payment is missed. **Installment due** is raised by a new `emitInstallmentReminders` sweep riding with the hour-4 payment-plan job. **Broken** is emitted in the same beat that lifts the hold, so the tenant hears it from us at hour 4 rather than from the dunning ladder at 5 or the keypad. **Kept** is emitted on completion.

**What it decided. The reminder's lead time is the facility's existing `invoiceLeadDays`, not a fifth number beside it.** CN-24 asks for "a per-facility configurable number of days ahead", and this facility already has a setting whose meaning is exactly that — how many days before money is due the tenant hears about it — with a control on `/admin/settings` and an operator who has already chosen a value. A new column would have been a second knob configuring one intent, which is the accumulation this repo's own first hard-learned rule is about, and it would have arrived with a fifth field in the billing-policy form to keep it honest. **The consequence is stated rather than hidden: an operator who changes their invoice reminder lead time changes their plan reminder lead time too.** That is the right coupling — both answer "how much warning do we give before a payment is due" — but a later item that needs them to differ should split the column, not read this note as permission to guess.

**Once-per-installment comes from the `JobRun`, not from a stamp.** The scheduler holds a unique `(jobName, facilityId, businessDate)`, so a catch-up tick walking five business dates emits each installment's reminder on the one date it is due. That is exactly what `emitDueReminders` has relied on for invoices since B-044, and it is why neither needed a `reminderSentAt` column. **No migration in this item at all.**

**The reminder deliberately carries no autopay skip**, which is the opposite of CN-1's rule for an ordinary invoice and is why CN-24 says so in as many words: a tenant who believes a payment is automatic and is wrong loses the plan over a misunderstanding. The message states which kind of plan it is instead, through `plan.collection_line` — and that line reports D-97's **effective** answer (`isAutoCollecting`: the plan's own opt-in AND autopay on AND a card saved), read fresh at send time. A plan agreed as automatic against a card since removed now says "not taken automatically" in the message, not "we'll take care of it". The three-part expression existed in three copies; it is now one function in `packages/core/payment-plans` used by the plan view, the breach job and the message.

**None of the four skips `tenant_moved_out`, unlike almost every rule above them.** A plan is about ARREARS, which outlive the tenancy. Suppressing the break notice because the lease had ended would reproduce, on the one lease most likely to have one, the exact silence the rule exists to end.

**FR-9a now holds for every templated email in the product, not only these four.** The acceptance criterion named B-084 part 3's standard, which had only ever been met by the *generated* report kind. Every seeded template here ships text-only, so `renderEmail`'s fallback IS the HTML body of essentially every message the product sends — and it was one `<p>` of the entire body with `<br>`s in it, no language, no heading, and the merged values interpolated **raw**: a facility genuinely called "Bob & Sons" emitted broken markup into every message it sent. It is now a language-declared wrapper with the subject as a single `<h1>`, one `<p>` per block, single line breaks preserved inside a block, and every value escaped. Fixed once in the shared function rather than four times in four seeded bodies, which is also what makes it survive a template edit.

**What FR-9a still does not get, and why it was raised rather than half-built.** CN-24 asks for the schedule as a real `<table>` with a `<caption>` and `<th scope>`. That needs an HTML body of its own, and **a seeded `bodyHtml` would be a lie waiting to happen**: CN-16's editor (`lib/admin/templates.ts`) writes `bodyHtml: null` on every save, so the first staff edit of that template would silently downgrade it back to the fallback with nobody told. The card states all three cases in words, never by colour (1.4.1 A), and stays a server-rendered `role="status"` present at page load rather than inserted on change (4.1.3 AA). `completed` and `cancelled` are still null here — a permanent route to a plan's history is B-193's.

### B-197 — The four limits that decide who may give money away were reachable only from a database client

`0be870b`

**What it built.** `/admin/settings/roles` — one org-level screen showing every staff role against the four monetary limits on it (`maxFeeWaiverCents`, `maxRefundCents`, `maxCreditCents` and D-98's `maxPlanDeferralCents`), and the forms that change them. Until this row those four columns were seeded by `packages/db/rbac-catalog.ts` and written by **nothing anywhere in the product**, which made PRD 02 RBAC-2 — "starting values the owner can change in configuration; they are not hardcoded policy" — false in as many words. An operator who wanted their managers to waive $100 rather than $50 had to ring somebody.

Beside role and permission management rather than in facility settings, because the limits live on `Role` and a role is one row for the whole portfolio: there is no per-site version of "what a facility manager may waive" to put on a facility's own page. The gate is therefore `users:manage` asked with a **null facilityId**, the same question `/admin/settings/staff` asks — only an owner, or a manager assigned to every facility, satisfies it. Every save is audited as `role.limits_changed` with all four limits on both sides.

**What it decided.**

- **The ladder check is scoped to the roles that hold the permission, and to the fields the save actually moved.** `nextApproverRole` walks roles above the actor's rank in ascending order and stops at the first whose limit covers the amount — so a lower-ranked role with a bigger limit means every over-limit request escalates to somebody with *less* authority than the person who asked, and the rung above never sees it. `ladderViolation` refuses that at the point of editing. It compares only roles that hold the action's permission, because those are exactly the roles `nextApproverRole` considers; an inert number on a role that cannot waive a fee is not in the chain and cannot break it. And it checks only the actions a save changed: validating all four every time means a ladder that was already crooked when the screen loaded blocks an unrelated field, and the only way to fix it is the database client this row exists to remove.
- **Roles at the same rank are not compared.** Counter staff and the bookkeeper are both rank 10. Neither is above the other, so neither is where the other's over-limit request goes.
- **Blank is unlimited, `0` is no authority, and nothing in the chain may collapse them.** `null` sorts as `Infinity` in the ladder check, not as zero — the two would get the comparison exactly backwards. `parseScaled` is never reached for an empty box (it refuses an empty string by design), and `describe()` is the single place either becomes words, used by the table, the success announcement and the refusal message alike. This is the same `undefined`-is-not-`null` distinction `checkMonetaryAuthority` already carries a paragraph about.
- **Five forms, one per role, rather than inputs inside the table.** A cell-level input would have to reach its `AdminForm` through the `form=` owner attribute (the B-180 pattern), which carries the DOM relationship but not the React context `Field` reads its error from — so 3.3.1 would have been satisfied by a summary at the top of the page and nothing beside the field that caused it. The table stays read-only and says today's figures; the forms sit under it.
- **`LIMIT_FIELD` and `REQUIRED_PERMISSION` are exported from `authorize.ts` rather than re-declared.** Which column carries which limit, and which permission gates it, is one fact — a second copy in the editing screen is a second thing to keep true.

**Accessibility (PRD 02 §5.5).** A real `<table>` with `<th scope="col">` on each limit and `<th scope="row">` on the role name, inside the same focusable `overflow-x-auto` wrapper the staff table uses. Every amount field is named after its role *and* its act — "Fee waiver limit for Facility Manager (dollars)", never four fields called "Limit" on a page carrying five forms (2.4.6) — and each form is labelled for its role for the same reason. Unlimited and "nothing (no authority)" are stated in words in every cell, never by a blank alone (3.3.2). A refusal is keyed to the action, which is the name of the input it belongs beside (3.3.1). A role that does not hold the permission behind a limit says so, in the cell and in the field hint, rather than letting somebody raise a figure that changes nothing.

**No claim on the public accessibility statement changes, and that is deliberate:** every surface here is under `/admin`, which the statement makes no claims about. The route IS added to `ADMIN_SCAN_ROUTES`, so `admin.spec.ts` scans and reflow-checks it like every other admin page — B-139's contract refuses a route that appears in neither list — and the reasoning is written into that page's header so the next reader does not have to re-derive it.

**A real bug found along the way:** none. The defect was the absence itself.

**Test verification.** Typecheck (including `tsconfig.tests.json`) clean; lint clean (7 pre-existing warnings, unrelated); `prisma migrate diff --exit-code` reports **no difference** — no migration in this row, because the columns already existed. Full unit suite **3,865 passed, 8 skipped of 3,873** (229 files passed, 1 skipped), exit 0. `tests/role-limits-db.test.ts` adds ten: five against real rows (the save and its audit both sides, the ladder refusal writing nothing at all, an unknown role, the org-level gate refusing a single-facility manager, and the listing excluding the non-staff `tenant` role) and five pure ones covering every shape the ladder comparison has to get right — including the two it must **not** complain about. The targeted e2e for the new route passed **10/10** on both viewports.

**A shared-table trap the fixtures had to dodge.** `role` is global to the whole test schema, so a fixture role holding a real permission *and* a real limit is visible to every other suite's `nextApproverRole` for as long as the file runs. Both fixtures therefore sit at $0 throughout — an escalation only ever considers a role whose limit covers the amount, so a $0 rung can never be picked — and the one test that raises a limit above $0 on a role holding `fees:waive` is the test that is **refused**, which writes nothing. The staff fixture is not reclaimed and cannot be: `audit_log`'s RESTRICT foreign key pins its author forever (B-185), and `npm run db:reset-test` is the only cleanup.

**Left behind.** **The ladder is checked on save and never again**, so inserting a role *below* an existing one with a bigger limit still produces a crooked chain that only the next edit of that action reports — creating and ranking roles is not built and **B-004** still owns it. **There is no approval queue for an over-limit action**, which is the same posture `waiveFeeInvoice`, B-167's charge control and B-190's plan builder all already take; B-048 owns it. **Nothing warns that lowering a limit has no effect on what is already approved** beyond the sentence at the top of the screen — no in-flight approval is revisited, by design.

`f859549`

Not a backlog row. The session was scoped to demo configuration — a real org phone number, outbound email, the shared password gate — and the second of those turned up a defect that had to be fixed before the configuration it blocked could be applied safely.

### B-199 — Seven wide staff tables clipped their own action links on a phone, and the reflow test could not see it

`f9a9823`

**What it built.** The `overflow-x-auto` wrapper this codebase already uses for a wide table, on the seven admin tables that did not have one: the tenant profile's leases table (the one the row was about), the billing runs table, the POS deposit-slip table, two report tables, the rent roll, and the tenant search results. On a 375px phone the leases table's last column holds up to four action links — Ledger, Notices, Move out, Transfer — and they sat outside the document, untappable and unreachable by scrolling.

Six e2e tests had recorded it as a `mobile-chrome` flake for four items running. All six now pass on both projects, which is what the row named as its proof.

**What it decided.**

- **The floor is the load-bearing half, and the row did not say so.** `overflow-x-auto` on its own fixes nothing here: `w-full` sizes the table to exactly the wrapper's width, so the wrapper never has anything to scroll and seven columns are crushed into 327px instead. The links become reachable and unusable. The pairing is `w-full` **plus `min-w-2xl`** (or `min-w-max` past eight columns), which is what every already-wrapped table in this repo uses — the pattern was there to copy and copying half of it would have passed the six tests while leaving the screen unusable.
- **Seven tables, not one, and not seventeen.** The row asked for a check across every admin table wider than four columns, and that is an audit rather than a fix of the page the tests named. ### B-198 — The template's second HTML body is deleted, and the schedule became a real table anyway

`cb4e6f1`

**What it built.** `MessageTemplate.bodyHtml` is gone — the column, the schema field, the `checkPublishable` input, both of the unconditional `bodyHtml: null` writes in the editor, and the branch in `renderEmail` that would have used it. Migration `20260828175542_drop_message_template_body_html`, verified beforehand against the dev database: 40 template rows, **0** with a non-null value, so nothing was lost.

In its place, one thing the second body could never have done safely: **a structured merge value.** `MergeValue = string | { text, html }`. Most merged values stay a plain string. A value that is inherently tabular supplies both renderings, built side by side by whoever has the data. `plan.schedule` is the first: the numbered list a tenant reads in the text part, and a captioned table with `<th scope="col">` and `<th scope="row">` in the HTML part, both from the one array of installments — through `tableHtml`, which B-084 part 3 already wrote, reviewed and tested for report emails, now exported rather than re-implemented.

The HTML part of a templated email is now built from the **template body**, not from the already-rendered text: literal text escaped, merged values escaped or contributing their own markup, `<br>` only where the author put a line break. A structured value alone in its own paragraph owns that block unwrapped, because a `<table>` inside a `<p>` is invalid and the browser's fixup for it strands the caption.

**What it decided.**

- **D-102: one body per template, and the HTML part is that body rendered as markup.** The row asked for the deletion to be priced against the build, and the deletion won on evidence rather than taste: the column was null on every row that ever existed, the editor wrote it null on every save, and no message in the product's life ever rendered from it. Reversing this is a migration.
- **CN-24's table was never a body problem.** It is one merge field whose value is tabular. That reframing is the whole item — it turns an M with a second editable document, a second publish gate and a permanent drift surface into a union type and a helper, and it meets the criterion more completely, because two renderings of one array cannot disagree about a date the way two authored bodies can.
- **The deleted column was also an escaping hole.** `renderString` interpolated a `bodyHtml` **raw**. Had anyone ever seeded one, a facility called "Bob & Sons" would have emitted broken markup from the very body an operator had been told was the safer, richer one — the exact bug B-191 fixed on the text path. Nobody found this because nobody could reach the code. It left with the column.
- **`tableHtml` was exported, not copied.** One table implementation in the product, reviewed once. A tenant's schedule and a staff report email are now accessible in the same way for the same reason.
- **The text part keeps the numbered list, deliberately.** `tableText`'s monospace column padding is right for a report a staffer opens and wrong for a tenant's mail client in a proportional font. Same document, two renderings — which is what FR-9a asks for and not the same as "both parts identical".

**Accessibility.** CN-24's email criteria are met in full for the first time: `lang` on the wrapper, the subject as the single `<h1>`, `<caption>` and both header scopes on the schedule, every merged value escaped, and a text part built from the same document rather than by stripping tags. The public accessibility statement gets a new entry and **no claim on it moves** — it says nothing about email in either direction, and it should not start; but the B-191 entry's written statement that the schedule "is a numbered list rather than a `<table>`" had gone false, so it is corrected in place rather than left reading as current. `LAST_REVIEWED` does not move.

**Test verification.** Full unit suite green: **3872 passed + 8 skipped = 3880**, exit 0, 231 files. Typecheck clean including `tsconfig.tests.json`. `prisma migrate diff --exit-code`: **no difference**. `db:migrate:test` and `db:migrate:e2e` both run. Two checks were added where the logic actually is: `tests/comms-render.test.ts` covers a structured value unwrapped in its own block, inline in a sentence, absent from the text part, and empty (which must still throw `RenderError`); `tests/payment-plan-comms-db.test.ts` asserts the caption, both header scopes and the absence of `<p><table` **on a real send**, so the table is checked end to end rather than only in the renderer.

**Left behind.** **The move-out settlement and invoice figures are still prose, not tables.** The row listed them alongside the schedule; they are not CN-24 criteria, no acceptance criterion names them, and each one now costs a `scheduleValue`-shaped helper and nothing else — so they get done when a row asks for them rather than speculatively here. **No template offers rich formatting to an operator**, which is the thing the deleted column looked like it might one day become; D-102 says what would have to be true to revisit it. **B-200 and B-201 are still open** and untouched by this item.

### B-201 — The reflow check that `[contain:layout]` cannot mask, and the seven defects it found on green routes

`bf21eba`

**What it built.** Two halves of one gap, both named by B-199 and both closed here.

**(1) The seven dynamic routes are checked at 320px, 200% zoom and forced text spacing.** `SCANNED_BY_OWN_SPEC` exists because a route with a `[param]` segment needs a live fixture to render its real content, and B-156 correctly gave each one an axe scan inside its own topic spec. It did that for axe and only for axe: `/admin/tenants/[tenantId]`, its ledger, notices, move-out and transfer, plus `/portal/pay` and `/portal/transfer`, had never been measured at any width by anything. B-199's defect lived on one of them for four items. `e2e/a11y-own-spec-routes.spec.ts` reaches all seven through **one** table of click-throughs rather than seven copies pasted into seven topic files, and a route in `SCANNED_BY_OWN_SPEC` with no entry in that table is a failing test named for the route — so this cannot fall behind the axe coverage again the way it already had.

**(2) The assertion no longer goes through the document.** Every reflow, zoom and text-spacing loop in the suite asked one question of one element, `document.documentElement.scrollWidth > clientWidth`. B-116 put `contain: layout` on admin's `<main>` so a correctly-wrapped table's own scroll region would stop Chromium's root-level scrollWidth walk, and it stops the walk either way — an unwrapped wide table overflows inside a containing block the document cannot see past, so the root reports 320 while the columns are unreachable by any means. `expectNoHorizontalOverflow` asks instead, per element, whether anything is **painted past the right edge of the screen that no scrollbar reaches**. The root comparison is still inside it. All four loop sites — public, portal, admin and the new file — route through the one function.

**The seven defects it found on its first run, on four routes that were green throughout.**

- **Six are one bug, and B-116's own comment names one of them.** `Field`'s wrapper `<div>` is a flex item in `AdminForm`'s wrapping row, so its default `min-width: auto` holds it at its own min-content width — and for a `<select>`, that is the **longest option**. B-116's `max-w-full` on `CONTROL_CLASS` then caps the control at 100% of a parent that is already too wide, and the pair cancels out. `protectionRequired` — the control B-116's comment cites as the reason for `max-w-full` — still ran to 365px on a 320px phone, with that fix present and applied. The others: `autoEnrolProtectionOnLapse`, the promotion minimum-stay and transfer-discount settings, `/admin/rate-increases`' tenant picker, and the transfer wizard's unit picker.
- **The seventh** is `GOOGLE_SEARCH_CONSOLE_CLIENT_EMAIL` on `/admin/reports/indexation`, a token with no break opportunity in it, at 347px. `break-all`, the same fix as B-094's JSON example on `/admin/units/setup`.

**What it decided.**

- **`min-w-0` is prepended to every `Field` wrapper, not written into its default class string** — and that distinction is a finding rather than a style choice. Five files (`admin/pos`, the tenant profile, its move-out and transfer wizards, `/portal/contact`) declare a local `FIELD_CLASS` that is a hand-copy of `Field`'s own default and **replaces** it. The first attempt put `min-w-0` in the default: `/admin/settings` and `/admin/reports/indexation` went green and `/admin/rate-increases` and the transfer wizard did not move, because neither uses the default. One line inside the component reaches all of them whatever they were handed; consolidating the five copies would have been a bigger diff for the same result, and the copies are now merely redundant rather than load-bearing.
- **The check is a right-edge test, not a per-element `scrollWidth` test, and the rejected version is worth recording** because it is the obvious one to reach for next time. Comparing `scrollWidth > clientWidth` on every element is more sensitive and was unusable: 60+ findings over seven routes, not one a defect. `sr-only` is `position: absolute`, and a `div.overflow-x-auto` here is `position: static` — so an sr-only span inside a horizontally scrolled table is **not** clipped by that scroll container, escapes to the nearest positioned ancestor, and by itself pushed `<main>`'s scrollWidth to 675px on the tenant profile, which then looked exactly like the defect the check exists to find on a page that renders correctly. Chromium also lays out `content-visibility: hidden` subtrees, so the portal nav's **closed** "Manage" menu reported six links overflowing a 45px column nobody can see. Both exclusions are named in the helper with the reason; `checkVisibility` covers the second and the `sr-only` clip is matched in both spellings the utility has used.
- **`[contain:layout]` stays.** The row required it, B-116 measured it, and its reasoning holds. What was missing was a check it cannot mask, not the removal of a rule that is doing its job.
- **The alarm was verified against a real failing run before being relied on.** Deleting the tenant profile's `overflow-x-auto` reproduces B-199's exact defect, and the check names it — `table.w-full.min-w-2xl.text-sm — right edge at 696px, screen is 320px — "Leases held by this tenant…"` — then goes green when it is restored. The failure message carries the element's classes, its right edge, the screen width and the first 60 characters of its text, for the same reason B-199 put axe's target selectors in `assertNoAxeViolations`: without them a hand check starts by re-running the scan under a debugger.

**Accessibility.** `/portal/pay` and `/portal/transfer` are customer-facing and had never been reflow-, zoom- or text-spacing-checked; both pass all three. The public statement's reflow sentence is unchanged and no claim on it moves — every defect found was on `/admin`, which the page makes no claim about. What did change is what carries that sentence: B-199's entry on the page argued the public claim was safe because `[contain:layout]` is admin-only and the public assertion could therefore still go red. The public loops now use a check that does not depend on that remaining true. `LAST_REVIEWED` does not move.

**Found along the way, and fixed here.** The full sweep turned up the move-out screen's axe scan failing on a `color-contrast` incomplete naming `.text-left > .text-right` — a **tenant profile** element that is not on that route. It is the race B-199 diagnosed on the lien-notices scan and fixed there only: the click-through leaves the profile, whose `<main>` is already visible, so `await expect(getByRole('main')).toBeVisible()` is satisfied instantly and axe can read the old DOM while `page.url()` already reports the new path. The same two lines were sitting in the move-out scan and the ledger scan the whole time; both now `waitForURL` first. It is worth noting that this diagnosed itself — the "some of these are no longer in the DOM, so this scan probably raced a navigation" note B-199 added to `assertNoAxeViolations` is what turned a contrast finding on nodes that do not exist into a one-line fix, which is exactly the work that message was added to save.

**Test verification.** Typecheck clean including `tsconfig.tests.json`. Lint clean (7 pre-existing warnings, none in these files). Full unit suite **3,875 passed, 8 skipped of 3,883**, exit 0. `prisma migrate diff --exit-code`: **no difference** — no schema change, so no migration. Full e2e sweep **1,288 passed, 10 skipped of 1,298**, exit 0, on both `mobile-chrome` and `desktop-chrome`; the four-file reflow sweep was run green immediately before it against the same un-reseeded database, and the new spec writes nothing at all, so B-120's shared-fixture rule is met by the first of its three disciplines rather than by luck. The `min-w-0` change reaches `/checkout`, `/reservations` and eight portal pages, which is why the whole suite was run here rather than left to CI's own lane.
