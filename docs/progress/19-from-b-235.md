### B-235 — "All facilities" answers with a worklist on the six screens that used to refuse

`4de7885`

US-2's own criterion is that in the All facilities context every roll-up-capable screen renders a roll-up rather than a refusal, and D-12 makes owner + all-facilities the *ordinary* unrestricted account — so this is the owner's own default context, not an exotic state. Thirty-two admin screens hard-refuse it and five had a roll-up behind the refusal. That left the regional over ten sites who must approve every lien sale and every ECRI batch switching facility ten times a morning with no signal about which sites had anything waiting. Staff time is the first cost. The compliance edge is real: an auction approval sitting unseen past its sale date means the advertisement ran for a sale that did not happen, which is its own claim.

**What it built.**

1. **`waitingRollup`** in `apps/web/lib/admin/rollups.ts` — one helper, six thin callers. It takes the screen's permissions, its path, a phrase function and a count, and returns one `RollupRow` per facility the actor can both see and act on.
2. **Six roll-ups**: `auctionApprovalRollup`, `rateIncreaseApprovalRollup`, `overlockRollup`, `walkthroughRollup`, `formerTenantRollup`, `protectionGapRollup`, rendered in the `mode !== 'single'` branch of `/admin/auctions`, `/admin/rate-increases`, `/admin/overlocks`, `/admin/walkthrough`, `/admin/tenants/former` and `/admin/reports/protection`.
3. **`?facility=` on the four screens that had none.** Auctions, rate increases, overlocks and walkthrough only ever read the switcher cookie, so a roll-up row would have linked straight back to the refusal it came from.
4. **A named link in `FacilityRollup`** — `aria-label` carrying the facility and its figure.

**What it decided.**

- **Rows count what is WAITING, never what merely exists.** An unapproved open auction case, a batch at `pending_approval`, a lock the controller and our records disagree about, an unconfirmed walk, a balance left behind, an uncovered unit. A count of everything at a site is a number nobody acts on, and it makes a busy site and a blocked site look the same.
- **A zero is stated in words** — "Nothing waiting for approval", "Nobody owing", "No uncovered units". An em dash or a missing row carries the same signal as "I have not looked", which is exactly the state this row exists to remove.
- **The helper filters by permission before calling, and shows nothing rather than a zero.** Those screens' data functions *throw* for a facility the actor lacks the permission at, so the filter is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. It is also the honest answer: a zero shown to somebody who may not see the figure is a claim they cannot make — the same reasoning `moneyOwedRollup` already used to return `[]` for a manager without financial reporting.
- **The screen's own call is the count wherever one exists.** `overlockReconciliation`, `formerTenantDebts` and `coverageGaps` are called per facility, so the roll-up figure is the screen's list length by construction (D-25). Auctions and rate increases are `count` queries instead, because `auctionCasesFor` resolves every case in full — affordable for one facility, not for a portfolio. Named as a deliberate exception rather than left to look like an oversight.
- **The walk says which state a site is in, not a count.** `daily_walkthrough` is one task per facility per day, so "1" is not a figure worth reading out. The row says "Walk not confirmed today" or "Walk confirmed, or not yet raised" — the second phrasing deliberately covers both, because the job that raises the task runs on its own schedule and a site it has not reached yet has not failed anything.
- **The roll-up is a router; no list is merged.** Every refusal keeps its reason and every reason is still true — a lien sale is governed by the state its facility is in, a notice period is a per-facility setting, a protection policy differs site to site. The link carries `?facility=` and leaves the persistent switcher alone, so a regional checking one site does not have to remember to switch back.
- **The link naming was fixed in the shared component, not per screen.** Read out of context — a screen reader's link list, one row at a time — "Cedar Park" does not say what is waiting there, which is the whole reason somebody is on the screen. Putting the `aria-label` in `FacilityRollup` gave the five roll-ups that shipped before B-235 the same fix; the visible text is contained in the accessible name, so 2.5.3 holds.

**What it left behind.**

- **Twenty-six admin screens still hard-refuse All facilities**, and most correctly: a wizard, a single tenant's profile, a form about one unit. The row named six and named them in priority order; the remaining refusals are not known to be wrong.
- **No portfolio total.** US-2's AC mentions "plus a portfolio total"; the counts here are per site and a sum of "waiting" figures across sites is not obviously an action anyone takes. Left out deliberately rather than added because the sentence allows it.
- **No a11y scan of the All-facilities state**, for these six or for the five that shipped before — `SCANNED_STATES` has never carried an all-facilities entry. The link-naming criterion this row carries is asserted in the new e2e instead, by accessible name.

**Test verification.** Typecheck (including `tsconfig.tests.json`) clean; lint clean (7 pre-existing warnings, unrelated); no schema change, so no migration and no drift. New `tests/all-facilities-rollups-db.test.ts` — **7 passed**, and passed again on an immediate re-run against the same database, with every assertion scoped to the suite's own two facilities rather than to a list length (B-185's accumulation). It pins the four properties the six roll-ups share through the helper: a zero is stated in words, an already-approved case is not counted, the href drills in with `?facility=`, and a facility the actor can see but lacks the permission at is omitted rather than shown a zero. Full unit suite **4,047 passed, 8 skipped of 4,055** (243 files passed, 1 skipped), exit 0. The two new e2e cases ran against a production build — `npm run test:e2e -- --grep "rolls up the approvals waiting"`, **6 passed** across the three projects, exit 0 — after the `$PWD`-anchored pre-sweep cleanup; the spec is read-only, so it needs none of B-120's three shared-fixture disciplines.

### B-236 — the hourly tick bounds its work, and what it cannot reach stays due

`ce675c1`

`/api/cron` looped `SCHEDULED_JOBS` × facilities × missed business dates, serially, against a remote Postgres, inside one request capped at 300 seconds. FR-13 states a 30-minute budget for 20 facilities and FR-14 promises catch-up runs; neither was bounded by anything in the code. The reviewer asked for a measurement before the fix was sized, and the measurement changed what the fix is about.

**What it measured.** 40 seeded facilities, all `America/Chicago`, at local hour 0 — the busiest hour, four per-facility jobs. **160 (job, facility, business date) tuples cost 870 Postgres round trips and 281ms against local Postgres.** Neon's own round trip, measured the same afternoon with 20 and 120 sequential `SELECT 1`s: **51.6ms, against 0.1ms local.** That puts the hour-0 tick at **~45 seconds of latency** and the 300-second cap somewhere past **~250 empty facilities** — earlier with real occupancy, because per-lease handler work is serial on top of the framework's 5.4 round trips per tuple.

**So the cap was not what was breaking first.** At the portfolio sizes this product is near, the tick finishes. What was breaking every night was the other half of the row: `facilitiesDueAt` matched only facilities whose local clock was *at* the target hour, so anything a tick did not reach — for any reason, including the tick never firing — was not due at the next tick and was never revisited that day.

**What it built.**

1. **`facilitiesDueSince`** (`packages/core/jobs/schedule.ts`), replacing `facilitiesDueAt`: `>=` the facility's local hour, not `==`. A tuple stays due until it runs.
2. **`dueRunQueue`** (`apps/web/lib/jobs/queue.ts`) — everything the scheduler still owes, resolved in **two queries for the whole portfolio** rather than one `lastSuccessfulRun` per (job, facility). One `groupBy` over `job_run`. 870 round trips → 718.
3. **`inParallel`**, and the route running the queue **six facilities at a time against a 240s budget**, checked before each tuple and never mid-run.
4. **A backlog line on `/admin/billing`** (`outstandingRuns` in `apps/web/lib/admin/billing-runs.ts`), and `deferred` in the cron response.

**What it decided.**

- **Parallel across facilities, serial within one.** That is what makes the fan-out safe without reasoning about job ordering: invoices still precede late fees precede dunning at a site, because a facility's runs stay one ordered list in registry order. Six at once, against the 10-connection cap the local convention sets — the jobs do not contend, so raising it trades connection headroom for wall clock and nothing else. **Measured after: 118ms local, ~6.5s extrapolated on Neon**, against ~45s.
- **The budget is checked before a tuple, never inside one.** A job that has started finishes and records its own `JobRun` row. Nothing is left half-done with no history of it, and what is left is left *due* rather than dropped.
- **The operator-visible backlog is DERIVED, not recorded.** A tick that runs out of budget could report "I deferred 40 runs" — but a tick that never fires reports nothing, and that is the failure that matters most. `/admin/billing` asks `dueRunQueue` the same question the scheduler asks, so it answers both and cannot drift from the scheduler's own view. This is what makes B-229's alarm mean something: **a run that FAILED is a row in the table with a status and an error; a run that has not HAPPENED writes nothing at all, and until now that was indistinguishable from a quiet night.**
- **A zero is stated in words** — "Nothing waiting: every run due so far today has happened" — per B-235's rule, and the count and date carry the meaning rather than a colour (1.4.1). `/admin/billing` is already in `ADMIN_SCAN_ROUTES`, so the existing axe sweep covers the new paragraph; it adds no route and no refused state, so `SCANNED_STATES` is unchanged.
- **The queue's `groupBy` is deliberately not bounded by date.** A window would change behaviour for a facility down longer than the window: `missedBusinessDates` treats "no history" as "run today only" and "history older than 30 days" as "run the 30 days after it", which are different answers. Correctness over a query that is already one round trip.

**Two real bugs, both found by measuring rather than by reasoning.**

- **Local hour 2 does not exist on the US spring-forward day.** An `=== 2` test matched *zero* ticks on 2026-03-08, so `billing.assess-late-fees`, `billing.scan-expiring-cards` and `billing.scan-protection-proofs` did not run that day at any US facility; the catch-up reached them the next night, a day late, once a year. `>=` gives them the same day. Pinned in `tests/schedule.test.ts`.
- **`missedBusinessDates` ran `businessDateFor` over an already-timezone-free business date.** `JobRun.businessDate` is a DATE at UTC midnight; shifting it through a US zone moves it back a day (2026-07-15T00:00Z is the evening of the 14th in Chicago), so the completed date came back due at every tick, at every facility west of UTC. Invisible while the only consumer was the runner — `runJob` skipped it as `already_ran` for one wasted round trip — and immediately fatal once the same list became the answer to "what is still waiting?", which would have reported every finished job as outstanding forever. Zones east of UTC never had it, which is the tell that it was a bug and not a margin.

**What it left behind.**

- **The pre-loop checks are still serial and outside the budget's control.** `dispatchEvents`, the reservation reminders, the abandonment sweep, the SMS retry, the waitlist sweep and the consumer-lag check all run before the queue, one after another. The budget is measured from the top of the request so they are paid out of it, but a slow one eats the jobs' time rather than its own. Not split out because none of them is per-facility, so none of them grows with the portfolio the way the queue does.
- **`raiseStaleMoneyJobTasks` still loops facilities × money jobs in memory.** Zero round trips on the normal path (it only writes when something is genuinely stale), so it was left alone.
- **The DST tests changed what they assert, deliberately.** Under the exact-hour rule the safety property was "fires exactly once a day". Dueness now says due at many ticks by design; the property that prevents double-billing was never the tick count — it is that every tick within one facility-local day resolves to the same business date, which `JobRun`'s unique constraint is keyed on. The suite now asserts that.
- **No alarm on a persistent backlog.** The line on `/admin/billing` is a screen somebody has to open. B-229's 48-hour stale-money-job task is still the only thing that reaches a person, and it only covers the four money jobs.

**Test verification.** Typecheck (including `tsconfig.tests.json`) clean; lint clean (7 pre-existing warnings, unrelated); no schema change, so no migration. New `tests/cron-queue-db.test.ts` — **11 passed**: the queue holds every job whose local hour has been reached in registry order, a run stays due at every later hour of the same day until it runs (the B-236 property), a succeeded run leaves the queue and a *failed* one does not, the catch-up dates come back oldest-first with everything but today flagged, the global jobs form their own group, and `inParallel` covers every item without exceeding its limit or hanging on an empty queue. `tests/schedule.test.ts` rewritten for the new semantics — **28 passed**. Full unit suite **4,060 passed, 8 skipped of 4,068** (244 files passed, 1 skipped), exit 0, and **run twice with identical totals** because the new suite creates and drops a facility. `db:status` exits non-zero against the Neon dev branch, which is behind by B-219's two `clock_timestamp()` migrations — pre-existing, unrelated, and CI's `verify` lane builds its own database so the drift check passes there. **`main`'s CI is red for an unrelated reason**: `e2e/impersonation.spec.ts` fails on both projects in the last run on `main`, and this item does not touch impersonation.

### B-237 — a facility can be created, and one that is not ready says so

`6e6144f`

`prisma.facility.create` existed nowhere in the application — only in seeds and test fixtures — so onboarding a site an operator had just bought was a database session. `/admin/settings` is read-and-update against a facility that already exists; B-008's row said "Facility settings CRUD" and what shipped was RU.

**The silent half was the one that mattered.** A hand-inserted facility has no `DelinquencyTimeline`, no `LateFeeRule`, no `FeeSchedule` and no tax component, so it invoices rent, charges no late fee however far past due a tenant runs, executes no dunning step, and `auctionReadiness` blocks every sale with `no_timeline` — without an error anywhere. The dashboard tiles read normally the whole time.

**What it built.**

1. **`createFacility`** (`apps/web/lib/admin/facility-settings.ts`), gated on `org:defaults` asked with a **null facilityId** — only an all-facilities assignment satisfies it. There is no facility to be scoped to yet, and adding one to the portfolio is a portfolio-wide act, the same shape `saveOrgDefault` uses.
2. **The org-default push happens inside the create**, not as a thing to remember afterwards: all three scopes are pushed through B-079's existing `pushOrgDefault`, so the new site owns ordinary effective-dated rows from day one. D-41's "pushed, never resolved" applied at birth.
3. **`/admin/settings/facilities/new`** — the form, with the confirm-and-echo step reused from `/admin/settings`.
4. **`facilityReadiness`** (`apps/web/lib/admin/facility-readiness.ts`) and **`FacilityReadinessBanner`**, on both `/admin` and `/admin/settings`.
5. **Latitude and longitude got controls**, on the create form and on `/admin/settings`' existing details form. They had none anywhere, and they are not cosmetic.

**What it decided.**

- **A failed or unconfigured push does not fail the create.** An operator who has bought a site needs it in the system today; the banner is what stops the gap going unnoticed. `createFacility` returns which scopes were actually pushed, and the rest become gaps.
- **A gap is an EMPTY table, never a value somebody chose.** A zero-dollar late fee is a real operator decision and is not reported; no ladder at all is. That is why readiness is a query rather than a comparison against the org default.
- **"Configured" means in force, not "a row exists".** All four money tables are effective-dated, so a rate filed for next quarter bills nothing today and is still a gap. Pinned by a test.
- **The banner renders nothing once the list is empty.** A permanent "all good" on a screen somebody opens daily is how they learn to skip the region it lives in.
- **Notice templates resolve org-level**, so an inherited template is not a gap — the readiness query uses the same precedence `effectiveNoticeTemplate` applies at generate time, rather than asking whether the facility has its own.
- **The create sets the facility-switcher cookie and redirects to `/admin/settings`, with no success message.** The readiness banner is the message, and it says what is still missing rather than "Facility created."
- **A blank coordinate derives from the postal code's centre** via the bundled dataset D-14 settled on, and the confirm step says so in those words. A centroid is a documented approximation of the place the postal code names, not a guess at the building — and a zip the dataset does not know leaves it null and becomes a gap.

**Accessibility (the row's own criteria).**

- **3.3.4 is the confirm-and-echo step already shipped at `/admin/settings`, reused rather than reinvented** — same `FormState` `confirm` branch, same pre-mounted status region, `confirmLabel` of "Yes, create this facility".
- **The readiness banner is text and links** (1.4.1): each gap names what is missing, what silently does not happen, and links to the control that fixes it.
- **Every control goes through `Field`**, so `aria-invalid`/`aria-describedby` cannot be omitted and the timezone `<select>` gets `max-w-full` (from `CONTROL_CLASS`) and `min-w-0` (from `Field`'s wrapper) — B-201's exact defect.
- **The route joined `ADMIN_SCAN_ROUTES` and both new states joined `SCANNED_STATES` in the same commit.** The refusal is `layout: 'excepted'` against the admin loop's own measurement; the confirm step is **`layout: 'reached'` and deliberately not excepted**, because six `<dl>` rows of long values is not the tax step's two short ones.

**One real defect found and fixed at the root.** `AdminForm`'s echo `<dl>` is `grid-cols-[auto_1fr]`, and a grid item defaults to `min-width: auto` — so a single long unbroken value widens the track instead of wrapping in it. The tax step's two short rows never showed it; a facility's web address does. Fixed on the shared component (`min-w-0 break-words` on the `<dd>`), so every confirm step in the product gets it, not just this one.

**What it left behind.**

- **No unit types, units or office hours are created.** A site still needs B-009's unit setup before it can rent anything; readiness names the money and compliance gaps the row named, not the whole onboarding.
- **No bulk import.** Taking on a portfolio is still one form per site.
- **`slug` is typed, not suggested.** Deriving it from the name would be one line, and it would silently produce a public URL nobody chose — the field is required and hinted instead.
- **Nothing deletes or archives a facility.** `FacilityStatus` has the states; no screen sets them, and `audit_log`'s RESTRICT foreign key means a hard delete was never available anyway.

New `tests/facility-create-db.test.ts` — **9 passed**: the permission refusal, an unusable slug, a slug another site already holds, an unreal timezone, the state normalized to upper case (compliance config is keyed on it), the map-position gap appearing only when a coordinate is genuinely absent, a gap disappearing once the setting exists, a rate effective next quarter still counting as a gap, and the audit row. `tests/org-defaults-db.test.ts` gained two — **13 passed**. ### B-239 — the move-in confirmation says what happens next, and Pay replaces Move out in the portal nav

Two surfaces, one complaint: the product told a renter where the money goes and never told them where they go. - **The sticky bar's non-obstruction is asserted by construction, not measured.** `expectNoHorizontalOverflow` — what the portal reflow loop actually runs — sees horizontal overflow and cannot see a fixed element covering content. `pb-24` is the fix and nothing automated will notice if a later change removes it.

### B-240 — the tenant profile answers the four questions at the top, and every section is one click away

`18cfa93`

Fourteen sections in one column, 2,154 lines, no in-page navigation and no summary. "Waive this fee for the person in front of me" was a scroll past referral history and gate events, worst on the phone viewport B-217 established is real counter work.

**What it built.**

2. **`<nav aria-label="On this profile">`** — six anchors in the order a counter uses them: Balance & leases · Take action · Payment plans · Fees · Contact · History. Two of the six render conditionally and the list is built from what actually painted, so no anchor can point at an id nothing rendered.
3. **`TenantLeaseSummary.daysPastDue`** (`apps/web/lib/admin/tenants.ts`), from `@storage/core/metrics` — D-25's one definition, the same one the late-fee run and the access gate use. **What it decided.**

- **D-95's section ORDER is untouched.** The reversal ("money and actions near the top") is D-114's to settle, and an anchor list is the answer that does not need it: one click to Actions with the read stack intact.
- **The bar is not a live region, and the `role="alert"` it replaced is gone.** B-245's rule: these are the facts the page opens with, not an announcement that something changed. ### B-255 — a web move-in's payment reaches the ledger

`7c9350f`

`openingLedger`'s docstring promised both halves of a move-in's opening ledger — *"what was owed today, and the payment that cleared it. Both entries, not just the payment."* — and wrote only the charge. The payment side is `postPaymentToLedger`, which runs **inside** the `payment_intent.succeeded` transaction, and `provisionMoveIn` runs **after** that transaction commits (`apps/web/lib/payments/reconcile.ts`), so at the moment it looked for a lease to post against there was none and it returned. Nothing wrote the entry afterwards. A redelivery could not repair it either: the handler short-circuits on `payment.status === 'succeeded'` before reaching the post. Every lease created by a card move-in therefore carried a phantom balance equal to the full move-in total, forever — read by the arrears reports, the delinquency ladder and every statement a tenant has been shown. B-230's counter path is what made it visible: a cash move-in provisions first and records second, so it nets to zero on the same screen where a card one does not.

**What it built.**

1. **`postMoveInPaymentToLedger`** (`apps/web/lib/payments/reconcile.ts`), called from the `payment_intent.succeeded` branch after `provisionMoveIn` returns, with the lease ids provisioning just created. Idempotent on the same `paymentId` + `type` guard the original post uses.
2. **The in-transaction post is now skipped for a checkout intent** (`if (!referenceSessionId(intent))`). It could only ever find nothing and return, and skipping it is what lets the after-provisioning post own the whole checkout case — card and bank debit alike.
3. **`npm run db:backfill:move-in-payments`** (`apps/web/scripts/backfill-move-in-payments.mts`) — the repair for leases already created. Dry-run by default; `--apply` writes. Its matching half is `planMoveInBackfill`, exported so it can be tested.

**What it decided.**

- **The payment is mirrored off the charge rows, not re-derived.** `openingLedger` splits the move-in total across a basket's leases (B-106), with the shared fees on the first. Reading those `Move-in charges` entries back and negating each one credits every lease exactly what it was charged, with no second copy of the apportionment arithmetic to drift. Any difference between what was charged and what was actually paid lands on the last lease, so the tenant's total is exact whichever way it drifted.
- **The ACH path is repaired by the same code, not by a second one.** At `payment_intent.processing` nothing is posted (the money has not arrived — that is deliberate and unchanged); the leases exist by then, so when `succeeded` arrives it takes the same after-provisioning route, and `provisionMoveIn` returns `alreadyProvisioned` with every lease id. A bank-debit basket now splits correctly too, which the in-transaction post never did.
- **The entry reads `Move-in payment`, not `Card payment`.** It mirrors `Move-in charges` in the tenant's own history, and it is not always a card.
- **No opening charge means nothing is posted.** A credit against a lease with no charge would read as the facility owing the tenant.
- **The backfill matches on an exact amount and refuses to guess.** It groups un-credited move-in charges by tenant and facility, sums them, and posts only when **exactly one** succeeded payment of exactly that amount with nothing posted from it exists. Two candidates of the same amount is a person's problem, not a script's — crediting the wrong one is money moved on a guess.
- **The backfill is deliberately NOT wrapped in `assertDevDatabase`.** The leases that need it are in production. The guard is that it prints and changes nothing until `--apply`.

**What it left behind.**

- **A move-in whose tenant has since paid an invoice is out of the backfill's scope**, because the lease's ledger already holds a `payment` entry and the script only considers leases with none. Those are the accounts where the phantom balance has already been partly paid against or written off, and an amount match is the wrong instrument for them — they need a person.
- **Nothing was backfilled in this session.** The dry run found **0 groups** in both the Neon dev branch and the local test database, so the matching logic is proven by its test rather than against real rows. It has never been run against production, and doing so is the operator's call.
- **The reconciliation report still shows a checkout payment as unposted for the moment between the transaction committing and provisioning finishing.** That window existed before and is unchanged; it is bounded by the same webhook that is already retrying.

**Test verification.** Typecheck (including `tsconfig.tests.json`) clean; lint clean (7 pre-existing warnings, unrelated); `prisma migrate diff --exit-code` reports no drift; no schema change. Full unit suite **4,082 passed, 8 skipped of 4,090** (246 files passed, 1 skipped — the live-integration file), exit 0. Two new files: `tests/provision-db.test.ts` gained a two-unit case driven through `applyStripeEvent` (not `provisionMoveIn`, because the defect **is** the ordering between them) asserting each lease nets to zero on its own and a redelivery does not credit twice, and `tests/backfill-move-in-payments.test.ts` covers the match, the ambiguous two-candidate refusal, and the no-match case. **The regression test was verified against the broken build** — with the new call disabled it fails `expected [] to have a length of 2`, so it is not vacuous. `apps/web/app/(public)/accessibility/page.tsx` was re-read: no route, control or copy changed, all three "Where we fall short" entries are still true, `LAST_REVIEWED` did not move (D-115 / B-254), and a dated entry was appended to its comment log. ### B-090e — Business accounts: the payer above the lease (2026-09-04)

**Commit:** `1449a39`

B-090 part 5. The commercial customer with eleven units got eleven leases, eleven invoices, eleven Pay buttons in the portal and eleven separate allocations at the counter. B-090's own audit called this "the only one needing a schema change — a payer ABOVE lease". It needed one table and one nullable column.

**What it built.**

1. **`BillingAccount`** (`facilityId`, `name`, `payerTenantId`) and a nullable **`Lease.billingAccountId`** — migration `20260905011748_billing_account`. The payer is a `Tenant` rather than a new identity, because the payer has to be someone the POS can take money from, the portal can sign in, and `Payment.tenantId` can point at.
2. **`apps/web/lib/billing/accounts.ts`** — create, attach a unit, detach a unit, list with balances, one account's detail. Every mutation audited (`billing_account.created`, `.lease_attached`, `.lease_detached`).
3. **`payableLeaseFilter`** — the single `where` fragment that is the whole of what an account does to the money path, read by `claimsFor` and `coveredByPlan` in `apps/web/lib/billing/allocation.ts`.
4. **`billing_accounts:manage`**, a new permission granted to manager and above.
5. **`/admin/billing/accounts` and `/admin/billing/accounts/[id]`**, plus a `Business Accounts` nav item in the `money` group.
6. **A demo business account** — `Acme Contracting`, payer `business@demo.example.com` (Casey Contractor, no lease of their own), paying for `DEMO_POS_TENANT_EMAIL`'s unit.

**What it decided.**

- **`Invoice` is unchanged, and that is the design, not a shortcut (D-118).** PRD 01 §12's "multiple units under one invoice" reads as one invoice row over eleven units, and building that would be wrong: a Texas lien attaches to the goods in ONE unit, a §59 notice names ONE unit, late-fee assessment reads one lease's `rent` invoices, the ledger is per-lease and invoice numbers are gapless per facility. An account consolidates the **payment** and the **total**, never the document. The PRD's own wording is corrected in place rather than left to disagree with the build.
- **`Lease.tenantId` is untouched.** The person whose goods are in the unit and the person who pays are different facts, and only the first is who a notice is served on. `billingAccountId` is additive and nullable, so every existing lease keeps exactly the behaviour it had.
- **One `where` fragment, not a second payment path.** `claimsFor` and `coveredByPlan` are the only readers, so the counter, the portal, a pay link and autopay's plan-installment narrowing all inherit consolidation without knowing accounts exist. It is a **UNION, never a swap**: a tenant's own leases stay claimable whatever else they pay for, so an employee can still walk in and pay for their own unit while their employer usually does.
- **`coveredByPlan` was widened with it, deliberately.** A plan on an account's lease must defer the payer's money exactly as it defers the tenant's, or the consolidated payment walks over the arrears the plan froze and B-203's defect returns through the business-account door.
- **An account is scoped to ONE facility, and the ceiling is a composite foreign key rather than a service check.** `Payment.facilityId` is not nullable, and deposit slips, drawer sessions, accounting periods and the allocation order are all per-facility — a lease attached across sites would settle out of the wrong facility's books. `Lease(billingAccountId, facilityId) → BillingAccount(id, facilityId)` makes that a database fact; Postgres does not enforce a composite FK with a NULL in it, which is exactly right for an unattached lease. A customer at three sites gets three accounts; a payer group above accounts is the upgrade path, marked `ponytail:` on the model.
- **Attaching refuses a unit already on another account rather than moving it.** Silently re-pointing who pays for a unit is the one change on that screen a person would not notice having made.
- **The payer must already be a tenant.** Creating one from this form would mint an identity that can sign in and see other people's balances; that is a move-in's job.
- **`restoreAccessIfSettled` was widened in the same commit, and it was not optional.** It takes the PAYER, which every payment path passes. Without the widening, a business account clearing the foreman's balance at the counter left the foreman's gate code off until the 4am pass — the exact phone call US-45's inline restore exists to prevent. Each tenant is still judged on their own grant and their own balance; only the set considered changed.

**A behaviour worth naming, which is pre-existing rather than new.** A payer pressing "Pay" on one lease has the money allocated across the whole claimable pool in the facility's order (tax → fee → protection → rent, oldest first), not necessarily onto the lease whose button was pressed. That is how the product has always behaved for a tenant holding two leases at one facility; the account widens the pool it applies to. The portal wording that would say so belongs to B-256, which is the row that makes the payer's view consolidated at all.

**What it left behind.**

- **The portal is untouched — B-256.** A payer still sees one card and one Pay button per lease, because the portal renders per lease and this item changed no portal file. One account card with one total, the consolidated statement, and the authorized users PRD 01 §12 asks for are all that row's.
- **No `BillingAccountMember` table.** Deliberate: a table nothing reads is not a feature. B-256 builds it when `resolveAudience` needs it, and it collides there with **D-111**/B-238 — two members of one household cannot exist while `Tenant.email` is unique.
- **Autopay still charges each lease's own tenant, not the payer.** `chargeAutopay` reads `lease.tenant.stripeDefaultPaymentMethodId`, so attaching a lease does not move the mandate. Left as-is on purpose: moving it silently would charge a company card on an authority nobody gave. Named as open in D-118 and carried by B-256.
- **A payer holding no lease has no counter entry point.** POS is lease-anchored, so staff take the consolidated payment against one of the payer's own leases. The demo payer holds none, which is the honest shape of the gap.
- **No PO number, no per-account allocation order, no account close.** None had a caller.

**Test verification.** Typecheck (including `tsconfig.tests.json`) clean; lint clean (7 pre-existing warnings, unchanged in count and all pre-existing); `prisma migrate diff --from-schema-datasource --to-schema-datamodel --exit-code` reports **no difference** against the local database. Full unit suite **4,107 passed, 8 skipped of 4,115** (248 files passed, 1 skipped — the live-integration file), exit 0, and the totals reconcile. New `tests/billing-accounts-db.test.ts` (9 cases), run twice for repeatability, pins: nothing of another tenant's is claimable until the unit is attached; the payer's one $350 payment settles their own $150 invoice **and** the foreman's $200 one, with a neighbour at the same facility untouched; the foreman's suspended grant comes back on a call made with the PAYER's id; the account adds its units up as one balance; a unit already on another account is refused **by name**; a payer who is not a tenant here is refused; a staffer without `billing_accounts:manage` is refused; **a cross-facility attachment is rejected by the database**, asserted with raw SQL because the service never builds that row and a check inside the service would only prove the service; and detaching gives the unit back to its own tenant. New `e2e/admin-billing-accounts.spec.ts` (10 checks, desktop and mobile): axe clean on `/admin/billing/accounts` and on `/admin/billing/accounts/[id]` reached by a real click, plus the screen naming the payer, the lease's own tenant and one total. **The scan contract caught both new routes before the suite did** — `/admin/billing/accounts` joined `ADMIN_SCAN_ROUTES` and `[id]` joined `SCANNED_BY_OWN_SPEC` rather than becoming an exception, because a bare `goto` cannot produce an account id and the substance of the page only exists once an account has units. `npm run db:migrate:e2e` was run **twice** to prove the demo seed is still re-runnable: `Lease.billingAccount` is `Restrict` in both directions, so the reset clears the pointer and deletes the accounts before the leases — the same failure shape B-132's auction case had. **`apps/web/app/(public)/accessibility/page.tsx` was re-read and is unchanged**, correctly: this item ships admin-only surfaces, adds no `SCAN_EXCEPTIONS` row, and so renders byte-identically; all three "Where we fall short" entries still true, `LAST_REVIEWED` did not move (D-115 / B-254). ### B-257 — One payment settled several leases; the ledger credited one (2026-09-04)

**Commit:** `a8588e7`

Found while starting B-256, and **verified by reproduction before any code was changed**: a tenant with two $100 units pays $200 at the counter. Both invoices come out `paid`. The ledger reads **−$10,000 on unit A and +$10,000 on unit B**. The portal then asks that tenant for $100 they handed over at the desk, and each unit's statement is wrong in opposite directions.

**Pre-existing, and not caused by B-090e.** `claimsFor` has spread a payment across every lease a tenant holds at a facility since **B-048**; both money-in paths wrote a single `ledgerEntry` for the whole amount against one named lease. B-090e widened the pool to a business account and B-256 makes one-payment-many-leases the *normal* path, which is why this had to land first — the same constraint B-137 carried against B-138.

**What it built.**

1. **`postPaymentLedger`** (`apps/web/lib/billing/allocation.ts`) — writes the ledger side of a payment split by what the allocation actually settled, one entry per lease. Mirrored off the allocation rather than re-deriving the arithmetic, which is the device `postMoveInPaymentToLedger` (B-255) already uses for a multi-unit basket.
2. **`apps/web/lib/admin/pos.ts`** — the inline single-entry create is gone; the ledger is posted after `applyPayment`, anchored to the lease the counter had open.
3. **`apps/web/lib/payments/reconcile.ts`** — `postPaymentToLedger` is now `anchorLeaseFor`, answering only the narrower question it was always really answering. `settlePayment` returns its `AppliedPayment`, and the ledger is posted **after** it.
4. **`apps/web/lib/billing/reversals.ts`** — the return path reverses **every** posted entry and resumes the dunning ladder per lease; the reinstate path puts every one back.

**What it decided.**

- **The allocation is the authority on where money went, and the ledger mirrors it.** Anything else is a second opinion that can disagree with the first, which is exactly the state this defect was.
- **The unallocated remainder anchors to the lease the payer named.** Money over and above what any invoice claimed still has to land on a lease ledger or a prepayment vanishes from the balance a tenant is shown. Credit on account stays derived at tenant × facility (B-225, untouched); this is only its per-lease view, and it preserves the behaviour a single-lease prepayment has always had.
- **The webhook posts after settlement, not before.** The old order was safe only because the entry did not depend on the allocation. It does now. Nothing else in that transaction reads the ledger, so the move costs nothing.
- **The NSF fee stays ONE per returned payment.** A bank reversing one debit is one NSF event; one fee per unit would charge a two-unit tenant twice for it. `assessNsfFee` now carries an `orderBy` so a re-run lands it on the same unit rather than whichever row came back first.
- **`reversalEntryId` stays in the audit context and the result type**, with `reversalEntryIds`/`entryIds` added beside it. Removing the singular would have broken a shipped result shape to say something the plural already says.

**What it left behind.**

- **Historical payments are not repaired.** Every ledger written before this commit still carries the whole amount on one lease. A backfill is possible from the allocations, and is deliberately not done here: it would rewrite an append-only ledger, which is FR-8's own prohibition. Any tenant actually affected is corrected by a staff adjustment, which is the mechanism that exists for it.
- **`assessNsfFee` picks the anchor by id order, not by which lease the payer named.** Deterministic, which was the actual defect; naming the anchor would mean threading it through a function that does not otherwise need it.

**Test verification.** Typecheck (including `tsconfig.tests.json`) clean; lint clean (7 pre-existing warnings, unchanged in count and all pre-existing). Full unit suite **4,112 passed, 8 skipped of 4,120** (249 files passed, 1 skipped — the live-integration file), exit 0, totals reconcile. New `tests/payment-ledger-split-db.test.ts` (5 cases), run twice: both of one tenant's units are credited rather than just the one the counter had open; a prepayment no invoice claimed stays visible on the lease the payer named; a business account's payment credits **another tenant's** unit; a returned payment reverses every lease and a reinstatement puts them all back; and one returned-payment fee is raised however many units the money reached. **Four of the five fail with the fix stashed and all five pass with it** — the fifth is a guard against a future per-lease fee and the test says so, since this facility has no configured NSF amount, which is the shipped state. Every assertion is a per-lease **balance** rather than a count of rows, deliberately: the defect was invisible at the invoice level, and a test counting ledger entries would have passed before the fix as readily as after. Money-adjacent suites re-run green: `allocation`, `credit-db`, `pos-db`, `billing-accounts-db`, `stripe-webhook-db`, `return-fee-choice`, `refunds-db`, `payment-plans-db`, `delinquency-engine-db`. `apps/web/app/(public)/accessibility/page.tsx` re-read and unchanged: nothing customer-facing moved — no route, no copy, no scan exception — and all three "Where we fall short" entries are still true, `LAST_REVIEWED` unmoved (D-115 / B-254). **The bug is the item**, so there is none to note separately.

### B-256 — The business account's portal half: one card, one Pay button (2026-09-04)

**Commit:** `d357e28`

B-090 part 5's portal side, split out because that part is L. B-090e built the payer, the consolidated allocation and the admin screen and **changed no portal file** — so a payer still saw one card and one Pay button per lease, and a payer holding no lease of their own saw *"We don't see an active unit on this account yet."* The row named three parts; two shipped, the third turned out to be a decision rather than a gap, and a fourth thing was a defect found on the way.

**What it built.**

1. **`payableLeaseWhere(tenantId)`** (`apps/web/lib/billing/accounts.ts`) — B-090e's `payableLeaseFilter` with the facility taken off it, and the ONE definition of what an account lets a tenant reach. Read by the pay screen, the nav's Pay link, the statements list and the statement authorization check, so what a payer may SEE and what they may PAY cannot drift apart.
2. **`portalAccountsForPayer`** — the payer's read model: the account's occupying units with their own tenants named, each unit's ledger balance, and the total. Scoped by `payerTenantId` with no permission check, because being the payer is the same fact that makes their money settle these leases.
3. **One account card at `/portal`** — the units as a real table, one total, one Pay button to `/portal/pay?account=`, and two sentences the row asked for: that a payment goes to the oldest amounts owed across every unit rather than to one in particular, and that autopay does not move (D-119).
4. **`payableAccount` + `/portal/pay?account=`** — the whole account as one thing to pay, through the same `validatePaymentAmount` → `startPortalPayment` → Payment Element path a single lease uses. The bill lists units instead of ledger lines; `PayAmountForm` carries the subject so "pay a different amount" re-submits as the account.
5. **The consolidated statement** — `/portal/statements` groups the payer's account units under one account heading with the month's closing balances summed, and `/portal/statements/account/[accountId]/[period]` is that month over every unit, each row a link into the unit's own document.
6. **The demo account is re-pointed and its payer gains a password** — see *what it decided* below; the account had no money on it to show, scan, or measure.

**What it decided.**

- **D-119: attaching a lease does not move the autopay mandate.** It stays per unit, charging the card that unit's own tenant has on file, and the card says so in words. A mandate is consent by a named person to a named card; `attachLease` is a staff action on a screen the payer never sees, so the alternative would have been a way to move somebody's autopay onto a third party's card with no consent step anywhere. A payer-level autopay is unbuilt rather than refused — its own row, with its own consent step.
- **A summary over the per-unit statements, never instead of them.** `Invoice` stays per lease (D-118), so the consolidated month is an addition; every row links to the unit's own document, which is what a dispute or a lien file needs. Its columns are **owed at start, change, owed at end** rather than the four an accountant would ask for: a statement's six totals do not compose into start-plus-charges-minus-payments, so a month with a write-off or a refund would have printed four figures that do not reach the fifth. `reconciles` guarantees `opening + movement = closing` and nothing else, so that is what is shown.
- **The lease card keeps its Pay button when somebody else is billed, and loses it when the viewer is the payer.** B-090e's rule is that a tenant handing over cash for their own unit is never refused because their employer usually pays — so the foreman's card is unchanged, and gains a line naming who is billed so the bill is not paid twice. A lease the VIEWER holds *and* pays for through their own account is the double-count case: the account card owns that money, and two buttons for one debt is how a screen gets it paid twice.
- **`facilityBalanceCents` stays the viewer's own leases, deliberately.** It feeds `restoreShortfallCents`, and the gate rule judges a tenant on what THEY owe — widening it to what they pay for would state a shortfall the gate does not agree with. The named lease's own balance is read separately, because for a payer it is not in that sum at all.
- **No prepayment on an account payment.** `prepayCeilingFor` is overridden to zero, so overpayment is refused there as it was everywhere before B-225. Credit on account is derived at tenant × facility (B-225) and a payer typically holds no lease at that facility, so money past the balance would bank credit where none of the three jobs that spend it would reach it. Paying an account ahead needs an owner decision about whose credit it is.
- **The demo account came off `DEMO_POS_TENANT_EMAIL`'s unit, and that reverses a B-090e choice deliberately.** B-090e attached the POS lease and argued it was safe because allocation widens for the PAYER and the POS tenant is not the payer — true, and it stayed true. What changed is that an account now has a portal card with a total and a Pay button on it: the POS specs take ~$25 against that lease every sweep and never reverse it, so the account's total walks downwards. Measured: **$86.00 owed after one sweep, $39.00 in credit after two**, at which point the card correctly stops offering a Pay button and every spec below it fails. The account now holds the two anonymous active units instead — it still shows a payer settling more than one unit that is somebody else's, which is all B-090e wanted from it, and its total no longer moves. **That is B-120's repeatability rule catching a fixture choice**, and it was caught by running the sweep twice rather than by reasoning about it.
- **`tenantOwnsLease` became `tenantMayViewLease`.** Ownership is no longer the whole of who may read a statement: a payer settles these invoices out of their own money and gets the bookkeeping record. Deliberately not symmetric — being billed for something does not make the rest of the account's history yours.

**A defect found and fixed here, pre-existing but unreachable.** `anchorLeaseFor` (`apps/web/lib/payments/reconcile.ts`) scoped an explicitly named lease to `tenantId: payment.tenantId`, so a business account's payer naming one of their account's units got **no anchor at all** — and the part of a payment no invoice claimed then reached no lease ledger. The payment existed, the per-unit balances did not move for it, and the account total the portal shows would have been stale by exactly that amount. It has been latent since B-090e and was unreachable until this row gave the payer a Pay button. Fixed with `payableLeaseFilter`, the same fragment `claimsFor` reads — the check that a named lease is one this payer may post against has to be the same question the money asks. A test drives it through `applyStripeEvent` and fails against the old `where`.

A `REACH` entry is added here.

**What it left behind.**

- **Authorized users — B-258.** An account still has exactly one person who can see it, so giving the office manager sight of it means making them the payer. Split out rather than deferred silently: it is a table, a migration, a control and an authorization boundary, and it collides with **D-111**'s unique `Tenant.email`.
