**Status:** Draft v1.0 — 2026-07-30
**Author:** Product Management

> **Legal disclaimer:** This document describes software behavior for a *configurable* delinquency and lien workflow. Self-storage lien law varies significantly by US state (notice types, delivery methods, waiting periods, advertising requirements, sale conduct). Nothing in this PRD is legal advice; operators must have their lien timelines and notice templates reviewed by an attorney licensed in each state where they operate. The system ships with sensible defaults and per-state configuration, not legal guarantees.

## 1. Overview & Goals

The Admin/Operator Dashboard is the internal web application used by staff of a multi-facility self-storage business to run daily operations: manage unit inventory, price units, move tenants in and out, bill them, chase delinquency through a legally-sensitive lien process, and report on the health of the business.

It is the operational core of the platform. The customer website (PRD 03) creates reservations, online move-ins, and payments that land here; hardware integrations (PRD 04) consume access rules (gate codes, overlock status) that are decided here; marketing (PRD 05) consumes availability and street rates published from here.

Industry incumbents (Storable SiteLink/storEDGE, Tenant Inc's Hummingbird, Easy Storage Solutions, Unit Trac) converge on the same operator feature set: multi-facility "corporate" tools, tenant lifecycle management (move-in/out, transfers), electronic lease signing, payment processing, collections/delinquency automation, rate management, and reporting dashboards ([Storable SiteLink](https://www.storable.com/products/sitelink/), [Storable products](https://www.storable.com/products/), [Taloflow comparison of Tenant Inc and Unit Trac](https://www.taloflow.ai/guides/comparisons/tenant-vs-unittrac-self-storage)). This PRD targets parity on the core of that set at a scale appropriate for a 1–20 facility operator, with a cleaner, modern UX.

### Goals

1. **Single pane of glass for operations.** A facility manager can run a site for a full day — move-ins, payments, walkthrough, delinquency actions — without leaving the dashboard.
2. **Multi-facility from day one.** Owner and regional roles see roll-ups across facilities; every operational entity (unit, tenant, rate, fee, task) is scoped to a facility.
3. **Automated, auditable billing.** Recurring invoicing, autopay, retries, late fees, and refunds run on schedules with a complete audit trail — cash handling is the exception, not the rule.
4. **Compliant-by-configuration delinquency.** The delinquency → overlock → pre-lien → lien → auction pipeline is driven by per-state, per-facility configurable timelines and templated notices, with immutable evidence of what was sent, when, and how.
5. **Decision-grade reporting.** Occupancy (unit and economic), move activity, delinquency aging, revenue, and deposit reconciliation are accurate enough to run the business and export cleanly to CSV.

### Non-Goals (module-level)

- **Public-facing surfaces.** Customer website, tenant self-service portal, online reservation/move-in checkout UX — PRD 03. This module exposes the APIs and settings they consume.
- **Physical hardware control.** Gate controller protocols, keypad firmware, camera feeds, smart-lock pairing — PRD 04. This module owns the *business state* (access allowed/denied, overlock flag) that hardware syncs against.
- **Marketing execution.** SEO, listings syndication, paid campaigns, email drip marketing — PRD 05.
- **Accounting system of record.** We produce journals/exports and reconciliation reports; we do not replace QuickBooks/Xero (integration is a data export in MVP, API sync later).
- **Payroll, HR, scheduling of staff shifts.**
- **Non-US operations.** US-only tax, lien, and notice assumptions in v1.
- **Native mobile apps.** Responsive web only in v1 (the daily walkthrough is designed mobile-web-first).
- **Automated legal compliance.** The system enforces *configured* timelines; it does not itself validate configurations against state statutes.

## 2. Personas

### 2.1 Priya — Facility Manager (primary daily user)
Runs one facility, on site 6 days/week. Splits time between the counter, the phone, and walking the property. Moderate computer skills; lives on a desktop at the counter and a phone on the lot.
- **Jobs:** counter move-ins/move-outs, taking payments (card/cash/check), daily walkthrough and lock checks, applying/removing overlocks, answering "what do I owe?" questions, opening maintenance tickets, executing delinquency steps the system queues for her.
- **Pain to avoid:** re-keying data, ambiguity about which delinquency step is due today, cash-drawer mismatches at close.

### 2.2 Marcus — Owner/Operator (economic buyer)
Owns 3 facilities, visits each weekly, manages from a laptop and phone. Cares about revenue, occupancy, delinquency exposure, and not getting sued over a bad lien sale.
- **Jobs:** portfolio dashboard review, approving rate increases and refunds over threshold, reviewing auction candidates before sale, monthly reconciliation and export to his accountant, configuring fees/policies.
- **Pain to avoid:** discovering a mis-run lien sale after the fact; occupancy numbers that don't match reality; margin leaking through unmanaged street rates.

### 2.3 Dana — Regional Manager (scale persona)
Oversees 8 facilities for a larger operator; each has its own manager. Desk-based, heavy report user.
- **Jobs:** cross-facility comparisons, enforcing pricing strategy, auditing manager actions (waived fees, manual discounts, deleted charges), pushing standard settings/templates to facilities, covering for absent managers remotely.
- **Pain to avoid:** having to log in "as" each facility; not being able to tell which manager waived which fee.

Secondary personas: **relief/part-time counter staff** (needs a constrained, mistake-resistant subset), **bookkeeper/accountant** (read-only financial reports and exports).

## 3. Roles & Permissions

Role-based access control (RBAC) with facility scoping. A user has exactly one role per facility assignment; a user may be assigned to one, several, or all facilities.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| View tenants/units (assigned facilities) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Take payments, POS move-in | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Move-out, transfers | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Waive fees / manual credits (≤ limit) | — | ✓ (limit) | ✓ (higher limit) | ✓ (unlimited) | — |
| Refunds | — | request | approve ≤ limit | approve | — |
| Edit unit inventory / statuses | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Street rate changes | — | propose | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Existing-tenant rate increases | — | — | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Delinquency step execution (queued) | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Delinquency timeline configuration | — | — | — | ✓ | — |
| Approve auction eligibility | — | — | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Facility settings (hours, fees, taxes) | — | — | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| User management, roles | — | — | — | ✓ | — |
| Reports (own facility) | ✓ (ops only) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (financial) |
| Cross-facility roll-ups | — | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Audit log | — | — | view | view | — |

**Requirements:**
- **RBAC-1:** Permissions are enforced server-side on every API endpoint; the UI hides what the role can't do.
- **RBAC-2:** Monetary authority limits (max fee waiver, max refund without approval) are configurable per role at the org level. **Corrected 2026-08-31 (B-211) to what is built, and to what D-98 settled:** exceeding the limit is **refused on the spot**, and the refusal **names** the lowest role above whose limit covers the amount. It does not create an approval request: nothing is routed, queued or notified, and the person named has to perform the action themselves. A routed approval request has never been built and **no backlog row owns it** — the comment on `checkMonetaryAuthority` promised it would arrive with refunds in B-048, B-048 shipped without it, and `/admin/settings/roles` went on telling owners that over-limit work "does not fail: it escalates to the next role up" for four months after. An owner who believes that sets manager limits low, and every over-limit waiver dead-ends at the counter with a tenant standing there.
- **RBAC-3:** All auth via the shared identity service (master PRD); staff accounts require MFA for Owner/Admin and Regional roles.
- **RBAC-4:** Session facility context (see 5.1) never widens data access beyond assigned facilities.

## 4. User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

Grouped by feature area. AC = acceptance criteria. Stories tagged **[MVP]** or **[P2]** (see Phasing, §9).

> **Numbering note.** US-41 through US-44 were added on 2026-07-31 from the operator review and are filed in the section they belong to, so numbering is not sequential *within* a section. Numbers are never reused or renumbered — they are referenced from `06-backlog.md` and commit messages.

### 4.1 Multi-Facility

**US-1 [MVP]** As a regional manager, I can switch my working facility from a persistent facility switcher so that all screens scope to that facility.
- AC: Switcher visible in the global header on every screen; shows facility name + code; search/filter for >5 facilities; switching preserves the current screen where meaningful (e.g., unit list reloads scoped) and lands on the facility dashboard otherwise; last selection persists per user across sessions.
- AC: An "All facilities" context is available only on roll-up screens (dashboard, reports) for roles with cross-facility access.

**US-2 [MVP]** As an owner, I see a portfolio dashboard rolling up occupancy, revenue MTD, delinquency exposure, and today's move-ins/outs per facility, so I can spot problems in under a minute.
- AC: One row/card per facility with unit occupancy %, economic occupancy %, revenue MTD vs prior month, delinquent tenant count and delinquent AR $, today's scheduled move-ins/outs; each metric links to the corresponding facility-scoped report; loads in <2s for 20 facilities.
- AC: **No tile shows a lifetime cumulative count.** Every count carries a time window or a resolution concept in its own label ("Failed payments (this month)"), because a number that only ever goes up under a heading like "needs attention" trains the reader to ignore the row within a week. A tile with nothing to show says so in words; a permanent em dash is noise that teaches the eye to skip the whole row.
- AC: Every tile is a link to the filtered list or report behind it, as those screens land. A tile whose destination does not exist yet is not a tile.
- AC: The facility dashboard answers the counter's actual question — **"what can I rent right now?"** — with an *Available now* tile showing the real count of `available` units, hinted with the reserved count. `occupied / total` does not answer it, because reserved, maintenance and unrentable all sit in the gap.
- **AC (added 2026-08-12 from the UX review; built in B-113):** delinquency is reported in **dollars**, from the metrics module (D-25), with its window in the label — "Past due — $4,318 across 6 leases". The owner's question is "how much am I owed", and a count of leases is not an answer to it. A tile counting `Lease.status = 'delinquent'` is worse than no tile: nothing sets that status until the delinquency engine (US-45) ships, so it reads 0 beside real past-due AR. **The tile figure and the report figure behind it are asserted equal in a test** — D-25 exists because the first time the dashboard says 91% and the rent roll says 88%, the owner stops trusting both.
- **AC (same review):** in the **"All facilities"** context, every roll-up-capable screen renders a roll-up, not a refusal. The owner is the persona holding the all-facilities assignment (D-12) and the switcher offers the mode to exactly that person; answering it with five differently-worded versions of "pick a single facility above" makes the owner's natural default a dead end on six screens. The shape is one card or row per facility with the figures that screen exists to show, each linking through, plus a portfolio total — the pattern `/admin/tasks` already implements. Where a screen genuinely cannot roll up, the action still works: **New inquiry opens its form with a facility selector as the first field rather than refusing**, because that button exists so a ringing phone costs one click (US-43) and refusing costs three with someone on the line.

**US-3 [MVP]** As an owner, I configure per-facility settings — office hours, gate hours, tax rates (state/county/city components), fee schedule (admin fee, late fees, NSF fee, lien fees), timezone, address — so each site operates under local rules.
- AC: Settings are versioned (effective-dated for tax rates and fees); gate hours are exposed via API for PRD 04; changing a tax rate applies to invoices generated after the effective date, never retroactively; every change is audit-logged.

**US-4 [P2]** As an owner, I can define org-level defaults (fee schedule, notice templates, delinquency timeline) and push them to selected facilities, with per-facility overrides flagged visibly.

- **Built in B-079** at `/admin/settings/org`. A default is **pushed, never resolved at runtime** (D-41): the push writes ordinary effective-dated rows into the receiving facility's own tables, so invoicing, the late-fee job and the delinquency engine keep reading exactly one place. "Overridden" is therefore a **computed comparison** against the values in force today, not a stored flag — and it names what diverges ("overridden: admin fee, late step 2") rather than only that something does. **Notice templates get no push**, deliberately: `MessageTemplate` already resolves org-level → facility override at render time, so the org default is already live everywhere that has not diverged; what shipped for templates is the visibility half. Editing a default requires `org:defaults` org-wide (an all-facilities assignment); pushing to a given site is checked against `facility:settings` **at that site**, per facility rather than once per batch.

### 4.2 Unit Inventory

**US-5 [MVP]** As a facility manager, I see an interactive facility map (or auto-generated grid fallback) of all units, color-coded by status, so I can answer "what's available" and find a unit visually.
- AC: Statuses and colors: `available` (green), `occupied` (blue), `reserved` (yellow), `overlocked` (red), `maintenance` (gray), `unrentable` (hatched). Clicking a unit opens a detail panel (size, type, attributes, rate, tenant if occupied, balance, quick actions). Map supports zoom/pan; a list view with the same filters is always available. Facilities without an uploaded map layout get the grid view grouped by building/floor.
- AC: Map layout is editable by admin: place/resize unit rectangles on a floor plan image (P2: layout editor; MVP: grid view + optional pre-built JSON layout import).

**US-6 [MVP]** As a facility manager, I manage unit types (e.g., 10x10 Climate, 5x5 Drive-Up, Parking) with attributes (dimensions, floor, climate control, drive-up, power, door type) and assign units to types.
- AC: Unit types are per-facility but clonable across facilities; a unit belongs to exactly one type; type changes on an occupied unit warn and do not change the tenant's current rate.

**US-7 [MVP]** As a facility manager, I can bulk-edit units (select by filter → change status, type, or attributes) so setup and corrections don't require unit-by-unit clicks.
- AC: Bulk operations preview affected units and require confirmation; blocked transitions (e.g., occupied → available) are skipped and reported; bulk changes appear as one grouped audit entry with per-unit detail.

**US-8 [MVP]** Unit status is derived, not free-form: `occupied`/`reserved` come from lease state; `overlocked` from delinquency state; only `maintenance`/`unrentable`/`available` are manually settable, and only when no active lease exists.
- AC: The system rejects manual status edits that contradict lease/delinquency state, with a clear error naming the blocking record.

### 4.3 Pricing & Revenue Management

**US-9 [MVP]** As a regional manager, I set street rates per unit type per facility, with effective dates, so new rentals price correctly and the website (PRD 03) always shows the current rate.
- AC: Street rate changes are effective-dated and never alter existing leases; rate history per unit type is viewable; current street rates are exposed via API to PRDs 03/05.

**US-10 [MVP]** As an owner, I create promotions (e.g., "first month 50% off", "$1 first month", fixed $ off for N months) with constraints (facilities, unit types, date range, online-only vs in-store, min stay implied by recapture rules).
- AC: A promotion applied at move-in generates the correct discounted charges on the right invoices automatically; promotions show on the lease and on invoices as discount line items; expired promotions can't be applied; overlapping promotions require explicit stacking rules (default: no stacking, best single discount).
- **AC (added 2026-08-21 from the operator review; verified by repo-wide grep — `Promotion.minStayMonths` has no reference outside the schema):** every column that configures promotional behaviour has an operator control in the same item that adds the column. `minStayMonths` has none, so **"first month free with a six-month minimum" cannot be expressed at all** — the operator either gives the month away unconditionally or does not run the promotion. The minimum stay is also stated on the lease and on the checkout summary, because it is a term the renter is being held to.
- **AC (recapture):** this story's own parenthetical says "min stay implied by **recapture rules**", and there are none. A lease that ends before the promotion's minimum stay charges back the discount already given, as a line item on the final statement, at the facility's configured recapture policy — full, prorated, or none, **defaulted to none** and labelled as configuration (D-10). Nothing recaptures silently: the amount and the reason appear on the move-out preview (US-14 move-out, PRD 01 US-707) before the tenant agrees to it. A recapture a tenant first sees on a final invoice is a chargeback.
- **AC (the lease states the consequence, not only the condition — added 2026-08-25 from the UX review; B-175):** the minimum-stay sentence on the signed agreement **names what is recovered**, branched on the facility's `promoRecapturePolicy`: `prorated` says the share of the discount covering the months not stayed, `full` says the whole discount given, and `none` states the condition and stops — because at a facility that recovers nothing, a signed promise to charge the discount back is a term the operator does not do, which is the same defect mirrored. B-144 wrote the sentence deliberately silent because nothing recovered anything yet; B-145 built the recapture and the sentence stayed silent, so the word "recovered" first reached the tenant on the move-out screen, with the amount already computed and the tenant already leaving. **The wording and the arithmetic are pinned to each other by test** — each policy's sentence asserted beside what `recaptureFor` actually returns under it — so the two cannot drift apart again the way they did between B-144 and B-145.
- **AC (the screen that sets the minimum stay states whether it is enforced — added 2026-08-25 from the UX and accessibility reviews; B-176):** `minStayMonths` and `promoRecapturePolicy` are set on two different screens, and neither mentioned the other, so an operator could run a six-month minimum for a year in the belief it was enforced while the facility's policy was the `none` default. The promotions screen now **reads the facility's current policy and states it** beside the minimum-stay field, with a link to the setting; a submitted minimum stay at a `none` facility returns a **confirm-and-echo step** (3.3.4) naming the consequence before it is created; and the promotions list appends "not recovered on early move-out" to any minimum stay the facility does not recover. The confirm exists because **a promotion cannot be edited after creation** — the only writes are create, status and codes — so the screen also says so, and the operator who believed the term was enforced cannot correct it, only end it and start again. The operator-facing sentence lives beside `recaptureFor` with the tenant-facing one from B-175 and is pinned by the same test.
- **AC (a per-row control is named by its row, not by its form):** the three status controls render once per promotion, so a rotor listing the page's buttons heard "Make live, Pause, End" repeated with nothing identifying which promotion (2.4.6). Each carries `aria-label` composing the action with the promotion's name. The `aria-label` on the wrapping form buys nothing — **no screen reader composes a form's label into its descendants' accessible names**, the rule `task-complete-form.tsx` was created to state.
- **AC (a recapture is a fee, and forgiving one is its own decision — added 2026-08-24 from the operator review; B-168):** the recapture posts as a **`kind: 'fee'` invoice** through the same primitive as every other fee, so autopay collects it, `waivableFees` lists it, `waiveFeeInvoice` voids it, it ages and it dunns — a charge that no existing tool can reach is the same defect one layer down. It is **its own settleable line beside arrears**, never folded into a residual: a departed tenant can pay, dispute or have forgiven the recapture without touching genuine rent debt. Reducing or waiving it at the counter is a **lever of its own**, carrying a reason code and gated by the monetary-authority ladder **on the amount forgiven** rather than on the amount charged — the discretion being exercised is the gap. It is deliberately NOT the move-out write-off, which is all-or-nothing across the entire residual and would forgive $400 of arrears to settle a disputed $60 recapture under one reason code. **Recapture billed, waived and collected are reportable per promotion** on the promotions report: a minimum stay that bills $4,000 and waives $3,800 at the desk is not a term, and one that bills $4,000 and collects $600 from tenants who have already left is a different failure — one figure cannot show either. D-89 is untouched: a transfer still recovers nothing.

**US-11 [MVP]** As a regional manager, I schedule rate increases for existing tenants — one-off (selected tenants) or rule-based (e.g., tenants ≥ 9 months since last increase and ≥ $15 below street) — with required advance notice, so revenue keeps pace without violating notice terms.
- AC: Each scheduled increase records: tenant, unit, current rate, new rate, effective date, notice date. The system generates the notice letter/email from a template on the notice date (see 4.7 for delivery), and blocks effective dates that violate the facility's configured minimum notice period (default 30 days; configurable, since required notice varies by state/lease). The new rate applies automatically to the first invoice on/after the effective date, with proration if mid-cycle billing anniversary rules require it. Increases are cancellable up to the effective date; cancellation is audit-logged.
- AC: A rate-increase review screen shows pending increases with projected revenue delta; regional/owner approval is required before notices go out.
- **AC (schema, MVP — must exist before the data does):** every change to a lease's in-place rate writes a `LeaseRateChange` row — lease, previous cents, new cents, effective date, reason (`move_in`, `ecri`, `transfer`, `promo_expiry`, `manual`), actor, notice-sent reference (nullable until this story ships), and the notice days given. The **first** row is written at move-in. `Lease.monthlyRateCents` remains the current rate and is written **only** through the function that writes the history row — the same discipline as `recomputeUnitStatus()`.
  This is deliberately built ahead of the workflow it serves (D-5 keeps the workflow in Phase 2, and that stands). Rule-based eligibility asks two questions — when did we last raise this tenant, and how far below street is he — and neither can be reconstructed retroactively. If the history starts when the workflow ships, the first year of tenants is permanently ineligible.
- **AC (lowering a rate, added 2026-08-23 — built in B-153):** the same workflow runs in the other direction as a **retention save**. A `TenantRateIncrease` row with a negative delta, a `LeaseRateChange` written through the same `applyRateChange()` with reason `retention`, and **no notice period** — US-11's minimum notice protects a tenant about to be charged more, and nothing statutory governs charging less. There is **no separate approval step**: the authority check is the approval, so the row is created `approved` and applies on its effective date, which may be today. Authority is the **existing monetary limits** — `credits:manual` measured against the monthly amount given away — rather than `rates:tenant_increase`, which is regional-and-above by seed and is the wrong bar for a counter conversation; that lands on manager-and-above by default with no new threshold, and over the limit it escalates to the next role that can carry it, exactly as a refund does. A reason code is required and audit-logged (`rate.tenant_decreased`). One live rate change per lease, in either direction.
- **AC (notice delivery, added 2026-08-23 from D-88 — built in B-152):** the minimum-notice guarantee above is about **delivery**, not intent, and the workflow must be able to show that the notice went out. Each increase records the domain event its notice was emitted as, written in the same transaction as the status, and the nightly apply run reconciles that event against the resulting `Message` rows before anything is charged. A hard bounce, a suppression hit, or **no send record at all** moves the increase to `notice_failed` and raises a high-priority task; a held increase never applies, is dropped from the projected revenue delta, and the remedy is to re-notice it from a good address — which restarts the notice clock (D-88, Option A). The check runs against every live notice daily, not only those reaching their effective date: an operator who first hears about a dead address on the effective date has no notice period left to give.
- **AC (the way back from a held increase, added 2026-08-24 from the operator review; B-166):** D-88's remedy is an operation, not an instruction. A held increase carries a **re-notice** action that clones it at the same figures with a recomputed notice and effective date, cancels the held row, and links the two so the pair reads as one attempt to notice one increase rather than as two increases on the same tenant. The clone keeps the **original approval** — the approver signed off on a delta from a rate the hold did not change — and the re-notice is refused if that rate has since moved. Re-noticing is **refused while the tenant's contact record still carries the address the notice failed at**, naming that address: re-sending to an address that has already bounced gives no notice and holds the increase again a day later. The clock restarts as a guarantee about the **period**, not about the date moving — an increase with a full notice period still left in it keeps the effective date the tenant would have been told about, and one whose runway the hold consumed slides to the soonest legal date. A **batch re-notice scoped to a facility** exists because the usual cause is a provider incident or a bounced corporate domain rather than one bad address, and it reports the rows it could not send by **name**, not as a count. `rate_increase_notice_undelivered` is completed **only** by an actual re-notice or cancel; a note cannot close it, because the increase is still on hold whatever anybody types. Audited as `rate.increase_renoticed` with a required reason.
- **AC (the increase is a step, not a jump — added 2026-08-24 from the operator review; B-165):** rule-based eligibility answers *who*, and the PRD has never said *how much*. The target rate is computed from a **per-facility ECRI policy** — percent step, minimum and maximum dollar step, cap-at-street, minimum months since the last change, minimum gap to street — as `min(street, inPlace × (1 + pct))` bounded by the min and max and rounded to whole dollars. Street rate is a **ceiling, not a target**: raising an in-place rate straight to street in one letter is a 60%-class increase on a long tenant, and it is what the code does today because `targetRateFor` returns the street rate and nothing else. Every eligibility figure is facility configuration with a form control in the same item (never a module constant), and the batch preview states **the rule and the percentage** alongside the dollar delta, because that is what the person approving it is approving. **Settled 2026-08-24 as D-94 and built in B-165:** the seeded default for a new facility is **10%, floor $5, ceiling $30, capped at street**, with the eligibility example above (≥ 9 months, ≥ $15 below street) becoming two more configurable columns. The old behaviour was deliberately not kept as the default — a facility nobody has configured must not send the letter that loses the cohort. A policy configured down to no step at all drops the lease from the worklist rather than scheduling a notice announcing that nothing changed.
- **AC (the screen names the tenant, and the id is never typed — added 2026-08-25 from the UX and accessibility reviews; B-177):** both money forms on the rate-changes screen took a **free-text "Lease ID"** with the hint "From the tenant's lease page", while the eligible table beside them already listed every tenant, unit and current rate and offered no action at all. The workflow was therefore copy a cuid, paste a cuid — and a mistyped one permanently reduced a **different** tenant's rate, with a reason code recorded against them and nothing between the paste and the commit. Both forms now take a **picker of the facility's occupying leases** ("Ada Renter — unit 104 — $129.00/mo now"), so there is no id to mistype and no lease on offer that a rate change is illegal on; the eligible table carries **per-row links that prefill it**; and **both forms return a confirm-and-echo step** (3.3.4) naming tenant, unit, what they pay now, what they will pay and from when — plus the reason code on a decrease, which is the half that is recorded permanently. The echo is read from the database, never from the submitted form: the current rate is the figure being agreed *against*, and echoing back what the browser sent would confirm whatever the page happened to be showing.
- **AC (a per-row control is named by its row):** "Approve" and "Cancel" render once per scheduled increase with identical accessible names and identical styling side by side, so the rotor read "Approve, Cancel, Approve, Cancel…" and the destructive one was one row-misread away (**2.4.6 AA**). Each carries an `aria-label` naming its tenant and unit, **Cancel is visually destructive** rather than a twin of Approve, and the batch controls are named by what the batch holds ("Cancel 12 increases effective Sep 23, 2026") rather than by the word "batch" repeated. Money columns are headed with their unit — `Now ($/mo)`, not `Now` — and every date on the screen is in one format; the notice-period sentence rendered a raw `2026-09-23` beside two "Sep 23, 2026"s.
- **AC (promotional rates):** a discount is a line item; the lease rate is the real rate. A "$1 first month" tenant is not a $1 tenant. Recording a promotion as the lease rate makes economic occupancy lie and makes the first rules-based increase batch do something absurd.

**US-12 [P2]** Rules-based revenue management: automatic street-rate suggestions from occupancy per unit type (e.g., raise when type occupancy > 92%), one-click apply. (Comparable to incumbent "rate management" offerings — [Storable SiteLink](https://www.storable.com/products/sitelink/).)

**US-44 [MVP]** As an owner, I configure the protection-plan catalog and the facility's protection policy, so every move-in either carries a plan or carries evidence of the tenant's own cover.
*(Added 2026-07-31 from the operator review. Protection is the highest-margin line on the invoice and is currently modelled as a checkbox: the tenant waives by claiming homeowner's cover, nobody sees the declaration page, the policy lapses eight months later, the unit floods, and the operator is in a coverage argument with no record.)*
- AC: Per-facility policy setting — protection **required** (with a proof-of-insurance waiver permitted) vs **optional**. Texas practice is generally "required, or show proof"; the setting ships defaulted and clearly labelled as configuration (D-10).

- AC: **Lapse handling, decided as D-17 — notice, then auto-enrolment.** A nightly scan (B-043) finds recorded proof expiring within 30 days and sends the tenant a notice with a portal path to upload replacement proof. If the proof lapses without replacement, the lease is **enrolled into the facility's default protection tier**, the premium begins on the next invoice, and the tenant is notified of the enrolment and its cost. The behaviour is a per-facility policy setting and applies regardless of what the lease captured at signing. Lapsed proof also flags the lease and shows on the tenant profile, and a task is created so a human sees it.

Before this runs against a real tenant it needs an attorney pass on three specific points — the notice copy, the lease clause that authorises enrolment, and whether the facility policy may apply to leases signed before the setting existed. As with every legal artifact in this project, the shipped text is a draft and is not legal advice.
- **Built in B-043, and how it ships (D-26):** the scan, the 30-day notice event, the lapse task and the enrolment all exist. The per-facility switch ships **off** and the default tier ships **unset**, so a lapsed proof raises a high-priority task and charges nothing until a facility deliberately turns it on in Settings — the attorney pass above is what the switch is waiting on. Two refusals guard it: Settings will not enable auto-enrolment with no tier chosen, and the scan will not enrol when the configured tier is no longer on sale, in both cases leaving the task for a person.
- **AC (a transfer does not end the monitoring) — added 2026-08-24, B-163:** the `ProtectionWaiver` moves to the lease a transfer opens, carrying its expiry unchanged — a transfer is not a renewal — **and an already-lapsed waiver moves too**, or a unit swap becomes a way to shed a lapse that has already happened. The nightly scan additionally follows an orphaned waiver forward along `transferredFromLeaseId` and re-attaches it, which is both the repair for waivers stranded before this shipped and the backstop against a future writer that opens a lease from another and forgets. It matters more than it looks: the scan reads waivers by `leaseId` and then filters to occupying leases, so a waiver naming an ended lease was not missed once — it was dropped permanently, `insurance_proof_lapsed` never fired, and this policy was silently unenforced for exactly the tenants who moved. A successor that recorded its own certificate keeps it.
- **AC (the number the policy is about) — added 2026-08-24, B-163:** a staff list of **occupying leases carrying neither a plan nor an unexpired waiver**, per facility, longest-uncovered first, distinguishing a certificate that lapsed from one that was never recorded — the two are different phone calls. Attach rate (below) is a period metric about how last month's move-ins were sold and cannot show this: a tenant who waived at signing two years ago and let the certificate lapse has never appeared in it and is exactly who this policy is for. A waiver a manager accepted with **no expiry** counts as cover — that is the override case two ACs above, a decision on the record rather than an absence. The list renders at facilities where protection is optional too, and says so rather than implying a breach.
- AC: "insurance" describes cover the tenant already holds somewhere else. What we sell is a **protection plan**. The two words are not interchangeable in copy, in the schema, or on an invoice.
- AC: Attach rate is reportable: plan-enrolled leases ÷ new move-ins, per facility, per month, **and per staff member who completed the move-in**. Per-staff is the point — it is a coaching number, not a vanity metric.
- **Scope, decided 2026-07-31:** this ships as an **in-house protection plan catalog** with our own waiver record. §10 Q5 (whether a full insurance *program* — provider integration, claims — belongs in any phase, and which PRD would own it) **stays open** and is not a blocker: if a program is adopted later it replaces the catalog behind the same lease-facing behaviour.

**US-14 [MVP]** Lease lifecycle: reserve → move-in → (transfer)* → move-out.

Restarting the ladder first would run it against exactly the tenants whose holds have not yet been carried. This is B-091's rule and it applies here for the same reason.
  - **AC (collections continuity): a transfer must not be a way to stop being past due.** After a transfer the delinquency engine neither halts the tenant as `moved_out` — because the arrears sit on a lease the transfer just ended — nor restarts them at zero days, because the new lease has no invoices. `daysPastDue` still anchors to the oldest unpaid invoice's original due date (D-25) across the transfer boundary, and the ladder resumes at the step it was on. **Whether the arrears follow the tenant or stay on the closed lease is D-86**; this AC is written to whichever answer that decision takes. **D-86 settled 2026-08-21 (Option A) and B-138 built it:** the unpaid invoices — `open` and `partially_paid`, the part-paid one included because allocation is oldest-first and it *is* the anchor — are re-pointed at the new lease, and the balance follows them as a pair of `adjustment` ledger entries per invoice, each naming that invoice so the ledger screen still reconciles. Nothing already posted is edited. **Two pieces of collections state deliberately do NOT move**, and both are read along `Lease.transferredFromLeaseId` instead: a `DelinquencyStepRun`, because re-pointing it would make the record of a served notice name a unit it never named; and a *paid* late-fee invoice, which records that a fee step has already been charged. The timeline engine and the late-fee ladder both read their position along that link, so the ladder resumes at the step it was on and the fee steps already charged are not charged again. The new lease also carries the tenant's standing (`delinquent`, `pending_auction`) and the timeline pin rather than opening `active` beside its own arrears.
  - **AC (eligibility): a tenant may not self-serve out of the lien pipeline.** `OCCUPYING_LEASE_STATUSES` includes `pending_auction` and the tenant-facing transfer scopes on it, so the product currently offers a unit swap to a lease whose goods are being prepared for sale. The tenant-facing path refuses at `pending_auction`. **Whether staff may still transfer such a lease, and under whose authority, is D-85** — blocking outright is deliberately *not* the default, because an operator often wants a delinquent tenant to downsize into something they can afford, and refusing that costs the operator the tenant and the balance. **D-85 settled 2026-08-21 (Option A):** the portal never offers a lien-pipeline transfer at all, and staff may perform one with manager-and-above approval, a reason code and a lien clock that does not reset. B-137 built the tenant-facing refusal; the staff-side approval, reason code and clock guarantee are **not yet built** and are B-157 — until then a staff transfer of a `pending_auction` lease is possible with only `leases:transfer`, which is manager-and-above by role but carries no reason code and asserts nothing about the clock.
  - **AC (the tenant-facing request states its own limits):** the requested move date carries a server-side ceiling as well as a `max` on the input, and every screen showing a pending transfer states the hold's absolute facility-local expiry date and time (PRD 01 §6.8.1). No screen may claim the unit is held "until you complete or cancel it" — the hold also lapses, and `holdWindowSentence` already renders the truth.
  - **AC (a transfer must not silently drop what was already true of the tenant — added 2026-08-24 from the operator review; B-162, B-163):** an approved, noticed `TenantRateIncrease` due on the old lease either **re-points to the new lease or the transfer refuses with a named problem** — the current `ok: true, "skipped — the lease has ended"` reports success for work it discarded, which is the worst of the three possible behaviours. ECRI tenure (months since the last rate change) reads **through `transferredFromLeaseId`** rather than from the new lease's own first `LeaseRateChange`, which a transfer writes dated today and which therefore exempts every transferred tenant from the next cycle. A live `ProtectionWaiver` is re-pointed **in the same transaction** as the lease copy, and the expiry scan reads across the transfer chain as a backstop, since a waiver naming an ended lease is dropped by the scan permanently rather than missed once — the same applies to `deceased` and `litigation` waivers. **What the new rate should be settled 2026-08-24 as D-93 and B-162 built it** — see the AC below. The `TenantRateIncrease` half was settled the same way: it is **cancelled**, not re-pointed, for the reason D-93 gives. The `ProtectionWaiver` half is B-163 and remains open.
  - **AC (what the new lease costs) — added 2026-08-24, B-162, D-93:** the transfer opens the new lease at the rate the facility's `transferRatePolicy` gives, and **the shipped default keeps the tenant's discount off street** — `new street × (in-place ÷ old street)`, capped at street — so a like-for-like move costs exactly what they were paying, a downsize prices down and an upsize prices up. `street` and `in_place` are the two alternatives, both per-facility (D-10). **The figure is settable on the preview**, echoed back with the policy figure and the street rate beside it so a manager who moved off the policy can see that they did, and both are recorded on the audit row. **A transfer that raises the rent says so before it is confirmed**, naming both figures and stating that there is no notice period behind the new one: a transfer is not a rate increase, and re-pricing to street through a screen for a service request the tenant asked for is how US-11's notice, approval and record are bypassed without anyone deciding to.
  - **AC (the promotion follows the tenant) — added 2026-08-24, B-162, D-93:** the `PromoRedemption` is re-pointed to the new lease, so the remaining discounted periods, the minimum stay and the recapture obligation all move with the tenant. D-89 settled that a transfer recaptures nothing and named the hole that left: a tenant inside a minimum stay could transfer to the cheapest unit on site and walk out of the next lease owing nothing, while also losing the discounted months they were promised. **The period index a discount schedule is keyed to is counted across the transfer chain**, not from the new lease's own start, or the remaining discounts land months late; and **the minimum stay is measured from when the tenancy began**, not from the transfer date, or a tenant who has served the whole minimum is billed a recapture.
  - **AC (ECRI tenure) — added 2026-08-24, B-162:** months-since-last-change ignores a `LeaseRateChange` whose reason is `transfer` and falls back to the **chain's origin start date**, not the new lease's. The transfer writes such a row dated today and the new lease's `startDate` is the transfer date, so both halves of the fallback lied and asking to swap units was a way to opt out of every increase.
- *Move-out:* records date, computes final balance (prorated refund or amount due per facility policy), settles or writes off small balances (≤ configurable threshold, logged), revokes gate access (event to PRD 04).   - **AC (notice given off-platform is recorded, and the recording says whether it worked) — added 2026-08-25, B-186/B-194:** `noticeGivenAt` had exactly one writer, the tenant's own portal request, so every walk-in read as having given no notice at all and the shortfall figure was a constant that looked like a measurement. Where two date fields appear on one screen, the screen says which is which and which one feeds the shortfall.

Whether a short-notice move-out is charged, and on what basis, is **D-99** — a lease-terms and per-state question (D-10), draft-only and not legal advice. ### 4.5 Billing Engine

**US-17 [MVP]** As the system, I generate recurring monthly invoices per lease on the configured billing day (anniversary-date or first-of-month per facility policy), including rent, insurance, recurring fees, and taxes, so tenants are billed accurately without manual work.
- AC: Invoices generate N days before due (configurable, default 5) and notify the tenant (email; SMS P2, shared comms service with PRD 03); line items carry tax treatment per facility tax settings; invoice numbering is sequential per facility and gapless.
- **Built in B-044, and how it ships (D-27):** `anniversary` is the default policy and a lease's billing day is the **facility-local day it started**, clamped to 28 — so the move-in payment buys a whole period from that day and nothing is prorated on the way in. `first_of_month` is built and selectable. Idempotency is the unique constraint on `(leaseId, periodStart)`, so the run is re-runnable and catches up missed dates without re-billing. Numbering uses the same gapless row-lock counter as receipts (D-22), zero-padded so the series sorts as it reads. Tax is applied per jurisdiction to the **rent line only** — a protection plan is not rent; per-component taxability per state is the seam a second state will need (D-10).

**US-18 [MVP]** Proration: move-ins, move-outs, and transfers prorate rent by day count against the billing period, per facility policy (prorate in/out both configurable; option: no refund on move-out).
- AC: Proration math is deterministic and unit-tested (documented formula: daily rate = monthly rate / days in billing period; rounding half-up to cents at line level); every prorated line shows the day range on the invoice.
- **Built in B-044 as `packages/core/billing`**, which is the only implementation — B-077's transfer wizard calls it twice (a prorated move-out and a prorated move-in on one day) rather than building a second one. Rounding is at **line** level, asserted in a test: a daily rate rounded first and multiplied out is systematically two cents in the operator's favour on a $129 month. A full period never goes through the division at all, so a whole month bills exactly the rate. `charged + refunded === the full period` is asserted for every day of a 31-day month.

**US-19 [MVP]** Autopay: tenants with a stored payment method (card/ACH via the shared payment provider) are charged automatically on the due date in a nightly autopay run.
- AC: Autopay run is idempotent (safe to re-run; never double-charges); run results are visible in a Billing Runs screen (succeeded / failed / skipped with reasons); failures immediately enter the retry schedule.
- **Built in B-045.** "Never double-charges" is four layered guards, not one: `JobRun`'s uniqueness on (job, facility, business date) serialises the run against itself; the query excludes any invoice with a payment attempt already pending or succeeded; Stripe's idempotency key is derived from the invoice **and** the business date; and `createChargeIntent` recognises a deduplicated intent and discards its own duplicate row instead of colliding. The second guard is durable only because settlement writes a `PaymentAllocation` before the Stripe call and the webhook moves the invoice to paid — **D-28**. Skips are named, not counted: autopay off, no saved card, attempt in flight, nothing outstanding. An off-session decline arrives **synchronously** rather than as a webhook, so the run records it and emits `payment.failed` with Stripe's decline code itself — B-046's retry schedule and the `expired_card` short-circuit both read that code. Invoices due **on or before** the business date are collected, so an outage does not become a delinquency the tenant did not earn.

**US-20 [MVP]** Failed-payment retry: configurable retry schedule (default: retry on day +1, +3, +5; max 3 retries), tenant notified on each failure, staff see a "failed payments" queue.
- AC: A payment that ultimately fails leaves the invoice unpaid and the lease enters the delinquency clock from the original due date (not the last retry); card-expired failures skip retries and notify the tenant to update the card (deep link to portal, PRD 03).
- **Built in B-046.** The schedule is `Facility.paymentRetryDays`, defaulting to +1/+3/+5, and **every offset is measured from the invoice's original due date, never from the last attempt** — measuring from the previous try stretches a 5-day schedule into a 9-day one and the tenant drifts further past due on each decline instead of the schedule converging. That is the same anchoring rule `daysPastDue` uses (D-25). A terminal decline — `expired_card` and eight siblings — stops the schedule wherever it is, and is reported ahead of exhaustion because "the card has expired" is actionable where "we ran out of retries" is not. The failed-payments queue is a `Task` of type `failed_payment` (US-41), raised **once** and **withdrawn automatically** when the invoice is paid.
- **Owner decision, 2026-08-06 — when a person gets involved, and what the tenant hears.** The task is raised on the **second decline**, not when the schedule finishes: waiting for the whole +1/+3/+5 sequence means nobody looks for six days, by which time the tenant is most of the way to a late fee over something a phone call fixes. It is high priority whichever trigger raised it, and the retry schedule keeps running afterwards — a person and a retry are not alternatives. Separately, the tenant is messaged **once a day for three days from the first decline** (`payment.retry_reminder`), deliberately not one message per retry attempt: retries land on +1/+3/+5, so attempt-driven messages would arrive on days 1, 3 and 5 with silence in between, and the day the tenant is most likely to act — the day after they first hear — would say nothing. **This sends email until B-074 configures the SMS channel**, because MVP comms is email-only (PRD 05 FR-4); the message is an event and the channel is a rule, so it becomes a text with no code change here. The decline code travels on `payment.failed` for B-050 to branch on, and the portal deep link is B-051's.

**US-21 [MVP]** Late fee schedule: per-facility rules such as "$X or Y% (greater/lesser) at N days late; second fee at M days" — applied automatically by the delinquency engine, itemized on the ledger.
- AC: Late fees respect configurable caps; waiving a late fee requires the fee-waive permission and a reason code; waivers are audit-logged and reportable.
- AC: the fee catalogue covers what a facility actually charges: `admin`, `late`, `nsf`, `lien`, **`lock_cut`, `cleaning`, `damage`, `transfer`, `certified_mail`, `auction_cost`** — all effective-dated per facility like the original four (FR-9). Uncharged lock-cut and cleaning is $50–75 per move-out, and a fee with nowhere to post is a fee nobody charges.
- **AC (a configurable fee type has a control that posts it — added 2026-08-24 from the operator review; B-167):** six of the ten types above (`lock_cut`, `cleaning`, `damage`, `lien`, `certified_mail`, `auction_cost`) are configurable per facility and read by no code, so the schedule is a settings screen with no consequence. One ad-hoc charge control, reached from the move-out screen and the tenant profile — fee type, amount pre-filled from the facility schedule and overridable, required note — posts through the **same fee-invoice primitive** as `nsf` so autopay collects it, `waiveFee` reaches it, it ages and it dunns; the override is gated by the existing monetary-authority ladder on the overridden amount and audited; `lien` and `certified_mail` costs post **as incurred** from the notice pipeline, so a cure quote is arithmetically complete rather than short by the postage. A charge that no existing tool can waive or report on is the same defect one layer down, so no new charge shape is introduced.
- **Built in B-167, and it settled three things the AC left open.** (1) **Posting a fee is its own permission, `fees:charge`**, held by counter staff and above: the person who cut the lock or walked the dirty unit is who knows the fee is owed, and the amount is the schedule's decision rather than theirs. (2) **The authority ladder is measured against the DEPARTURE from the schedule, in either direction, against the existing fee-waiver limit** — under-charging is a waiver by another name, over-charging is discretion over a tenant's money in the direction that hurts them, and one symmetric rule needs no new limit column. A counter staffer's $0 limit therefore means "the facility's price, exactly"; a fee type the facility has never priced is a departure from zero, so all of it needs waiver authority. (3) **`(Invoice.leaseId, periodStart)` is now unique only for `kind: 'rent'`** — it was the rent generator's idempotency key and nothing else's, and unscoped it capped a lease at one fee invoice per calendar day: a move-out posting cleaning and damage on the same afternoon lost the second silently, and an NSF fee landing on a day that already had a late fee threw. Late fees keep their own idempotency, which was always the real one (`chargedSteps` reads back which ladder steps have been charged from the fee line descriptions). The `lien` cost posts on generating the lien notice — **not** on a correction, and after that notice's own claim is snapshotted, so a notice never quotes a fee it is itself creating — and the `certified_mail` cost posts in `recordNoticeDelivery`, which both the hand-recorded and the automated (B-083) routes pass through.
- **Built in B-047.** The ladder is `LateFeeRule`, effective-dated **per step** so changing the second fee leaves the first alone, with `basis` of flat / percent / greater / lesser and a per-step cap. **The cap applies after the greater/lesser choice, not before** — "the greater of $20 or 10%, capped at $15" on a $900 balance is $15, where capping each side first returns $20 and breaches the configured cap. A fee never exceeds what is owed, and a credit balance can never produce a negative fee.
- **A late fee is its own invoice (`Invoice.kind = 'fee'`), and fees are never charged on fees.** The base and the days-past-due anchor both read **rent** invoices only; without that split an unpaid fee would age and earn more fees, and a balance would compound with nobody having decided it should. Its own invoice rather than a line appended to the rent invoice, because an invoice the tenant has already been sent must not change totals after the fact — and because autopay collects invoices, so a fee posted only to the ledger would never be charged automatically.
- **Assessment runs nightly at 2am local** (`billing.assess-late-fees`), between invoice generation at 1am and autopay at 3am, so a fee raised tonight is collected tonight. US-21 assigns this to the delinquency engine, which is **B-057** in Phase 2; that item drives these same functions from a timeline stage rather than reimplementing the arithmetic. A facility with no configured ladder is charged nothing — no ladder is an operator choice, not a reason to fall back to a default nobody agreed to.
- **Waiving** requires `fees:waive` at the facility, a reason code, and the amount within the actor's monetary limit (RBAC-2) — an over-limit refusal names the rank that can approve it rather than simply failing. The waiver posts a **credit** and marks the invoice `void`, never `paid` and never deleted: the charge and the credit that cancelled it both stay visible, and the revenue report can tell forgiven money from collected money.

**US-22 [MVP]** Partial payments: staff (and portal) can accept any amount; allocation order is configurable per facility (default: taxes → fees → insurance → oldest rent first).
- AC: Allocation is displayed at payment time and on the receipt; a partial payment does not by itself stop the delinquency clock (configurable: option to require balance below threshold to exit delinquency).
- **Built in B-048.** The order is `Facility.paymentAllocationOrder`, four categories edited as four selects (a permutation, not free text). Within a category, **oldest due date first**, then by invoice id so a receipt reprinted tomorrow matches the one the tenant was handed. A category left out of the order is paid **last rather than never** — a misconfigured order should degrade to a worse sequence, not make money uncollectable. An **over-payment is reported, never placed**: where to put it is a decision (refuse, hold, refund) and the allocator does not make it silently.
- **Found and fixed: counter payments were never allocated at all.** B-039 posted a ledger entry and nothing else, so however much cash came across the desk every invoice stayed `open` — the balance moved and the invoices did not, which is the split that makes autopay re-charge and AR ageing lie. Counter payments now allocate in the same transaction, and the result is returned for the screen and the receipt.
- **US-23's refunds:** a refund is its own `Payment` row pointing at the original through `refundOfPaymentId`, never an edit of it — the original is a fact with a receipt number a tenant is holding. Card refunds go to the original method via Stripe (idempotency-keyed on payment and amount) and are made **before** the local write, because a refund we recorded and the provider never made is the worse way to fail. **Cash and cheque refunds are recorded `pending` — a payable** — because the money has not left until somebody hands it over. Refunding **unwinds the allocation** so the invoices reopen; an invoice left reading `paid` on money that went back is uncollected forever and invisible to every ageing report. Refunding a card payment as cash is permitted and **audited as a changed method**, because that is the shape an internal fraud takes.

**US-23 [MVP]** Refunds: card refunds to original payment method via provider; cash/check refunds recorded as payable with a check-number field; all refunds require reason code and permission per RBAC-2.
- AC: Refund cannot exceed the original payment; partial refunds supported; refunds appear on deposits reconciliation and the audit log.

**US-46 [MVP]** As a facility manager, when a payment comes back — a bounced check, an ACH return, a card chargeback — I record it, the tenant owes the money again, the NSF fee is charged, and the drawer is not short.
*(Added 2026-08-21 from the operator review, and verified: `feeType: 'nsf'` and `'lien'` are configurable per facility and read by nothing; `LedgerEntry.reversalOfId` exists with a schema comment citing FR-8 and is written by no code; the payment reconciler's `HANDLED_EVENTS` covers five Stripe events and no `charge.dispute.*`. There is no path at all — today a returned payment stays recorded as collected, forever.)*
- AC: a returned payment is recorded as a **reversal, not a refund**. It writes a new append-only `LedgerEntry` pointing at the original through `reversalOfId`; the original `Payment` row is never edited — the US-23 rule, because the original is a fact with a receipt number on it (D-22).
- AC: **a reversal never touches the cash drawer.** `refundPayment` pulls cash and check refunds from the open drawer, which is correct for a refund and wrong for a bounce: using it would make the till short by money that never left it. Reversal and refund are different operations with different ledger shapes, and the screens must not offer one where the other is meant.
- AC: the reversal unwinds the original payment's `PaymentAllocation` rows, so the invoices it settled re-open and `daysPastDue` (D-25) sees the arrears it would have seen had the payment never arrived. A returned payment that leaves an invoice reading as paid is invisible to every collections mechanism in the product.
- AC: the facility's configured `nsf` fee is assessed automatically, itemised on the ledger as its own line, and waivable with permission and a reason code like every other fee (US-21).
- AC: this raises the **existing** `failed_payment` task at high priority — no new queue, per US-41's rule — carrying the tenant, the amount and the return reason.
- AC: `charge.dispute.created` and `charge.dispute.closed` are handled by the reconciler under the same idempotency contract as the events already there, and post their reversal and their task the same way a returned check does. A chargeback that reaches nothing is a balance an operator first learns about from a bank statement.
- AC: recording a returned payment is audit-logged (US-38) with actor, reason code and the original payment id.

**US-24 [MVP]** Tenant ledger: a single chronological ledger per lease showing every charge, tax, payment, credit, refund, and write-off with running balance. AC: ledger totals always reconcile to invoice totals and reported AR; exportable to CSV/PDF.
- **Built in B-049.** The running balance, the totals and the reconciliation all come from `packages/core/billing` — no figure is computed in the page, the same rule D-25 set for metrics. **The reconciliation is a function with a test, not a claim in a document**, and it is stated on the screen: reconciled, or the difference with its likely cause in plain English, because a manager who sees a discrepancy needs to know whether it is expected before they ring anyone.
- **Entries are never re-signed** — a payment stays negative, because the ledger is append-only (FR-8) and a screen that flipped signs to tidy a column would be presenting something other than the record. The *summary* reports payments, credits and write-offs as positive magnitudes, since "Payments: −$129.00" reads as a payment that went the wrong way.
- **Ordering breaks ties on id**, because a charge and the payment settling it are routinely written in one transaction with identical timestamps — without a stable second key a reprinted statement would disagree with the one the tenant was handed.
- **A charge with no invoice behind it reconciles rather than alarming**: B-026 posts the move-in charge before the billing engine exists, and a system that called that a discrepancy would cry wolf on every tenant who ever moved in.
- **CSV export only.** US-24 says "CSV/PDF" and the PDF is not built — there is no document renderer in this project, and B-061's notice generation is where one arrives.

### 4.6 Delinquency & Lien Workflow

Design informed by the commonly described US lien-sale process: default → denial of access/overlock → written lien notice with itemized claim and conspicuous sale warning → advertising per state rules → auction after statutory waiting periods → application of proceeds and handling of surplus. Notice delivery is commonly by verified mail or email where state law permits, with proof-of-delivery requirements varying by state; waiting periods (e.g., 5–10 days before access denial, ~15 days after first advertisement before sale) and advertising rules (e.g., newspaper publication once a week for two consecutive weeks vs. online advertising) vary by state ([Inside Self-Storage: 7 Steps to Lien-Sale Success](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/legal-issues/7-steps-to-lien-sale-success-avoiding-wrongful-sale-liability-in-the-self-storage-auction-process); see also [OpenTech lien-law update](https://opentechalliance.com/blog/self-storage-legislation-update-changes-to-your-self-storage-lien-laws/) and [Column's California notice guide](https://www.column.us/resources/what-you-need-to-know-about-storage-units-and-public-notice-of-sale-in-california/)). **The workflow below is therefore a configurable state machine, not a hardcoded legal process. Not legal advice; operators must validate configuration with counsel per state.**

**US-45 [MVP]** As an owner, a tenant who stops paying loses gate access on a fixed day and gets it back the moment he is current, without anyone deciding to act.
*(Added 2026-07-31, decided as **D-16**. Everything else in this section is Phase 2 per D-1 and stays there — this is the single threshold moved forward, because the rest of MVP has emails and late fees and no leverage. A tenant twelve days past due who can still open his unit with a working code has no reason to pay this week.)*
- AC: one per-facility, versioned rule — **suspend the tenant's `AccessGrant` at N days past due**, default 6 (Texas practice), configurable. Not a timeline engine: one threshold, two transitions, driven by the shared `daysPastDue(lease)` definition (US-39) and executed through the Access Control Service that already exists (PRD 03 US-3).
- AC: **restore is automatic** within ~2 minutes of a qualifying payment, with no staff action. The tenant is notified on both transitions.
- AC: **"qualifying payment" means the balance reaches zero** — rent, late fees, everything — held as a per-facility setting alongside the partial-payment allocation policy (US-22) so it can be relaxed later without a migration. It does not default silently, and it is the same setting the Phase-2 timeline reads. Rationale (D-16): any partial-payment rule teaches a tenant to pay the minimum that reopens the gate, every month.
- AC: suspension and restore each write an audit entry carrying the triggering invoice and the day count, and each renders on the tenant profile in plain English — "Access suspended, 12 days past due, 2026-07-18".
- AC: an active `LeaseHold` whose declared effects include halting access suspension (US-42) blocks this rule outright.
- AC: the Phase-2 delinquency engine (US-25) **inherits** this rule as its access step rather than reimplementing it; the rule's per-facility threshold becomes one step in the configured timeline when that lands.
- **Built in B-098.** The decision is `gateDecision` in `packages/core/access` — pure, so every boundary is testable without a gate controller or a clock. **Evaluated per TENANT, not per lease:** `AccessGrant` is one row per (facility, tenant) by PRD 03 FR-1's design, which matches the physical reality that a shared gate either opens for someone or it does not. So the rule reads the highest day count across that tenant's leases at the facility and the sum of their balances — suspending on any one delinquent lease would lock a tenant out of a unit they had paid for, and requiring every lease to be delinquent would let them hold the gate open with one cheap unit kept current.
- **Suspension needs the day count AND an outstanding balance.** A day count with a zero balance is a rounding artefact or a credit; locking someone out over it is the version of this that produces a complaint rather than a payment. The threshold is `Facility.accessSuspendDaysPastDue` (default 6), and **zero disables the rule** — a real operator choice.
- **Restore is inline, not nightly.** The ~2-minute SLA is met by calling `restoreAccessIfSettled` directly from the paths that settle money — the Stripe webhook and the counter payment — inside the same request. Both calls are best-effort and outside the money transaction: a gate controller being unreachable must never roll back a payment. The 4am pass is the safety net for a balance that reached zero some other way (a credit, a waiver, a write-off), not the mechanism.
- **The hold check is first**, so no later branch can be reached past it, and a hold placed mid-cycle takes effect on the very next evaluation. A hold also holds a *suspended* grant where it is rather than restoring it — a hold is not a payment, and letting one lift a suspension would make it a free pass.
- **Found and fixed while building it:** `transitionGrant` emitted `access.granted` for a suspended→active transition, so CN-11's restore notice had no event to fire on. It now emits `access.restored`, mirroring the command type it already chose correctly.
- **Both settings are editable** in the facility billing policy form (US-3), not database-only: the day threshold with zero meaning "never suspend", and the restore balance with 0.00 meaning "paid in full".

**US-25 [MVP]** As an owner, I configure a delinquency timeline per facility as an ordered set of steps keyed to days-past-due, e.g.:

| Day (default) | Step | Automated action | Staff action queued |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Late | Late fee #1, email reminder | — |
| 6 | Access denied | Gate access revoked (event → PRD 04), tenant notified | — |
| 10 | Overlock | — | Apply physical overlock (checklist item), photo optional |
| 15 | Pre-lien notice | Generate pre-lien letter from template; send per configured delivery (email and/or mail) | Verify/print/mail if postal; record certificate/tracking # |
| 30 | Lien notice | Generate formal lien notice: itemized claim, deadline, conspicuous sale statement | Record delivery proof |
| 45 | Second late fee / lien fee | Fee applied | — |
| 60 | Auction eligible | Flag lease auction-eligible; appears in auction pipeline | Regional/owner approval required to schedule sale |

- AC: Every step has: trigger day, automated actions, required staff tasks, notice template (if any), delivery method(s), and required proof fields. Steps are re-orderable and per-facility; a facility's timeline is versioned; the lease records which timeline version governed it.
- AC: Paying the qualifying amount (configurable: full balance vs. rent-only) automatically halts the pipeline, restores gate access (event → PRD 04), and queues overlock removal.
- **AC (one step a night, always) — added 2026-08-24, B-161, D-92:** the nightly run executes **at most one step per lease**, however many are arithmetically due, and none at all if a step already ran for that lease on that business date. A ladder is a sequence of chances to pay; served all at once it is neither a sequence nor a chance, and a lien file whose ninety days of notices all bear one date is not a lien file. Catching up steps missed while nothing ran therefore takes a night per step, deliberately.
- **AC (a payment that came back) — added 2026-08-24, B-161, D-92:** a returned ACH, a bounced cheque or a chargeback (US-46) re-opens the invoice at its **original** due date per D-25, so the tenant reappears at full age. Two things follow. **The ladder is held** for `reversalGraceDays` (per-facility, 0–90, default 10) from the reversal, and for as long as the `settling_payment_failed` task stays open on top of that — while staff are working the bounce there is a person in the loop, which is what that queue is for. **The step history the cure superseded is restored**, so the ladder resumes at the stage already reached rather than re-serving every notice from day one; a facility may set `reversalResumes` false and re-serve everything instead. Resume is the default because restarting hands any tenant a way to defer an auction indefinitely by paying and reversing, and it is defensible because the earlier notices really were served and are still in the step history. Late fees are unaffected: the ladder reads which steps it has charged from the fee invoices, which a reversal does not void.
- **AC (a payment plan is a schedule, and progress is measured net) — added 2026-08-25, B-188, D-96:** the `payment_plan` hold has halted this pipeline since B-096 and B-090c gave it a schedule; what decides whether the schedule has been kept is **cumulative payment net of reversals, chargebacks and refunds**, never gross receipts. A returned ACH, a bounced cheque or a chargeback re-opens the invoice at its original due date (D-25) and must **withdraw** the plan progress it bought — a plan that completes itself and lifts its own hold on money the bank took back is the pay-then-reverse loop D-92 closed one layer up, re-opened one layer down. Progress stays **derived on read**, never a stored per-installment flag (the D-65 reasoning): a second record of the same fact is a second thing that can disagree with it. A plan whose progress goes backwards is re-evaluated the same night, not at the next installment. **D-96 settled which payments count (2026-08-26): rent first, then the plan.** An installment is satisfied only by money that settles the arrears the plan was agreed over — the invoices already past due when it was created, frozen on the plan — and current rent stays due on its own date on top. Progress is therefore read from **those invoices** rather than from `type: 'payment'` ledger entries, which is also what makes it net by construction: a reversal or a refund unwinds its allocations and re-opens the invoice. The plan total is validated against the lease's **actual** arrears at creation, naming the shortfall in the refusal; before B-188 the total was the sum of the installments themselves, so the check could not fire and a $50 plan against an $1,800 arrear halted the ladder and completed. The rule is stated in customer language on the tenant's plan page and on the staff builder **before** a plan is agreed (D-15).
- **AC (autopay knows a plan exists, in both directions) — added 2026-08-26, B-189, D-97:** a plan on a lease with autopay changes what autopay does, and before B-189 it changed nothing. **Arrears collection stands down; current rent does not.** `runAutopay` skips exactly the invoices the plan froze at creation and charges everything invoiced since on its own due date — a plan is forbearance on what is owed, not a rent holiday. The `payment_plan` hold deliberately does **not** declare `halt_autopay`: that effect is all-or-nothing per lease and would stop the rent too. Without this, the full arrears were taken from the card the same night the plan was agreed, on the retry ladder, for as long as it authorised — the precise outcome the plan exists to prevent. **An installment is collected on its due date** from the saved method, as one ledger-driven `PaymentIntent` (D-6) carrying the plan so the money is allocated only across the covered invoices and can never settle rent the plan never deferred. The amount charged is the cumulative schedule to date less what the plan has retired, so a tenant who part-paid at the counter is charged the remainder rather than the face value. **Auto-collection is the default with an opt-out at agreement (D-97)**, ticked, on the same form as the schedule — a tenant may ask to pay each installment themselves, which is what makes "you kept to the plan" a statement about the tenant rather than about their card. Three separate things make a plan manual-pay in fact — the box unticked, autopay off on the lease, no saved card — and staff and tenant screens must say which, never claim a charge that will not happen. **A declined installment is a decline, not a breach (CN-6).** It joins US-20's retry ladder anchored on the installment's own due date, and the breach job leaves the plan standing while that ladder runs — bounded by the facility's last retry offset, ended at once by a terminal decline code. A manual-pay plan has no ladder, so a missed installment breaks it the same night. **Telling the tenant any of this is not built** — the agreement, the installment-due reminder and the break notice are B-191's CN-24.
- **AC (a plan is checked against what is owed) — added 2026-08-25, B-188:** the schedule's total is validated against the lease's **actual outstanding balance** at the moment it is agreed, and a shortfall is named in the refusal. Validating the installments against their own sum is not a check; it is arithmetic. A plan for less than the arrears halts dunning, late fees and access suspension for its whole life and then completes, which is a way to stop collections by agreeing to pay a fraction of them.
- **AC (autopay knows about the plan) — added 2026-08-25, B-189, D-97:** a lease on an active plan has its **arrears collection stood down** — the plan replaces it — while **current rent keeps being collected**, because a plan is forbearance on what is owed and not a rent holiday. Charging the full arrears on the retry ladder the night a plan is agreed is the outcome the plan exists to prevent, done by the system that was told about it. Whether the installment itself is auto-collected on its due date, or a plan is deliberately manual-pay, is **D-97**. Either way it is a ledger-driven `PaymentIntent` (D-6) — never Stripe Billing, never a subscription. A failed installment charge is a failed payment on US-20's retry ladder, **not a breach**: a decline and a decision not to pay are different facts.
- **AC (a plan has a ceiling, and a chain of plans has a limit) — added 2026-08-25, built 2026-08-27, B-190, D-98:** agreeing a plan is a decision **not to collect**, made by one person, on a lease already in collections, so it passes `checkMonetaryAuthority(actor, 'payment_plan', arrears, facilityId)` against **`maxPlanDeferralCents`** — a limit of its own, not the fee-waiver one, because a deferral gives nothing away and the waiver limits are sized for gifts ($50 for a manager, against a typical $1,800 arrear). Without these, a broken plan replaced the next morning halts the lien clock indefinitely and no screen shows that it is happening. The numbers are **D-98**.
- **AC (the tenant is told, four times) — added 2026-08-25, B-191, PRD 05 CN-24:** a plan agreed, an installment due, a plan broken and a plan kept are each a message, and the broken one goes **the same night the hold lifts** — before the ladder resumes, before late fees re-arm and before the gate closes. A plan that breaks in silence turns the next dunning letter into the tenant's first news of it. Copy is customer language (D-15) and states what it costs to put right, not only that something happened.
- **AC (the grace window is a rule everyone quotes, not a number one job knows) — added and built 2026-08-31, B-210, D-98(4):** `planGraceDays` was applied only inside `evaluatePaymentPlanBreaches`, by moving that job's own clock back. - **AC (a refusal the staffer cannot avoid is not a control, and cent-exact arithmetic is not a form) — added and built 2026-08-31, B-212:** the builder is offered **only where a plan could be agreed**: the lease has arrears (`arrearsCents > 0`) and is under D-98's rolling-year cap, counted by the same function `createPaymentPlan` refuses with, so the screen and the server cannot disagree. - AC: Scheduling a sale requires regional/owner approval and completion of all prior steps with proofs — the system hard-blocks scheduling if any required step lacks proof; sale outcome recording captures: sale date, buyer, gross proceeds, applied-to-balance amount, surplus amount and disposition note, unit released to `maintenance` for cleanout verification.
- AC: Cancelling a sale (tenant paid) at any point restores the normal lifecycle and logs the reason.
- **AC (lock cut and inventory):** date and time of the cut, the cutting staff member, disposition of the old lock, an itemised inventory of the unit's contents, and photographs — timestamped, stored immutably and hashed like the notices in US-27. This is the primary evidence that you sold what you said you sold.
- **AC (buyer record):** name, address, government-ID reference, sales-tax resale certificate where exempt, amount, payment method, and the cleanout deadline with its forfeit terms. A sales-tax return on auction proceeds cannot be filed without it.
- **AC (proceeds waterfall):** applied by the system in a fixed and stated order — reasonable sale costs → lien balance (rent, late fees, lien fees) → surplus — and **posted as ledger entries against the lease**, never typed in as a total.
- **AC (surplus):** a surplus is a liability with a statutory life, not revenue. Notify the former tenant at the address of record (US-13), hold for the state's required period, and record the disposition — claimed, or remitted to the state comptroller. Surplus quietly retained is how a routine auction becomes a class-action-shaped problem.
- **AC (vehicles, boats, trailers):** flag unit contents as vehicle-containing and **hard-block the standard auction path** with "requires separate vehicle lien process". Titled property follows a different notice and sale route in Texas and most states; running one silently through this pipeline is a wrongful sale by construction.
- **AC (the goods moved after the notice was served) — added 2026-08-24, B-160, D-91:** D-85 lets staff transfer a `pending_auction` tenant to another unit, and a Ch. 59 notice **names the space**. So a case whose goods have moved states both facts wherever it is read — the unit the notice named, and the unit the contents are in now — and every downstream action names the unit somebody has to walk to: the lock cut, the inventory document, the advertising record, and the release to `maintenance` for cleanout. **The sale is hard-blocked until a lien notice is served naming the unit the goods are in now** (D-91), with its own blocker rather than the generic one, because a manager who served the notice themselves must not be told that no notice was served. **The proceeds waterfall posts to the lease the claim moved to**, not the pinned one: D-86 re-points the unpaid invoices on a transfer, so posting the credit back to the original lease credits a ledger that already nets to zero and leaves the live lease showing the full arrears after a completed sale. Re-serving restarts the **notice** period only; what the tenant owes, and how long they have owed it, are unchanged (D-85).
- Note: the Texas surplus holding period and the vehicle carve-out specifics need an attorney pass under D-10. The fields and the hard block are built now; the durations are configuration. **The re-service rule in the AC above is on that same list** — it is a Texas default, per-state configurable, and not legal advice.

**US-29 [MVP]** Per-state disclaimer & guardrails: the timeline configuration screen displays a persistent disclaimer that lien requirements vary by state and configurations must be attorney-reviewed; the system requires a facility "state" and shows the configured timeline summary on every auction approval screen.
- AC: No default timeline is presented as legally compliant; defaults are labeled "example configuration."

**US-30 [P2]** Integrations: certified-mail API for automated pre-lien/lien mailing with tracked proof; online auction platform listing (e.g., StorageTreasures-style) from the auction pipeline.
  - *Certified mail built B-083, 2026-08-18; the auction-platform half split out as **B-129**, blocked (**D-63**).* `mailNoticeCertified` posts the stored notice document by USPS certified mail and records the returned tracking number as proof of service, through `recordNoticeDelivery` so B-061's consent and proof gates still run. **`deliveredAt` is the MAILING date**, because service by certified mail is complete on mailing — a refused or unclaimed letter does not undo it, and the auction pipeline's served-notice precondition (US-28) unlocks on the same footing a court would use. **There is deliberately no simulator** and unconfigured is the shipped state: the button is not offered, the screen names the missing variable, and staff record the mailing by hand as before. A fabricated tracking number is a claim about what the postal service did, in the evidence chain for a lien sale. The provider key is refused both outside production (a live key would post real paper from a laptop; post has no sandbox inbox) and inside it if it is a test key (which returns a valid-looking tracking number and mails nothing). **The auction-platform listing is not built**: master PRD §11 OQ-9 — live on-site versus online auctions — has never been answered, and a listing needs a partner agreement with no published spec. The same no-simulator rule applies, because a fabricated listing record is a claim about a public advertisement Texas requires before a sale.

### 4.9 Tasks & Operations

**US-41 [MVP]** As a part-timer alone on a Saturday, I open **one list** that tells me what to do today at this facility, instead of checking seven queues.
*(Added 2026-07-31 from the operator review. Failed payments, move-out verifications, delinquency steps, overlocks, manual gate commands, bounced emails and move-in provisioning failures are each specced as their own queue, and `Task` appears in §6.1's entity list with nothing building it. Left alone, each of those grows its own table and its own screen, and the manager who works seven screens skips the last three — against a success metric of ≥95% of delinquency steps executed on their scheduled day.)*
- AC: one `Task` entity: facility, type, subject entity (lease / unit / tenant / payment), due date as a **facility-local business date**, priority, assignee (nullable = anyone at the facility), status, required proof fields as typed JSON, completion actor + timestamp, and the **source event id** so a task is traceable to whatever created it.
- AC: creation is **idempotent**, keyed on (type, entityId, businessDate) — a re-run of a nightly job produces one overlock task, not four. This matches the existing job-run idempotency contract.
- AC: one "My day" list per facility: everything due today or overdue, grouped by type, **mobile-first** because half of it is done while walking the property. Overdue items escalate and roll up to the regional view.
- AC: a task whose type demands proof (tracking number, photo) cannot be completed without it. There is no "mark done" shortcut past a required proof field.
- AC: completion is audit-logged wherever the underlying action is sensitive — overlock applied, delinquency step executed, gate command performed manually.
- AC: **every later queue is a filtered view of this list, not a new table.** US-26 (delinquency), US-35/36 (walkthrough, overlock), US-20's failed payments, PRD 03 US-6's manual gate queue, PRD 05 CN-19's failure follow-ups, and PRD 01 FR-4.6's provisioning failures all create and read `Task`.
- **AC (added 2026-08-21 from the operator and accessibility reviews, which found it independently; verified): a completion that is refused says so.** Every queue view over this list renders the type's required proof fields as required controls, **and the server action's `{ ok: false, missingFields }` result reaches the screen** — field-level `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby`, a focused error summary, and the persistent `role="status"` region of FR-19/FR-20. `/admin/tasks` shipped a note input with no `required` while all three sibling queues had it, and an action that discarded the rejection: the button was pressed, the page re-rendered identically, and the task stayed open. A control that returns a refusal nobody surfaces is indistinguishable from a broken control, and neither the operator nor CI could tell them apart.
- **AC (2.4.6, 4.1.3):** in a list of cards, each card's controls carry an accessible name naming that card's subject ("Complete returned-mail review for Ada Renter, unit 104") rather than the same bare verb repeated once per card. The outcome of a completion announces from a live region that was in the DOM **before** the submit.

**US-35 [MVP]** Daily walkthrough checklist: a mobile-web checklist generated daily per facility: overlocks to apply/remove (from delinquency engine), lock checks on recently vacated units, space-by-space verification items, and free-form findings that convert to maintenance tickets.
- AC: Checklist completion status is visible to regional roll-up; items can attach photos; skipped days are visible (not silently absent).

**US-36 [MVP]** Overlock list: a dedicated, always-current list of units that *should be* overlocked vs. *confirmed* overlocked (and the removal equivalent), reconciling system state with physical state.
- AC: Applying/removing an overlock is confirmed by the walker (with optional photo); mismatch > 24h old is flagged on manager and regional dashboards.
- **AC (B-151, 2026-08-22): an overlock is released when its lease ENDS, whatever the balance did.** US-25's "queues overlock removal" fired only on **cure**, and a lease that ends still owing halts the pipeline as `moved_out` instead — so a move-out with a balance, a transfer, or an auction sale each left the lock live. `deriveUnitStatus` returns `overlocked` ahead of everything, including the `maintenance` a move-out sets, so the unit read `overlocked` indefinitely; **this reconciliation view showed no mismatch, because system and physical state agreed and both were wrong**; and the unit stayed in the occupied numerator, so the site's sellable inventory shrank by one unit per event with nothing reporting it. All three lease-ending paths now release in their own transaction, the nightly delinquency run releases any already-ended lease as a backstop, and `releaseOverlock` is idempotent — `createTask` is unique per `(type, entityId, businessDate)`, so an unguarded re-call would raise a fresh removal task every morning until somebody acted. **The unit deliberately does NOT return to the denominator at lease end** — there is still a real lock on the door — it returns when the removal is confirmed, which is what the task now asks for.
- **AC (the backstop has to run where there is no timeline, and the card has to say why — added 2026-08-24 from the operator review; B-169):** the release backstop above lived **inside** the nightly delinquency run, which returns early for any facility with no configured timeline — so the sites most likely to have locks stuck on ended leases, the ones nobody has configured, were the only ones it could never reach: the units it was built to free were exactly the units it could not. It is now **its own nightly step, unconditional on timeline configuration**, and being a sweep rather than a one-off script is deliberate — a nightly job that fixes what it finds cannot be forgotten the way a script can, and its first run IS the repair. **The removal task's label states the FACT and no longer asserts a payment:** "take the overlock off" with the reason — cured, lease ended, transfer, auction sale, abandonment — carried per card in `Task.detail`, because the type cannot know which of the five produced it and the previous label said "the tenant has paid" on the two paths where they most certainly had not. **The reconciliation list gains a `stuck_no_lease` state**, flagged immediately rather than after 24 hours: every other mismatch there is a staff member not having got to it, and this one is a unit that has fallen out of sellable inventory with nothing in any queue about it. **The units board says so too** — a unit reading `overlocked` with no occupant is shown as not rentable, linked to its removal task, rather than as the ordinary delinquency case.
**US-37 [MVP]** Maintenance tickets: create (from walkthrough, unit page, or manually), assign, prioritize, track status (open/in-progress/blocked/done); tickets on a unit can set the unit to `maintenance`.
- AC: A unit cannot be set `available` while a blocking ticket is open; ticket history stays with the unit.

### 4.11 Reporting

**US-39 [MVP]** As an owner, I can run these reports, per facility and rolled up, with date-range selection and CSV export:
1. **Occupancy** — unit occupancy % (occupied ÷ rentable units), square-foot occupancy %, by unit type; point-in-time and trend.
   - **AC (B-131, D-68): unit occupancy answers for the period asked for, or says which instant it answers for instead.** `UnitStatusHistory` records every change to a unit's derived status, written by a database trigger rather than by each caller, and the occupancy read is an as-at query at the end of the selected period. A period ending before the history begins — or one that has not ended yet — is **not** answered with today's statuses under its heading: the report returns the current figures flagged as such, with one shared sentence naming the instant and, where relevant, the date before which nothing was recorded. That sentence is printed identically by the screen, the scheduled email and the CSV (as a date column), because a reader who sees two wordings of the same caveat trusts neither. Economic occupancy is unaffected — it comes from dated ledger rows and always covered the period. Unit TYPE is still read as it stands today, so a retyped or resized unit carries its current square footage into a past month; noted rather than fixed, and it is a second table if it ever matters.
2. **Economic occupancy** — actual rent revenue ÷ gross potential rent at street rates; plus rate variance report (in-place rate vs street per unit).
3. **Move-ins / move-outs** — counts, net, by source (walk-in, phone, web — source data shared with PRD 03/05), with conversion from reservations.
4. **Delinquency aging** — AR buckets (0–10, 11–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+), tenant-level detail, delinquency step distribution, total exposure.
5. **Revenue** — billed vs collected, by category (rent, fees, insurance, merchandise, taxes collected), discounts/promos given, write-offs.
6. **Deposits reconciliation** — per day per facility: system-recorded payments by method (card batches from processor, cash, check) vs drawer close-outs and processor settlement records; variances flagged (US-33).
- AC: Every report: facility filter, date range, on-screen table + summary tiles, CSV export matching on-screen data exactly; report numbers are consistent with each other (one metrics definition layer — e.g., "occupied" means the same everywhere); month-end snapshot values are frozen once the month closes (P2: formal close process).
- **AC (the metrics layer is a module, not an intention):** a shared `metrics` module in the core package owns every definition, each with a written formula and unit tests — unit occupancy (occupied ÷ rentable, where **rentable excludes `unrentable` and includes `maintenance`** — state it, do not leave it to be inferred), square-foot occupancy, economic occupancy (collected ÷ gross potential at street), rate variance, AR aging buckets, days-past-due, move-in/move-out counts, delinquent AR. **No screen, tile, or export computes any of these inline.** The first time the owner dashboard says 91% and the rent roll says 88%, he stops trusting both, and a week goes on proving which one was right.
- **AC:** `daysPastDue(lease)` has exactly one definition, computed from the **original** invoice due date and never from the last retry attempt (US-20), and is used by the dashboard tile, the late-fee run, and every later delinquency consumer.
- **AC:** roll-up equals the sum of the facility reports with no double counting — asserted in a test, not stated in a document.
- **AC (report 3 has an owner):** move-ins/move-outs is not an optional extra. Counts and net by facility and date range, by source (walk-in / phone / web per US-43), with reservation-to-move-in conversion and average days from reservation to move-in, CSV matching the screen exactly. It is the report a multi-site manager opens every morning.
- **AC (rate variance is a report, not a column):** in-place rate vs current street rate per occupied unit, sorted by gap, with months since the last change (US-11). That report is the worklist the Phase-2 rate-increase workflow runs from.
- **AC (added 2026-08-21 from the operator review; **built B-150, 2026-08-22** — confirmed, and answered the second way): AR aging answers for the date asked for, or states which instant it answers for instead.** This is B-131/D-68's rule applied to report 4. The aging section sits under a month picker and computes as of today, one section below the fix that made occupancy historical — so a month-end AR figure and the aging table beneath it disagree with nothing on screen explaining why. Where a historical bucket genuinely cannot be reconstructed, the section names the instant it does answer for, in facility-local time, rather than silently answering a different question. **B-150 confirmed the defect and took the second branch, because D-65 had already closed the first:** AR aging is a point-in-time figure stored as the sole record of a closed month and never recomputed, so there is no past instant to answer for and reconstructing one would answer a different question under the same name. `arAgingNote` is the sibling of B-131's `unitOccupancyNote` and prints the instant with its zone attached — facility-local when every facility in scope shares a timezone, UTC with a sentence saying so when they do not, since a portfolio table spanning zones has no single local clock. `delinquencyReport` now takes **one** clock for the whole run rather than a `new Date()` per facility, so the instant the sentence names is the instant every bucket was actually cut at.
- **AC (protection attach rate is a percentage, and it has an owner):** US-44 already requires attach rate to be reportable and no report owns it, so dollars are reported and the ratio is not. Plan-enrolled leases ÷ new move-ins as a **percentage**, per facility, per month, and per staff member who completed the move-in, plus a split by channel (web / counter / phone per US-43). Defined in the metrics module like every other figure (D-25), never inline. Dollars cannot be coached against; a percentage can. **Built 2026-08-23 (B-155).** `packages/core/metrics/attach-rate.ts` owns the formula; `attachRateReport` in `lib/admin/reports.ts` wires it into `/admin/reports` beside the move-ins section. Enrolled reads `Lease.protectionPlanName !== null` — the same column a waiver already nulls. **"Per staff member who completed the move-in" has no dedicated column** — `provisionLease` (B-026) is Stripe-webhook driven with no staff actor at all — so it is read from `Payment.receivedByStaffId` (US-32) on the move-in's earliest payment, which is exactly who was behind the counter for a phone or walk-in rental and genuinely null for an online, card-paid one. That null buckets under `UNASSIGNED_STAFF` ("Web / self-service"), visible rather than folded into a real staffer's count.
- **AC (waitlist demand is contactable):** the waitlist demand report renders the contact details the entry already stores. A demand report an operator cannot act from is a list of people who wanted to give us money.

**US-40 [P2]** Scheduled report emails; management summary pack (monthly PDF); accounting export (QuickBooks-compatible journal CSV).
  - *Split into four parts, 2026-08-18; **part 1 (monthly close + frozen snapshots) built**.* `AccountingPeriod` per facility per month, filed at `/admin/reports/close` under a new `accounting:close` permission held at regional and above. **D-65 settles what "frozen" means and it is the whole of §8's close process:** unit occupancy reads `Unit.status`, of which this system keeps no history, and `delinquencyReport` takes no date parameter — both answer "as of now" whatever range they are given, so those figures are observable only at the time and the filed copy is the only record there will ever be. They are frozen and never drift-checked. Billed, collected, discounts, write-offs, refunds, unapplied, economic occupancy and move counts come from dated rows, so they are frozen **and** re-run on view with any difference reported as drift — a voided invoice, a backdated adjustment, a corrected move-out. Month bounds use the same `monthBounds` a tenant statement uses and the resolved window is stored, so a later timezone correction cannot re-slice a filed month; a month cannot be closed before it has ended in facility-local time; reopening needs a reason and moves the withdrawn figures to the audit log. **D-64: the management pack is semantic HTML, not the monthly PDF named above** — B-023 found that no JavaScript PDF library in this runtime emits *tagged* PDFs, and an untagged one is a screen-reader dead end. **Part 2 (QuickBooks journal export) built, 2026-08-18 — D-66.** `/admin/reports/journal.csv` cuts a balanced general-journal entry from a CLOSED month's frozen figures and refuses an open one, which makes the ordering argument behind the split enforceable rather than advisory. Ten named accounts per facility, configured on the close screen and matched by NAME because that is what QuickBooks Online's import matches on, with a conventional chart as the per-field fallback. `buildJournal` asserts its own balance and throws otherwise, the same posture as US-28's proceeds waterfall — we are not the accounting system of record (§2), so the one thing the handoff must guarantee is that it balances. **Two deliberate omissions that read as bugs:** refunds are not journaled, because a refund unwinds its original payment's allocations so collected is already net of it and a second entry would take the money out twice while balancing; and referral rewards are taken out of the discount figure rather than posted beside it, because they are a subset of it. **Part 3 (scheduled report emails) built, 2026-08-18 — D-67.** `ReportSubscription` per facility, sent by a `reports.email` job at 6am facility-local — after the overnight billing and delinquency sweeps, so the figures already include last night's invoices and late fees. **This is the product's first generated email and therefore the first to meet PRD 05 FR-9a**, every clause of which is now a test rather than a review note. No `lastSentAt` column: the send is keyed by subscription, period and recipient, and `sendDirectEmail` already refuses a repeat. The job does not invent an actor — `facilityAccess` correctly gives the system actor nothing — so the reporting layer gained facility-explicit variants called after authorization already happened at subscribe time. A monthly report states whether its month has been closed, so a live figure is never mistaken for a filed one. **Part 4 (management summary pack) built, 2026-08-18 — US-40 is complete.** `/admin/reports/pack`, in HTML per D-64. Built as one `EmailDocument` so the page an owner opens and the pack an owner is sent cannot say different things about one month; subscribable monthly only, because a weekly pack would have nothing filed to read. It reads the FILED figures when the month is closed — the payoff of the four-part ordering — and **leads with a provenance sentence** in three forms: not closed and can still change; closed and settled; closed but N named figures no longer match, called a restatement to explain rather than an error. **Also found and filed as B-131:** the occupancy report's date range does not apply to its own occupancy figure — the close freezes around that rather than fixing it. **B-131 built 2026-08-19 (D-68)**, which makes unit occupancy historical from that date forward; the close's freeze is deliberately unchanged, because drift-checking it would report drift on every month filed before the history existed.

## 5. Functional Requirements (consolidated)

### 5.1 Shell & Navigation
- FR-1: Global header: facility switcher (US-1), universal search (tenant/unit/invoice #), notification bell (queued approvals, failed runs, overdue delinquency steps), user menu.
- FR-2: Left nav: Dashboard, Units (map/list), Tenants, Leases, Billing, Delinquency, Auctions, POS/Drawer, Tasks, Reports, Settings, Audit Log — filtered by role.
- FR-3: Facility dashboard (default landing): today's move-ins/outs, payments today, delinquency queue count, walkthrough status, failed payments, occupancy tile.

### 5.2 Engines & Jobs
- FR-4: **Billing scheduler** — nightly per-facility jobs (facility-local time): invoice generation, autopay run, retry run, late-fee assessment, delinquency step evaluation, reservation expiry. All runs idempotent, logged to a Billing Runs screen with per-item outcomes, and manually re-runnable by admin.
- FR-5: **Delinquency engine** — evaluates each delinquent lease against its facility timeline version nightly; executes **at most one step per lease per run** (B-161/D-92); queues staff tasks, emits gate-access events; holds the ladder while a returned payment is inside its grace window or its settlement task is open.
- FR-6: **Document generation service** — templated PDF generation (leases, notices, receipts, rate letters) with merge-field validation (generation fails loudly on missing fields; never sends a notice with blank merge fields).
- FR-7: **Event bus (per master PRD)** — this module emits: `unit.status_changed`, `lease.moved_in/out/transferred`, `access.revoke/restore` (PRD 04), `rates.updated` (PRD 03/05), `overlock.required/cleared` (PRD 04); consumes: `reservation.created`, `payment.succeeded/failed` (portal-initiated), `esign.completed` (PRD 03).

### 5.3 Data Integrity
- FR-8: Money stored as integer cents; all financial mutations are double-entry-style ledger events; no destructive edits to posted financial records (corrections are reversing entries).
- FR-9: Effective-dating for: tax rates, fee schedules, street rates, delinquency timelines, notice templates.
- FR-10: Soft delete only for tenants/leases/documents; hard delete never exposed in UI.
- FR-11: Concurrency: optimistic locking on lease/tenant edits; billing jobs take per-lease locks.

### 5.4 Non-Functional
- FR-12: Responsive web; walkthrough/overlock/tasks screens optimized for phone; POS optimized for desktop + card terminal.
- FR-13: p95 < 500ms for search and unit map; nightly runs complete in < 30 min for 20 facilities × 800 units.
- FR-14: Availability target 99.5%; billing jobs recover automatically after downtime (catch-up runs).
- FR-15: PII and payment data handling per master PRD (tokenized payments only — no PAN storage; provider handles PCI scope).

### 5.5 Accessibility — WCAG 2.1 AA (admin surfaces)

*Added 2026-07-31 from the accessibility review, which found that this PRD contained no accessibility text at all — no "accessib", no "WCAG", no "keyboard", no "contrast", no "aria". Master PRD §7.2 puts admin surfaces in scope in two sentences, and PRD 01 §6.8 is scoped to the customer site, so every admin item to date has been built from a spec that never mentioned AA. That is why the shipped admin routes carry the majority of the audit's blocking findings.*

This section is the admin equivalent of PRD 01 §6.8 and is an acceptance criterion on **every** story in this PRD, not a later cleanup. Staff are users, a manager may be the one with low vision, and a back office is exactly where nobody notices for three years.

- **FR-16 Inherited baseline.** Everything in PRD 01 §6.8 applies here: semantic HTML and landmarks, correct heading order, full keyboard operability, ≥4.5:1 text contrast, ≥3:1 for UI components *and focus indicators*, colour never the sole carrier of meaning, reflow to 320px, text resizable to 200%, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected. Admin shares the customer site's design tokens, so a token that fails on the public site fails here too — fix it once, in the token.
- **FR-17 Bypass and shell.** Every admin page has a skip link as its first focusable element, targeting a `<main id="main" tabIndex={-1}>`. Without one, a keyboard or switch user tabs the facility switcher, the search, the bell, the user menu, sign-out and every nav item **on every page load** before reaching content.
- **FR-18 No context change on input (3.2.2).** No `<select>`, checkbox, filter or sort control submits a form or navigates on `change`. The facility switcher, every queue filter, and every report control carry a visible submit. Arrow-keying a select fires `change` on every option passed on some platforms — an auto-submitting switcher navigates a keyboard user to three wrong facilities on the way to the fourth. Where the working context does change, the new context is announced (a `role="status"` on the header facility name is enough).
- **FR-19 Errors are identified, suggested, and announced (3.3.1, 3.3.3, 4.1.3).** Server actions **return** error state rather than throwing — a thrown error is not a user-facing error message. Field errors render adjacent to their control, referenced by `aria-describedby`, with `aria-invalid="true"`; a summary above the form receives focus on submit so the user hears the count and can jump to each. Messages carry a *suggestion*, not just an identification: "State must be a 2-letter code, e.g. TX."
- **FR-20 Success is announced too.** Every form has one `role="status"` region **rendered empty at page load** and filled on state change ("Tax rate added, effective 2026-08-01"). A live region inserted into the DOM already populated is unreliably announced by VoiceOver and routinely missed by NVDA — the region must pre-exist the event. The same rule governs every async control: a control that goes silent for ten seconds after activation has failed, and a control that disables itself while busy blurs the user's focus to `<body>`. Use `aria-busy` and a changed label instead of `disabled`.
- **FR-21 Error prevention for financial and legal data (3.3.4).** Any append-only or irreversible financial configuration — tax components, fee schedule rows, street rates, late-fee rules, refunds, scheduled rate increases — passes through a confirm step that **echoes the parsed result back in the user's terms**: "Add a **state** tax of **8.25%** to *Demo — Austin South*, effective **1 Aug 2026**. This cannot be edited or deleted," with explicit Confirm and Cancel. Values are range-checked server-side and implausible input is rejected with a suggestion rather than stored — a fat-fingered `825` must not become an 825% tax rate on every future invoice. The bulk-edit preview-then-apply shape (US-7) is the pattern to reuse, not to reinvent.
- **FR-22 Tables carry their relationships (1.3.1, 2.4.6).** The identifying cell of a row is a `<th scope="row">` (unit number, day name, tenant name) and header cells are `scope="col"`. **Every repeated control in a table names its row** — a rotor listing "Set" 200 times or fourteen checkboxes all named "Closed" is unusable by ear. Sortable columns carry `aria-sort`; the row count is announced after filtering; bulk actions announce their outcome ("14 units updated, 2 skipped") rather than only re-rendering the table. Disabling a control in response to another control's state is announced, or the control is made read-only and left reachable instead. **A dense table reflows rather than scrolling sideways (1.4.10):** below the small breakpoint each row becomes a card carrying its own labels, and no admin route is exempt — see **D-48**, which settled that the three routes shipped failing this are fixed rather than documented around. **A list whose length grows with inventory is paginated**, with a count line and the filter state in the URL. The two requirements are one requirement in practice: 290 rows each carrying a `<select>`, a submit and a link is roughly 900 tab stops before the content below the table, and no amount of CSS makes that reachable.
- **FR-23 Queues, escalation, and charts.** Visual escalation of overdue work never relies on colour alone — text or icon-plus-text (1.4.1). Dashboard charts carry a data-table equivalent or a full text summary, and series are distinguished by more than hue.
- **FR-24 Verification.** `/admin/*` routes are in the automated axe run using the existing authenticated staff session, alongside the public routes. Automated scanning is a floor: it checks text contrast only, cannot judge announcement, and only ever sees a freshly loaded page — so scans additionally run **after** an invalid form submit and after a bulk-edit preview opens, and token contrast pairs get a unit test. Focus-indicator visibility and screen-reader announcement get a recorded manual pass, and "recorded" means a line in `docs/PROGRESS.md`, not a memory.
- **FR-25 What the scan set has to cover (added 2026-08-21 from the accessibility review; needs confirmation at build time).** Three structural gaps make a green axe run mean less than it looks, and each closes with a mechanism rather than with remembering. (1) **Post-interaction states are scanned.** At least one scan per flow runs *after* the interaction that changes the DOM — an invalid submit, an opened `<details>`, a revealed panel, a wizard step change — because a scan of a page at rest never sees the states that carry the defects; B-122's promo box lives in a collapsed disclosure the checkout scan never opens, so its refusal state and its contrast have never been checked at all. (2) **Parameterised routes are covered explicitly.** `ADMIN_ROUTES` and `PORTAL_ROUTES` are static lists by construction, so every `[param]` route is covered only where somebody remembered a click-through; the suite carries a named list of parameterised routes with a fixture id for each, and a new one is added by the item that ships it. (3) **A control that does nothing fails CI.** After a submit, a live region that was **already attached before** the submit has non-empty text — the cheapest assertion that separates "the action succeeded silently" from "the action was refused silently", which is the exact pair `/admin/tasks` could not distinguish. **The accessibility statement's coverage-exception list is generated from these lists rather than hand-written**: a route absent from the scan set and absent from the exception list is a false public claim, which is how a linked, static portal page came to be covered by no scan and disclaimed by no sentence. **Built 2026-08-21 (B-139), for the third of these three:** the scan lists and the exception list live together in `apps/web/lib/a11y/scan-coverage.ts`, the specs loop over them, the public page renders the customer-facing exceptions from them, and `tests/a11y-scan-coverage.test.ts` fails when a route under `apps/web/app` is in neither — in the fast CI lane, on every push, with no browser or database. It asserts four things, not one: nothing uncovered, no exception for a route that no longer exists (the same overstatement in the other direction), no scanned URL the app does not serve, and that every route claimed as "scanned by its own spec" still has a file running axe against it. **Parts (1) and (2) remain open and are B-156.**
- **FR-25a What a scan set cannot express, and what it must therefore print (added 2026-08-24 from the accessibility review; **B-184**).** FR-25's contract is **route-keyed by construction** — the coverage test walks the app directory for `page.tsx`, and a post-interaction state has no directory — so once the public exception list is generated from it (B-139), the gap becomes invisible on the page as well as in CI. Three requirements close what can be closed. (1) **States are declared alongside routes.** A second pair of exported lists, keyed `{route, state, spec | reason}`, is rendered beside the route exceptions and cross-checked by a unit test against a comment convention in the specs. This does not achieve completeness and nothing can; it makes an unscanned state a **printed sentence** rather than a paragraph in `docs/PROGRESS.md`. (2) **The undecidable-result (`incomplete`) assertion belongs to the shared helper, not to one spec.** Asserting it on the public route loop alone, while the checkout steps, the portal and every admin spec destructure violations only, means the money path is scanned to a weaker standard than the marketing pages — and the public statement then describes the weaker path with the stronger sentence. (3) **The error and confirm states of the shared admin form are scanned like any other state**: an invalid submit on an admin or portal form gets a scan (FR-24 already requires it and no spec did it), and the `confirm` echo panel — the only implementation of 3.3.4 error prevention in the product — mounts its live region **before** the step it announces, so a pre-existing region can be asserted against it. **Focus after a SUCCESSFUL submit is asserted, not only the announcement**: a form that removes the card it reports on drops focus to `<body>`, and one line distinguishes that from success.

## 7. Success Metrics

**Operational efficiency**
- Counter move-in completed in ≤ 5 minutes (median, timed in-app).
- ≥ 95% of delinquency steps executed on their scheduled day (no silent skips).
- Daily walkthrough completion rate ≥ 90% of facility-days.

**Financial accuracy**
- 100% of nightly billing runs complete or auto-recover without manual intervention (target ≥ 99% of nights).
- Deposits reconciliation variance = $0 on ≥ 95% of facility-days; 100% of variances explained within 48h.
- Autopay success rate ≥ 92% after retries (industry-typical card churn assumed).

**Revenue management**
- 100% of tenant rate increases delivered with ≥ configured notice and correct first invoice.
- Rate variance (street vs in-place) visible for 100% of occupied units; economic occupancy report adopted (viewed weekly by owner persona).

**Risk**
- 0 auction approvals with missing step proofs (hard-blocked by design; metric verifies no override path).
- 100% of sensitive actions present in audit log (verified by periodic automated self-audit).

**Learning-project proxies (no real users):** end-to-end demo scenarios pass: 90-day simulated facility (move-ins, billing cycles, one full delinquency-to-auction arc, month-end reports reconcile).

## 8. Reporting (summary)
See US-39/40. Principles: one shared metrics-definition layer; every report exportable to CSV; roll-up = sum of facility reports with no double counting; frozen month-end snapshots (P2 close process). Occupancy and economic occupancy are the two headline KPIs on every dashboard.

**On "frozen" (B-084 part 1, D-65):** a snapshot freezes two different kinds of figure and they need opposite treatment. Some are **only knowable at the time** — unit occupancy and AR aging, because nothing records what a unit's status was and the aging report takes no date — so the filed copy is the sole record and comparing it to a later run is meaningless. The rest are **derived from dated rows** and can be reproduced, so the filed copy is what was reported and a later disagreement means the past was edited. The close reports drift over the second kind only.

## 10. Open Questions

1. **Billing anniversary vs first-of-month:** support both per facility in MVP, or anniversary-only to simplify proration? (Leaning: both, since incumbents support both and migration demand expects it.)
2. ~~**Delinquency clock vs partial payments:**~~ **Closed 2026-07-31 by D-16 — the balance must reach zero.** A partial payment does not restore gate access and does not exit delinquency; the threshold is a per-facility setting so it can be relaxed without a migration. Any partial rule teaches a tenant to pay the minimum that reopens the gate, every month.
3. **State configuration depth:** do we ship example timeline presets per state (clearly labeled non-legal-advice), or only a single generic example? Presets are valuable but raise "implied compliance" risk — needs a decision with the disclaimer language.
4. ~~**Map editor scope:**~~ **Closed 2026-07-31** — JSON layout import plus the grid view is the answer, and the drag-to-place editor is dropped from Phase 2 (see §9).
5. **Insurance/protection plans:** treated here as a recurring line item; is a full insurance-program feature (provider integration, claims) in scope for any phase, and which PRD owns it? — **Still open, deliberately.** Deferred on 2026-07-31 rather than answered: US-44 proceeds with an in-house protection plan catalog and our own waiver record, which is not blocked by the answer. Revisit when there is a reason to — a licensing question, a provider offer, or a claim we cannot handle ourselves.
6. **Approval routing:** single-step approvals (this PRD) vs a shared approval-workflow service used by other modules — master PRD decision.
7. **Processor settlement ingestion:** MVP reconciles against drawer + system records; when do we ingest processor settlement files automatically (needed for true 3-way reconciliation)?
8. **Data migration:** importing tenants/ledgers from SiteLink/storEDGE exports — Phase 2 feature or out of scope for the learning project?
9. **Timezone edge cases:** nightly runs at facility-local time across DST transitions — confirm job-scheduler behavior in master PRD architecture.
